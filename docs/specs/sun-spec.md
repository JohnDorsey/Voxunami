# The sun (Phase 41a; its shadows are in `shadow-spec.md`, 41b; its place in the sky, the night and the sunset are section 8)

Status: built 2026-10-28, the owner's "Lighting please" after the great rivers. What I ran or looked at is marked **[run]**; what I believe from reading the code **[belief]**.

## 1. What the owner wanted and what this is

The owner's direction (2026-10-27): shadows from shaders in place of the flooding of sky light "for most sky light". I recommended an order: the renderer's depth range (measured, not needed), then **the sun as a light of the game's clock with the terminator of each planet worked out as a sphere**, then cascaded shadow maps for what is near, then a cheap ambient term for caves. **This phase is the second step.** It makes the planet, the rivers and the overworld visible without `.ambient`, with a day and a night. It is *not* shadows: a hill does not shade the valley behind it and a cave under a sunlit hill is lit as the hill is (section 7).

## 2. The rule

- **A sun is a game's, per level** (`GameDefinition::sun(level) -> Option<Sun>`, default none: every other game is as it was). `Sun` (`engine/game_shell/src/sun.rs`) is a day length in seconds of the world's clock, the hour at clock 0, the direction at noon, the axis it turns about (a great circle if the axis is across noon), how bright it is (`light`), how bright the sky is where the sun reaches (`sky_light`), the share of it that reaches a place in a shadow (`sky_light_in_shadow`) and one with the terrain over it (`sky_light_under_cover`, `shadow-spec.md` section 8), whether the level's ground is flat, and the colour and thickness of the sky.
- **The clock is the world's**, not the count of ticks of a run: the sun is a function of `ServerWorld::world_tick()`, which is saved with the world (`rails-and-recipes-spec.md`, 40a). The server now sends it in the welcome (**protocol 10**: `Welcome::world_clock`, the clock at the welcome's tick) and the client counts one for each tick (`ClientWorld::world_clock`). So everyone has the same sun, a world left at noon is at noon when started again, and a recording has the day it was recorded in. **[run]** (`server/tests/behaviour.rs`, `client/tests/stream_handling.rs`, `game_shell/src/sun_view_tests.rs`, which includes a recording).
- **Hours** are worked out from whole ticks of the day, so a world of any age is as exact as a new one (`Sun::hours_at`; a test at 2.4 x 10^15 ticks). The direction is the noon direction turned about the axis by a twenty-fourth of a turn for each hour from noon, with `libm`.
- **What the sun does to a face**, for each pixel, in `voxel.wgsl` (`sunlight`) and, as the same arithmetic, in `engine/renderer/src/sun.rs` (`sunlight`, `sun_visibility`): `sun = sun_light * max(cos(angle to the face), 0) * visible`; the sky lights every face the sun reaches by `sky_light * visible` (shaded by the face's side as lamps are); and what there was, the sky's and the sun's light combine like screen blends with the light of lamps and the ambient light (`1 - (1 - a)(1 - b)`; the code adds `b (1 - a)`, which is the same and leaves a picture with no sun exactly as it was). **[run]** (`a_sun_with_no_light_leaves_every_picture_as_it_was...`, and all the older picture tests unchanged).
- **`visible`** is how much of the sun gets past what is in the way, 0 to 1 with a soft edge, from:
  - **balls**: each planet's *solid core* (`TerrainGenerator::solid_core`, the ball that is ground all through, which the horizon cull already used), where the planet is now. The sun is hidden when its disc is within the disc the ball takes up in the sky of the place; the edge is a smoothstep in angle, as wide as the sun's disc (0.005 rad) plus, for a place at the ground of the ball, a twilight (0.1 rad, about six degrees, falling off as `(radius / distance)^8`). A ball smaller in the sky than its edge is wide hides only part of the sun (an annular eclipse; a speck hides none). The balls multiply, so a moon in front of a planet's sun is an eclipse. At most eight balls (the nearest) are used.
  - **a flat ground** (the overworld): the sun is hidden when it is below the horizon (`y` up), with the same soft edge.
- **A face at the ground of a ball is lit by a turned normal.** The ground of a planet of voxels is faces that look along the axes of its cube whatever way the ground goes, so from far the planet was three flat patches of light and the sun went off a whole cube face at once (**[run]**: a first picture at 18:00 from above the pole was black). A face within a hundredth of the ball's radius over its ground, or inside it, is lit by the normal half-way between its own and the way out of the ball; the turn is gone by three hundredths of the radius, so a ship above a planet is not turned. **[run]** pictures: a lit globe with a soft terminator (section 6).
- **A face inside the core is lit as if it were on the ball's surface**, in its direction. (A far node's faces may be under the ground by a cell, hundreds of voxels; my first rule put them in the dark and the planet was black from 250 km: **[run]**.)
- **The sky** (`sun_view`): its colour is the game's colour times the daylight at the camera (the same rule at the camera's place) times the air there: 1 up to half of `thickness` over the nearest planet's core, fading to 0 at `thickness`, so the sky is black in space. The sky's light fades with the air too. The fog is the sky's colour as ever.
- **The sides of faces are shaded from one fixed direction** when a sun is on (`Atmosphere::side_direction`), so a lamp lights a wall as it did at every time of the day.

## 3. Cave's sun

A day of twenty minutes of the world's clock (1,200 s: 50 s an hour), beginning at 06:30 (it began at 07:00 until the owner asked for a lower sun at the start, 2026-10-28: the sun is 7 degrees up at the pole then, and shadows are long and easy to see; a world already made keeps its clock, so its hour moves back by half an hour; `--time` and `.time` pin any hour), in the level of space and in the overworld (no other level has a sun). Noon is at the direction (0, 0.94, 0.342) turned about (0, -0.342, 0.94): the sun is never straight overhead at the pole where players start (70 degrees at noon), rises towards +x and sets towards -x, and is 12 hours above the horizon and 12 below there. `light` 255, `sky_light` 110, sky `[0.55, 0.72, 0.92]` (the blue it always had) to 30 km over a planet. **All of these numbers are mine; the owner overrules** (a longer day, a different start, a dimmer sky).

## 4. For the player

- `.time` says the time of day by the world's clock; `.time 15:30`, `noon`, `midnight` pins the sun for this player (the client's own setting: nobody else's, nothing in a recording); `.time auto` lets the clock move it.
- `--time TIME` on the command line does the same for a window, a screenshot (`cave --screenshot x.png --time 18:30`) or a recording being watched (`replay FILE --time noon`).

## 5. Tests

Renderer: the rule on the CPU (14 unit tests: edges, sunsets that only darken, eclipses, a billion light years, the turn of normals); the shader on every adapter against the rule (sunsets over a planet, a face inside a ball, a face on the flank, 8 more); a sun with no light leaves every picture as it was. Protocol and client: the welcome's layout is pinned, the clock counts. Shell: the sun follows the clock, two players and a recording agree, `.time`, `--time`. Cave: the planet's core is the one ball in the sun's way, the sky at noon, midnight and in space, the overworld's day, and **a picture on every adapter** (the ground and the sky at noon, at 18:30 and at midnight, through `light_the_scene`, as a window runs it).

**Found by the tests on four adapters, and not by one:** on the integrated AMD GPU in DX12, `asin(1.0)` is not a number, so every place on or inside a ball was black there while the other three adapters were right. The shader asks `asin` of at most 0.999999 now (`MOST_OF_A_BALL`; a thousandth of a radian), and the test of a face inside a ball is what keeps it.

## 7. Limits, known and not hidden

- **Shadows are the next phase, built the same day** (`shadow-spec.md`): a hill shades what is behind it and a cave under a sunlit hill is in its shade. This phase had none: the planet's own terminator was the only shadow.
- **Nights were black** until section 8 (a night light and stars). There is still no moon.
- **The terminator is that of the core**, a ball 280 voxels smaller than the planet (about 4 degrees of arc, in the twilight's width), and the hills' own horizon is not in it.
- **A sunset's light is white** still: since section 8 the sky, the fog and the disc go warm, but the light on the ground is the sun's white, dimmer.
- **Far nodes are lit by the same rule** (they have no light of their own), so rivers and planets are lit from anywhere (the river is a flat blue: water has no shading but the sun's).
- **Eight balls**: a level with more than eight planets near the camera has the farther ones not shade.
- **The cost is none that I can see:** `cave --benchmark --size 1280x720` at the pole, release, RTX 3090, alternated with the commit before (built in a worktree): 3.90 ms against 3.90 ms and 4.41 against 4.36 ms average, over 200 frames each, twice. **[run]** (The planet's ball is in play there; the loop is at most eight balls per pixel.) Not measured on the integrated GPU.
- **Only Cave has a sun.** Packs cannot say one yet (a `sun` in a level's data is an easy thing to add).
- **The sky looked at nothing** until section 8: it is a disc, a glow and the stars now (and still a flat colour otherwise: no gradient from the zenith to the horizon, no scattering).

## 8. The sun as a star: the heavens, a night light and a sunset (built the same day, the owner's "the sun should be an actual star, like any other, visible from the surface of the planet", and "continue with the plan")

What I ran or looked at is marked **[run]**; what I believe from reading the code **[belief]**.

**How I read the owner's words.** The sun is one of the stars, a near one: the same field that has the stars at night has the sun in it, at one place for good, and it is seen from the ground of a planet as a disc and from space as a disc among stars. The plan I had given was a night light, a sunset colour, a sun disc and stars, in that order, and this phase is all four. Everything in the list of numbers below is mine; the owner overrules.

- **The sky pass** (`voxel.wgsl`, `sky_vertex` and `sky_fragment`; `engine/renderer/src/sky.rs` for the rule on the CPU). One triangle over the picture, drawn **after the terrain with the depth test `Equal` to the clear value** (depth is reversed, so what nothing was drawn at is 0): it costs only the pixels the terrain leaves. Each pixel's direction comes from the inverse of the camera's matrix. What it draws is `Atmosphere::heavens` (`Heavens`): the fog's colour (the sky's) behind, a glow round the sun, the stars, the sun's disc over them. **An empty `Heavens` (the default) draws nothing, and every picture is as it was.** **[run]** on four adapters: the depth test holds on the RTX 3090 and the integrated AMD GPU, in Vulkan and DX12.
- **The stars** are a field, not a picture and not random: the sphere is six faces of a cube, each 128 x 128 cells, **equal in angle to within a tenth** by a warp (an arctangent) of the face's coordinates, and a cell has a star if a hash of its face and place says so: 14 in 256, so about 5,500 in all, mostly faint (brightness 0.2 plus 0.8 of a cube of a byte), from blue-white to orange, a dot a pixel or so across (a pixel's width in radians is told to the shader, so a star is a pixel in a window of any size and a field of view of any width). `sky.rs` is the same rule on the CPU (`star_in_cell`, `star_cell_of`, `star_hash`) so that a test can aim a camera at a star that is there. **The field turns with the day**: `Heavens::star_frame` takes a direction of the world to one of the field, and `Sun::star_frame_at(hours)` is the turn of the sky since noon undone, so **the sun is at the same place in the field all day** (the direction of noon) and the stars go round it as the sun goes round the world. **[run]** (a unit test over 48 times of day).
- **The sun's disc** is `SUN_DISC_RADIUS`, 0.02 radians, with a pixel's soft edge; the real sun's is 0.0047, so this is four times its width (about 22 pixels across in a picture 1280 wide at the usual field of view: a speck would not be a sun in a window); the owner's to overrule (one constant). Its glow is the sun's colour times `0.5 / (1 + (angle / 0.15)^2)` times the air's (below). The disc is hidden by whatever is drawn over it (a hill, a planet), so **from the ground the sun rises over the hills and goes down behind them, and from space it is a disc among the stars with no glow (no air)**. **[run]** (pictures, section 8.4).
- **What the shell tells it** (`sun_view`, `Sun::night_light`): *the stars' light* is `1 - smoothstep(0.03, 0.4, daylight x air)`, so they come out as the sky darkens, are all there at midnight and **in space even at noon**; *the disc's colour* is warm white at noon and orange at a sunset, times the daylight at the camera (so it is out when the planet hides the sun); *the glow* is daylight times air.
- **A sunset** is a bump in the sun's height: `sunset_amount(elevation)` is 0 above 0.17 radians (10 degrees), 1 at the horizon and 0 again 0.1 radians under it. It takes the colour of the sky and the fog (so the far hills fade into it) 80% of the way to orange (`[1, 0.5, 0.28]` of the brightest part of the sky's own colour, so it is as bright as the sky and the same orange whatever colour a game's sky is by day), and the disc and glow to orange. **On a planet the horizon is that of the solid core**, a few degrees under the ground's own (the terminator is the core's: section 7): `horizon_dip` adds the angle whose cosine is the core's radius over the camera's distance from its middle to the sun's height, so that the sunset is when the light is going and not when the disc touches the ground. **[run]**: before this, the first pictures had a salmon sky at 17:50 when the daylight was still full and a plain pale blue at 18:20 when it was going. The sunset is of the air: none in thin air.
- **The night light** (`Atmosphere::night_light`, `Sun::night_light`: Cave 40 of 255): a light that lights every face **the sun does not reach, where the sky is open**: `night_light x (1 - visible) x uncovered` in the shader, with the sky's own cover (`sky_light_under_cover`, shadow-spec section 8) as `uncovered`, so **a cave is as black at night as by day** and the open ground is dim and still. By day it is nothing. It needed two changes to the maps' planning: **a sun below a flat horizon no longer stops the planning** when there is a night light (it plans the sky maps and no sun maps), and **the cover is applied whether or not the sun's maps were drawn** (before, it was inside the block for the sun's maps, which a night does not have). **[run]** (a hall with a roof at night; a night light with no sun light and no sky light).
- **For the player and the game.** Nothing to set for a player. A game says it in its `Sun` (`night_light`); a pack cannot yet (a `sun` in a level's data is still the easy thing to add).

### 8.1 Tests (first, then the code)

Renderer unit (7): the hash (pinned values), the density and the spread of brightness of the stars, a star is in the cell it was made for and in the middle of it, cells equal in angle from the middle of a face to its corner, the same density on all six faces, an empty sky is the default, and the shader's constants held to Rust's (the number of cells, the chance, the angle of a cell). Shell (8): the frame of the stars over 48 hours (the sun at noon in it all day, a turn, right-handed, once a day), the shape of a sunset, warm sky and disc at sunset and not at noon, the stars out as the sky darkens and in space, the night light in air and in space, no up and no sunset, a planet's sunset at the core's horizon, and a sun that is white in thin air to the last. **Found by the tests and not by looking**, all of them mine: the shader's `STAR_CELL_ANGLE` was a digit off the rule's (`0.012271846` against `0.012271847`: the constant check caught it at once); a star a half pixel from the middle of the picture is a third as bright as one on a pixel's middle, so the star tests turn the camera by a half pixel four ways and take the brightest (the failure looked like a faint star as bright as the star); the cover was not applied at night because it lived inside the block of the sun's maps (the first night test); a test with a sun 0.1 radians from the middle of a picture 0.17 across sees the disc at its corner.

### 8.2 Measured

`cave --benchmark --size 1280x720 --time noon|midnight`, dev profile (optimized), 200 frames, against the commit before in a worktree, alternated, four rounds: **no difference I can measure** on the integrated AMD GPU (noon 9.97 against 10.21 ms, midnight 9.59 against 9.62; the spread between runs is about a millisecond) and on the RTX 3090. **A picture of nothing but sky** (pitch 70, midnight; every pixel is a star's lookup): RTX 3090 0.57 against 0.55 ms, **integrated 1.54 against 0.67 ms**: the worst case, a millisecond over the weak GPU's whole frame of an empty sky. The first version drew the sky *before* the terrain over the whole picture, and the weak GPU measured about 0.6 ms more for it in a frame of 10, with the spread of the machine over it: **drawing it after, over the pixels the terrain leaves, took it out of the measure**.

### 8.5 Limits, known and not hidden

- **The sun is four times as wide as the real one**, a constant (`SUN_DISC_RADIUS`).
- **The horizon of the sunset on a planet is the core's**, not the hills': the sun may be behind a hill and the sky still at noon-blue, and the sun's disc is gone behind the ground's horizon some degrees before the light goes (the same limit as the terminator, section 7). In flat country the two are the same.
- **The sky is one colour** (and the fog): no gradient from the zenith to the horizon, no scattering, no reddening round the sun apart from the glow's colour; the glow is a fixed falloff.
- **No moon, no planets, no sun in the stars of another world**: a star field of one hash; the same stars on every planet and in space (they are the world's, not a planet's).
- **The stars do not twinkle** and are the same size at every brightness but the radius's small growth: no flicker (it would need a clock, and the rule is no wall-clock in cosmetics).
- **The night light is one number** for the whole night: no phase, no moon's light, no colour (it is white, so the night is grey-green and not blue).
- **A sun that is not in a level** has no sky of its own: a game without one is drawn as it was.
