# Far nodes for edited terrain and for ships (a plan, 2026-11-16)

Status: **a plan, nothing built; the owner answered on 2026-11-17 (section 7), and question 3 is reopened and worked out in section 8** (which changes F4 and F5). It finishes what `lod-spec.md` left open: the server's correction of far
nodes after an edit (`world-and-sky.md`: "Not done"), step 38j (ships), and the planet's nodes (`planet-spec.md`, "Not done" (c)). Marks as in `lod-spec.md`: **[code]** is what I read in the code,
**[run]** what I ran, **[belief]** what I did not check.

## 1. What is true today

- **[code]** The server half exists: `coarse_streaming::CoarseServerPlugin` keeps an *override* for each cube of detail level 1 to 12 that holds an edited brick (the generator's node patched with the exact cells of the edited
  bricks and of the overrides below), remakes dirty ones finest first (up to 32 a tick handed to **worker threads** since F3, one to four since F5 step 7; they were made on the thread of the tick before), and sends each client the overrides of the cubes it selects, coarsest first, within a byte credit. The client half
  exists: `LodView::set_override` (keyed by grid) makes the node from the override instead of the generator and replaces it where it is. A test in `game_shell/src/coarse_view_tests.rs` shows an edit far away in a watcher's
  view, a late arrival seeing the same, and a recording showing the same **[run, passes]**.
- **[code]** **No game installs the plugin**, and Cave asks for 0 bytes a second (`Cave::grid_detail`, `level_detail`), so in the game a dug hole shows in the bricks round the player and never in the nodes beyond. The reason
  written down (2026-10-23) is the cost of the generator's node in the overworld, 60 to 375 ms; **I have not re-measured it** and the build log suggests the overworld's nodes were made faster since (`build-log.md`, columns). The overworld is
  drawn in bricks only, so it does not need nodes at all; the level of space and the planet do, and their generators make a node in a millisecond or two.
- **[code]** The plugin handles **root grids only** (`State::mark_brick` returns for any other grid). A **planet** is a grid, so a block built on it, or a hole dug in it, is in no node. `ShipTerrainMesher` has a `LodView` but is
  never given overrides. A **plain ship** has no nodes at all: `GridMesher` draws bricks only.
- **[code]** **A ship is known to a client only if its bricks meet the watched region** (the view radius, 12 bricks by default, plus 2 or 4), or it is a planet, or the client's body is aboard it (`grid_replication::grid_messages`). A ship
  farther off is not in the picture: **a rocket 600 voxels away is not drawn** (read from the code; I have not flown it). The server sends all the bricks of a ship it tells of, up to 16,384 for a client.
- **[run, fixed by F1, 2026-11-17]** The client used to keep the overrides it was sent only in the `LodView` it had. The session makes a **new** `LodView` when the player goes to a level the game draws differently (`follow_level_detail`), and the server's record
  of what it sent (`ClientState::sent`) stays, and `SetDetail` does not clear it, so a game that installed the plugin and had both kinds of level lost its overrides for good. A test showed it (a crater dug in a coarse level was gone from the picture after a visit to a bricks-only level and back).
  The session now keeps every `CoarseNodes` update (`game_shell::coarse_overrides`) and gives them to any view it makes. **Not reachable in any game today** (no game installs the plugin).

## 2. What matters in Cave, in my order (a belief about what the owner will see first, not a measurement)

1. **What players build on or dig out of the planet, seen from orbit or from a few kilometres.** A planet is the main place a player builds. Cost to do: moderate (the machinery exists; the grid is not a root grid).
2. **A ship seen from far away** (a rocket leaving, two ships docking at a distance, a base on a moon that is a ship). Today it is not drawn past about 450 to 500 voxels (the default view radius is 12 bricks, and the region watched is 2 or 4 bricks more). Cost to do: large (the wire changes).
3. **Edits to the level of space's own terrain.** Rare in play; the plugin already does it once it is installed.
4. Edits in the overworld seen far away: **not needed**, the overworld is bricks only.

## 3. The design

**3.1 One mechanism for every grid.** An override is, as now, the cell summary of a cube made from *exact* data where there is any and from a base where there is not. The generalisation is only the base and the data:

| grid | base node | exact data |
|---|---|---|
| a level's root (as built) | the level's generator | edited bricks of the level |
| a planet (a ship with terrain) | the planet's generator, none outside its extent | the ship's stored bricks |
| a plain ship | empty | the ship's bricks (all of them are stored) |

`remake` takes a `Grid` of bricks and an optional generator and does not care which. `mark_brick` stops refusing non-root grids. The message already names the grid. Whether the *edit event* of a ship reaches the plugin is
**[belief]**: the plugin listens to `on_channels` with the event's grid, and a ship's `set_moving_voxel` records the same kind of change; the first test of F2 settles it.

**3.2 The server's cost.** (F3 built the worker, 2026-11-17: the generator's work is off the tick; what follows is the cost as it was.) Every dirty cube cost a call to the generator for the whole cube (`terrain.coarse_node(cube)`) on the thread of the tick, up to 8 a tick, up to 12 levels per edit. For a planet and for the level of
space this is milliseconds **[belief: from the build log, to be measured in F0]**. Two changes that do not change what is sent: do the work on a worker, as `prefetch.rs` does for bricks, and remember the generator's node of a cube
(it never changes once released, so the memory needs no invalidation, only a limit). Not recommended: sending only the patch and letting the client merge it with its own generator node. It would take the generator off the server
and cut bytes, but a parent's cells depend on the children's *exposure*, which is deliberately not sent, so the client could not make a coarser node from a patch alone. I have not tried it; this is reasoning.

**3.3 Who is sent what.** For a grid that stands still the selection stays as built: the same function on both ends from the viewer's brick and the `SetDetail` values (`lod-spec.md` 3.5).
A **moving** grid (a planet on a rail at 125 m/s moves 6 voxels a tick, a new brick about every 5 ticks; a rocket at 6 km/s moves 300 voxels a tick, 9 bricks) puts the viewer in a different brick of the grid's own axes every few ticks or every tick, so the per-client selection would be remade that often (about 4 ms for a
sphere **[measured in 38f]**, per client, per grid). Proposal: for moving grids send by **bands of distance**, not by brick: the levels from a coarsest down to `k`, where `k` follows the distance from the viewer to the box of the grid in steps of one detail level with a margin, so
a client far from a ship gets only its coarse nodes and one that comes closer gets the next level down before it needs it. It sends a little more than the exact selection and costs nothing per tick. To be decided after F0's numbers.

**3.4 A ship that is known by its nodes only (the wire).** `GridsSpawned` gets a **tier**: *near* (as now: all the bricks follow) and *far* (no bricks; the nodes arrive as `CoarseNodes`). A far ship is told of when it would be a few pixels
across (`lod-spec.md` 3.7 is the rule; it was never built), and promoted to near by the bricks being sent when its bounds meet the region; the other way, demoted, by the bricks being dropped and the ship kept. It costs no brick of the
budget (`maximum_ship_bricks`). Things to settle: whether the **entities aboard** a far ship are told of (the pilot of a far rocket: I would say yes, they are in `entity_messages` through `known_grids` already); the **protocol version** goes up (it is 11 today, so 12; an earlier draft of this plan said 9, which was wrong)
(owner, before the first release: formats may break); a **recording** carries it unchanged (it is a message stream), and a test shows a replay of a far ship draws the same.

**3.5 The client.** (a) The overrides belong to the **session**, not to a view: one store, keyed by grid, that a `LodView` reads (`set_override` now writes into the view). A view that is made again is given what the server has already sent.
That is the whole fix of the mismatch in section 1, and it is small and stands alone (F1). (b) `CoarseNodes` for a grid goes to the view that draws that grid: the level's `LodView`, a planet's `ShipTerrainMesher`, or a far-ship view.
(c) A far-ship view is a `LodView` over a `CoarseTerrainSource` for a ship with no generator: `known(brick)` is the stored brick or an empty one inside the bounds, `coarse_generator` is none (the override is the only source), and
**`version()` must change when the ship's bricks do** (today a planet's is the constant 0 because nothing about it changes, and the may-show memo is keyed by it; a ship's changes, so a stale memo would hide a node). `GridMesher`
stays for ships that are near.

**3.6 Hard rules.** Nothing here is simulation: nodes are cosmetic, so no cross-platform bit identity is needed beyond replay (which a message stream gives). The rate limit is by ticks (a credit refilled each tick), not by clock.
Saved files do not change (coarse data is never saved, owner's decision 16). A ship's cap on coarseness (38j) is a separate small field, not part of this plan.

## 5. Risks

- **The wire change (F5)** touches the replication of ships, the budget, entities aboard, the client's idea of which ships it knows and every golden recording. It is the step to stop at and say so if it grows, as 38a was.
- **A moving grid's selection** (3.3) is a guess until F0.
- **A far ship that is also a planet's neighbour** (a base on a moon): its nodes are in its own axes and its pose is the world's, so it is drawn right; the check that nodes of a ship that turns are placed exactly is `lod-spec.md` 3.3's property test, which holds for any grid.
- **Exposure and parents**: an override that is made from other overrides depends on their exposure, which is not sent; this only matters on the server and is unchanged.

## 6. What I would not do

- Send a ship's nodes to a client that already holds its bricks: it can make them itself from the bricks (`coarse_node_of_brick`); only the far tier needs the server's.
- Turn the plugin on in the overworld: its nodes are not drawn there and the generator's cost is what stopped it.

## 8. Ships far away (the owner's questions of 2026-11-17, answered from the code; reading, not runs, unless marked)

**8.1 What "known" means.** A client *knows* a ship when the server has told it the ship exists (`GridsSpawned`) and sent its bricks; a ship the client does not know is not drawn at all. Today **[code]** a ship is known if its bricks
meet the region watched (the view radius plus 2 or 4 bricks, about 450 to 500 voxels), or it is a planet, or the client's body is aboard it. The pixel rule of `lod-spec.md` 3.7 (known while a node of it would be a pixel across) was written and never built.
At 1080 lines and a 70 degree view a pixel is 0.0013 radians, so under that rule **a ship 10 voxels long is known out to about 7.7 km and a 1 km ship to about 770 km** (arithmetic, not a run).

**8.2 Is a ship ever simulated beyond the simulation distance?** The rule **[code: `grid_lifecycle::update`, `ShipSettings`]**: moving and edited within 1,024 voxels of the nearest brick of the ship (so already by the size of the ship: a ship's *near edge* counts, not its middle),
frozen beyond (1,126 on the way out, a tenth more), **parked in a file and out of memory beyond the unload distance** (1,024 too by default, so a ship is parked as soon as it freezes). Only players in the same level count; a level with no player has nothing awake.
The exceptions I found:
- **A planet and a ship on a rail are never parked** and are brought in at once whatever the distance. They are *frozen* when no one is near, but **their rail goes on** (its place is a function of the clock, `apply_rails` for all of them every tick, with the hand-overs between bodies at their exact instants). A coasting ship becomes a rail ship, so a ship thrown into orbit moves at any distance. That is movement, not physics: nothing of its rules, collisions or edits runs.
- **Ships coupled by joints are one group** kept by the nearest member: the far end of a long train is awake 1,000 or more voxels from every player; a ship in a file coupled to one on a rail comes in with it.
- **The host's setting** (`everywhere`, tests use it too) turns distances off.
- The other way: a ship that **waits for its pilot** is parked and is *not* brought in by a near player.
- A saved world has all its ships in files at the start; they come in when a player is within 1,024 voxels (or at once, for rails and planets).
- **Unpiloted ships** are simulated like any other within the distance (`players-and-ships.md`); the pilot does not matter.
Not checked: whether any *plugin* ticks a frozen ship by itself (I read the lifecycle plugin and the docs, not every rule). What this means for far display: **a ship that is farther than 1,126 voxels from every player is, for most ships, not in memory at all**, so the server has nothing to make nodes from without reading its file. The 8.5 proposal answers that.

**8.3 Are coarse nodes saved?** No, and nothing is planned yet that saves them **[code]**. Decision 16 ("never saved, rebuilt as needed") was made for terrain, where a generator can remake any node. For a ship **nothing can remake them but all of its bricks**. The ship file
(`world_storage::ship_file`) is a container of sections (`engine:ship`, `engine:grid_flight`, `engine:grid_terrain`, `engine:rail`, and any other, which is **kept byte for byte** by a reader that does not know it), so a new section is possible without breaking anything. But a **separate file** next to the ship's is better, in my opinion (medium confidence):
the ship's file is written whole when the ship is parked, and a sidecar can be written a cube at a time as the nodes are made; an external program that writes a huge ship can write the sidecar too, since it knows the shape; and a damaged or missing sidecar costs only the far view. It would be a **reversal of decision 16 for ships** only (the owner's word is the reversal; this is what the owner asked for).

**8.4 A very large ship ("a Death Star").** What would happen today **[code]**: bringing the ship in reads the **whole** file into memory (`read_ship` gives a whole `MovingGrid`), the first tick after it comes in walks **every voxel of every brick** for the rules (`take_changes`), and a client is sent its bricks only up to 16,384 (`maximum_ship_bricks`). So a ship of millions of bricks cannot be brought in *at all*, never mind drawn; that is a separate limit from LOD and needs bricks paged in as a level's are (not planned anywhere that I found; I would call it a prerequisite and say so to the owner).
What already exists: the **dirty-ancestor** machinery and the rate-limited remake of nodes after an edit (`coarse_streaming`; the generators' work is on a worker thread since F3); the bricks' own nodes are made by `voxel_meshing::coarse_node_of_brick` from a brick and its six neighbours. What is **not** built or planned: nodes that are kept between runs, nodes made from a file without bringing the ship in.

**8.5 The proposal (a far record).** (a) For every ship, parked or not, the server keeps a small **far record** in memory: its box, its pose or rail, its level, and its *coarsest nodes* (say the levels from 5 or 6 up: a few hundred cells for a ship of any size). The world already keeps the first three for a parked ship (`ParkedGrid`: bounds, pose, level, rail flag), so this is those plus the nodes.
(b) The nodes come from the **sidecar file** (8.3), read when the world starts or the ship is first seen. (c) A **worker thread** keeps the sidecar up to date: it builds missing nodes from the ship's bricks at low priority, so a ship made by another program gets them as time allows, and remakes the ancestors of a changed brick **while players edit** (the dirty marks exist; the worker is F3). It does not have to be quick, as the owner said; what it must do is *never block the tick* and *keep up on average*. A far client gets what exists and the rest as it arrives; the ship is drawn from what is there. (d) A tool command (`launcher prepare-ship FILE`) can build a sidecar offline for a ship made outside.
(e) **Parking distance by size.** I would keep the *simulation* distance at 1,024 voxels from the nearest brick (what moves and is edited is what a player can touch) and make the **unload distance grow with the size of the ship**, e.g. the larger of the setting and a multiple of the ship's longest side, because bringing a large ship back costs in proportion to its bricks and its far view should not flicker. This is an opinion (medium) and one number to choose with the owner; it needs the far record to be worth it, since a parked ship is drawn from the record.
(f) Far display is then decided by the **pixel rule with a minimum size** (8.1), and it needs no loaded ship.

**8.6 Entities (the owner's answer 4).** An entity kind is a set of components, each marked replicated or not (`EntityKindSpec::component`). A component such as *seen from afar* (the largest distance, or the smallest pixel size, at which it is told of and drawn) would let a kind decide, and a multi-voxel block made as an entity would set it from its size. For a far ship the server tells a client of the entities aboard that have the component and are near enough by their own rule. To decide: the component's name and unit, and whether a ship's far record keeps the list of such entities (it must, for a parked ship).

**8.8 F4a as built (2026-11-17).** The sidecar is `world_storage::far_nodes`: **a file named for the ship file it was made from** (`4294967296-12.voxship` has `4294967296-12.voxfar`) holding the checksum of that ship file (the world file already knows it for every ship in a file, so nothing is read to check), the names of the blocks used (never their numbers: the rule of every saved file) and the nodes, coarsest first, with their exposure (so the worker can go on from them). It is a `container` of three sections. A ship put away again gets a new ship file and so a new sidecar, which makes a stale one impossible to use by name and by checksum. Reading (`read_far_nodes`) gives `Found`, `Absent` or `Unusable(why)` (damaged, another state of the ship, a block this program lacks) and never a loss of the ship. Stores: `FarFiles` with a folder (the same one as the ship files; the extensions differ and neither listing sees the other) and a memory version. `remove_far_files_without_ship_file` cleans what the world file no longer names. The node bytes are shared with the wire: `coarse_cells::node_codec`.
**Not built, and why:** the *use* of it. Nothing makes nodes of a ship yet (F4), so nothing writes a sidecar when a ship is put away or saved and nothing reads one when the world starts; wiring that now would be code with no producer and no consumer. The pieces F4 needs from it are `FarNodes` (the record's nodes), `FarNodes::from_level` (the coarsest, for a ship that is not in memory), and the store. One limit: a file is read whole (`FarFiles::read` is a whole file), so a sidecar of a very large ship is read in full to take its coarse nodes; an index to read part of it can be added to the format as another section.

**8.9 F4 step 1 as built (2026-11-17).** The plugin keeps nodes for every ship in memory, a plain ship's from its bricks alone (empty where it has none, which is what the game sees there). They are made on the worker, from the same events as a planet's (a ship made while the server runs, or brought out of a file, is a change of every voxel it holds, and the plugin also marks what is in memory when it starts). A ship in a file **keeps** its nodes (they are what the far record is for) and a ship that is gone loses them (found when its nodes are next made, and by a sweep every 64 ticks). **Nothing is sent**: a client that knows a ship holds its bricks and makes its nodes itself (section 6), so a plain ship is not a place a client is selected in until the far tier (F5) exists; a test says so. **Not built, in order of what I would do next:** (1) the far record's use of the sidecar (**built in step 2, 8.10**); (2) the client's far-ship view (3.5c), which has nothing to draw until F5 sends a ship without its bricks; (3) `launcher prepare-ship`.

**8.10 F4 step 2 as built (2026-11-17): the sidecar's use.** The owner chose events. `ShipParked { grid }` (`engine:ship_parked`) is sent for each ship put into a file, in order, after the changes of the same drain; there is **no** `ShipLoaded` (a ship brought into memory is already a change of every voxel, which is all a plugin needs). The far files are **part of the ship files**: `ShipFiles::far_files_mut()`, one folder, cleaned by `WorldSave::write_with` with the ship files they were made from, so no host had to change. On the event the plugin writes the nodes it keeps for the ship, named for the file and with its checksum, **only if they are known to be of what the file holds**: a ship put away with nodes still to be made, or changed in the tick it was put away, has none written and none kept (and what the worker was making for it is thrown away), because the bricks are not in memory any more to make the rest; its far view is missing until it comes back. A planet is skipped. In its first tick the plugin reads the file of every ship in a file (`Found` keeps the nodes under new versions; anything else keeps none). **Not built:** the final save of a server writes ship files for the ships that were in memory with no far file, since a save does not ask plugins (a ship that is in memory when the game is closed has its nodes made again on the next load; a save hook would fix it); the client's far-ship view (3.5c) and `prepare-ship`.

**8.7 What changes in the phases.** F4 (ship nodes) and F5 (the wire) gain, in front of them: **F4a, the far record and the sidecar** (format, stores and tests built: 8.8) (the format, the reading, the writing; tests that a parked ship's nodes come from the sidecar and a damaged one costs nothing but the far view), and **F3 (the worker)** is no longer "if needed": it is where nodes of ships are made. The paging in of the bricks of a huge ship is a separate piece of work, outside this plan.

**8.11 F5 step 1 as built (2026-11-17): the far tier on the wire.** `GridState::content` is `Bricks`, `Generated(terrain)` or `Far { bounds }` (protocol 12). A far ship is told of with the box of its own bricks and **never gets a brick** while it is far; it is made near (or far) by being told to be forgotten and told of again in the same tick, which keeps one way in and one way out (the entities of a near ship come with the second telling, as for any ship). `ServerConfig::far_ships` (`FarShipSettings { minimum_apparent_size, maximum_ships }`, `None` by default): a ship of the client's level that is not in its picture is far while the longest side of its world box spans `minimum_apparent_size` radians from the middle of the watched region to the middle of the box (kept until 0.9 of it), nearest `maximum_ships` first; a planet and the ship the client is aboard are never far; **no edit and no entity aboard** a far ship is sent (an entity's being seen from afar is for its kind, 8.6, still open). The viewer is taken as the middle of the region the client asked to watch, since the server's replication does not know the client's body (the region is made round it); a pixel is a setting, not the client's (a pixel at 1,080 lines and 70 degrees is 0.0013, so 0.005 is about four). **Not built, in the order I would do it:** (1) the plugin sends the nodes of a far ship, selected by distance (the levels from a coarsest down to one that follows the distance: both ends must agree on it, since a client that selects a finer level than the server sent would draw a hole where a planet's generator would have filled it); (2) the client's far-ship view; (3) ships in files in the picture, from `ParkedGrid` (the box, the pose, the level) and the sidecar's nodes, which is where most far ships are (8.2); (4) Cave turns it on; (5) the 8.6 component.

**8.12 F5 step 2 as built (2026-11-17): the nodes of a far ship, sent and drawn.** **One selection on both ends** (`coarse_cells::select_far_ship`): the ordinary selection from the viewer's brick in the ship's own coordinates with no ball of bricks, so that no cube is a brick and the finest are nodes of level 1; the server sends (`far_ship_nodes_to_send`) those, every cube above them, and the eight a level finer inside each one coarser than level 1, coarsest first, among the cubes that have an override. The extra cubes are so that crossing a level (coming nearer, or going away) finds the next picture on the client already. **The hand-over:** `ServerWorld::far_ships_of(client)` is what the client was told of as far in the latest tick's updates (a plugin's system sees the tick before); the coarse plugin forgets what it sent of a ship that stops being far for a client, and sends it again if it is far again. **The client:** `ShipTerrainSource::far` under a `ShipTerrainMesher` with a ball of bricks of radius 0, one per far ship, drawn only by a game that draws terrain in detail; a ship told of again in the other tier is dropped whole and made afresh, as the events of the tick are taken in (not at the next draw: the nodes or bricks that arrive in a later tick must not be lost with the old ones). **Hooks:** `GameDefinition::far_ships()`, `build_server_telling_of_far_ships` (tests). **The "bands of distance" of 3.3 were not needed as such:** the selection from the viewer's brick, done on both ends with the pose the client was last told, is the band, and the extra levels either side are its margin; **whether that is enough for a ship that moves or turns fast is not measured** (the server's pose is a tick ahead of the client's). **Left:** ships in files (8.2: the box, pose and level are in `ParkedGrid`, the nodes in the sidecar); a client that keeps the old picture until the new has arrived (a rocket that comes nearer faster than the rate of the player's setting allows will see the ship thin out and come back); edits to a far ship (untested); Cave on; the entities aboard (8.6).

**8.13 F5 step 3 as built (2026-11-18): a far ship keeps its picture until it knows the finer one.** **Measured** (a probe that flew a player at a far ship): the ship **vanished whole** while the server's view of the player was stale (not thinned); holes need the viewer to close about three quarters of the distance (two levels, more than the one level of cubes sent in advance) before the server hears of it (60 bricks inside the lag in the runs, a jump of 100 bricks for one round at any lag); the server **ahead** of the client by up to ten rounds (the pose of a fast ship a tick ahead) caused none; Cave runs 20 ticks a second. The cause was that a far source has no generator, so a cube with no node was made as nothing and counted as ready: the client cannot tell empty from not yet arrived. **The fix:** the server says which cubes of the ship's box are empty (`far_ship_empty_cubes`: the cubes a client may draw by the box alone, and the halves of those it draws that hold a brick, each as the biggest cube that holds no brick; sent as a node with nothing in it, legal before and meaning the same for a ship with no generator, so no protocol change); the view of a far ship (`waits_for_nodes`) does not make a cube that has no node and has not been said to be empty (or to be in one that is), so the swap waits and the old picture stays; the cubes beside a node are not drawn for a far ship; the session keeps what was said empty for a mesher made later. **Not done:** the rate for a ship whose whole picture is more than the 200 KB a client may save up (the cubes come coarsest first, and what is drawn stays until the finer are known, so it should come in as the bytes do, **not run**); an edit to a far ship (the plugin's box for the ship is its box now, the client's is the one it was told); the selections of the two ends differing for a lasting reason would show as a coarse picture that never gets finer, quietly (`stalled()` says it for a test, nothing says it in the game).

**8.14 F5 step 4 as built (2026-11-18): ships in files, the sidecars at a save, and Cave on.** A ship in a file is told of as far from what the world knows of it without reading the file: its place (`ParkedGrid::pose`), the bricks of the world it touches (`bounds`) and **now the box of its own bricks (`ParkedGrid::extent`, in the world file as a fourth flag and a box; no box for a file from before, and then it is not told of)**; not a planet, not a ship that was on a rail, never as bricks. The plugin selects for it from the same two things and sends the nodes it kept of it. **A save writes the nodes of the ships in memory beside their new files** (`WorldSave::new_far_files`, made by what the world holds (`WorldData::set_far_file_maker`) and so by a game that ended as well as by an autosave), so a world started again has its ships in files with their far views; it writes none for a planet, a ship with no nodes or a ship whose nodes are still to be made. **Cave tells of far ships** (0.005 radians, sixteen: guesses). A far ship's view has one worker thread. **Looked at** (headless pictures): ships at 600 voxels (far, in memory) and 1,900 (far, in its file) are drawn from nodes with the shape of the near one. **Not built:** 8.2's exceptions are as they were (a planet and a rail ship are never in files); `prepare-ship`; a worker that makes the nodes of a ship put away before they were made; the rate for a big ship; edits; the 8.6 component; a check that the unload distance is above the region a client watches.

**8.15 F5 step 5 as built (2026-11-18): `prepare-ships`, and what was measured.** `voxunami prepare-ships GAME --world FILE` mends the three cases of a ship with no far view (an old world, a ship made by another program, a ship put away before its nodes were made) by running a server with no player over the world with every ship that can come in already in memory, until the plugins have no work in hand (`GameLogic::work_pending`), and saving: the save of 8.14 writes the node files and the box of each ship's bricks. Nothing moves (every ship is frozen, none is put away, the clock is put back); a ship that waits for those aboard, or whose file cannot be read, is left in its file and said. **Measured:** a ship of 256 bricks has 47 nodes, and the nearest picture of it is 32 nodes of about 8 KB (260 KB), the farthest 21 KB, so the 1 MB/s of Cave is no limit at this size (not run: ships ten and a hundred times as big); making its nodes took about 2 s on the worker and no tick took more than 2.4 ms; a player with sixteen far ships added 44 threads with one worker each and 364 with the pool of a planet's view. **Not built:** a worker that makes nodes while the server runs; preparing by starting the game; everything else in the lists of 8.14.

**8.16 F5 step 6 as built (2026-11-18): edits to a far ship, and big ships.** **Edits.** An edit inside the box of a far ship, one that fills a cube the server had said was empty, and one that digs a cube empty are seen by the player who is there and by the one who comes after, the same to the mesh (they already were). **A ship that grows was not, and is now:** a far ship is **told of again when its box changes** (the same despawn and spawn in one tick that a change of tier is: no new message), and the client's view works its cubes out again when the version of its terrain changes (a far source's version is that of its box). A box gets smaller only when a brick is taken out of the ship. **Big ships (measured, a probe):** nodes and bytes grow with the ship: 47 nodes for 256 bricks, 469 for 2,560, 3,028 for 20,480; **the nearest picture of the biggest is 608 nodes and 11 MB, 10.8 s at 1 MB/s** (the 256-brick ship's 0.34 MB is no limit); making the nodes of it was first written as **544 s with Cave's blocks, on the worker, which was wrong (8.17: that was the first tick of a world holding the ship, the colliders; the worker took about 6 ms a brick before 8.17, about two minutes for 20,480 bricks on one thread, an estimate not run)**, and the whole picture reached a player in about 32 s of play, coarsest first. **The tick thread was spending 10 to 25 ms on a tick of fifty copying nodes for the worker** (the nodes are shared now: 19.8 to 0.7 ms for 2,560 bricks). **Not decided:** whether the rate for far ships should be its own; **built in 8.17:** a faster worker for a world of such ships; **not run:** ships larger than 20,480 bricks, and memory.

**8.17 F5 step 7 as built (2026-11-19): a faster node worker.** What a job costs and how many threads make them (build log, "F5 step 7", for the measurements, the tests and the mutants). **The worker is a pool:** `CoarseConfig::node_workers` threads (default a third of the processors, 1 to 4), jobs numbered as they are handed over, results taken in by the tick **in the order of the numbers** so that the versions of the nodes do not depend on the threads' speed; a thread that panics sets a flag and the tick ends the server (the rule of 2026-11-16). **A job is cheaper:** `SubRect::mask` is arithmetic, a whole opaque cube with such cubes against all six of its faces in the brick is made without looking at its faces, the voxel in front is found without 128-bit arithmetic, and the brightness of a brick is put in place of the values of its channel as stored. **Measured** (a ship of 1,280 bricks, Cave's blocks): 7.47 s before, **0.71 s with the default of four threads** (2.45 s with one). **A correction of 8.16:** node making with Cave's blocks is about 6 ms a brick on one thread (as it was before this step), **not the 27 to 48 ms** (544 s for 20,480 bricks) that 8.16 first said: those times were of the whole first tick of a world holding the ship, which is `engine:grid_bodies_move` (5.3 s for 128 bricks: the terrain colliders under a probe ship that stood in the ground, and the ship's own bricks at 7.3 to 7.7 ms each; build log, "The first tick of a world that holds a ship"), not the worker. **Not built:** a cache of the facts of each model in the brick's loop; the short way for a half of a node that is all one cell in `patched_with_child`; a measured number of threads; nothing was done about the colliders (a ship of 20,480 bricks that comes into memory stalls one tick for about 2.5 minutes, by the figures of that entry: an extrapolation).
