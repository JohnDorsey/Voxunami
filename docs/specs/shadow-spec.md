# Shadows (Phase 41b)

Status: built 2026-10-28 after the sun (`sun-spec.md`), at the owner's "Continue", with this note: *"I'm hoping to modify the terrain of the overworld someday to include huge mountains. Like thousands or millions of blocks tall. So, the shadow maps shouldn't break at that scale."* What I ran or looked at is marked **[run]**; what I believe from reading the code **[belief]**.

## 1. What the owner wanted and what this is

The owner's direction (2026-10-27): shadows from shaders in place of the flooding of sky light "for most sky light". The order I recommended: the sun as a light of the world's clock (41a), then **cascaded shadow maps for what is near (this phase)**, then a cheap ambient term for caves. A hill now shades the valley behind it, a cave under a sunlit hill is dark by day, and the shadows are right at every size of world there is: a block four voxels high and a mountain four million voxels high are the same picture (section 3).

## 2. The rule

A shadow map is a picture of the world from the sun, drawn without perspective, that keeps for each texel the surface nearest to the sun. A place is in shadow if something is nearer the sun than it is, in its texel. The code is in `engine/renderer/src/shadow.rs` (the geometry: pure functions, tested on the CPU), `shadow_maps.rs` (the texture, the sampler and the pipelines), `voxel_renderer.rs` (`plan_shadows`, the passes) and `voxel.wgsl` (`shadow_in_cascade`, `terrain_light`, the two `shadow_vertex_*`).

- **Cascades.** One map is not enough: a texel must be about as wide as a pixel, and a picture shows things from a hand's breadth to the edge of the world. So there are up to eleven (`MAXIMUM_CASCADES`) maps of 1024 x 1024 texels (`CASCADE_RESOLUTION`) of squares across the light whose widths are, in half widths, 32, 64, 128, 256, 512 voxels (doubling: a texel of a sixteenth of a voxel to a voxel, as fine as the terrain the player is near), then 2,048 (fourfold), then 16,384, 131,072, ... eightfold (the terrain beyond is coarse nodes). `half_widths(distance)` stops at the first whose reach is the distance the picture is drawn to (the fog's end), so a short view has few maps and the cost grows with the logarithm of the distance. The eleven reach 59 million voxels; further than that the last is the coarsest and the rest of the picture is not shadowed.
- **A place looks in the finest cascade that holds it**, by its distance from the camera (the pixel's own distance, not the vertices' interpolated one: **found by a test**, the interpolated distance is wrong across the big quads of coarse nodes and chose a cascade too coarse), mixed with the next over the last 15% of the reach so the line between two does not show. A place beyond the last has no shadow. Every cascade is centred on the camera and measured from nothing else; there is no fixed size of world anywhere in it.
- **Only the faces that look away from the sun are drawn into a map** (the backs of things). A place the sun lights is never on one, so it cannot be in the shadow of its own surface, whatever the angle; the bias is only for the width of a texel, and it is in two parts, both in texels of the cascade, so a shadow looks the same at every scale. The place looked up is eased toward the sun by one texel (`DEPTH_BIAS_TEXELS`). What the map keeps of a face is eased away from the sun by how steeply the face's own depth changes across a texel, one and a half times (`CASTER_SLOPE_BIAS`: the pipeline's slope-scaled depth bias, in `shadow_maps.rs`): the underside of a floor a place on the floor looks up is as steep as the floor and needs it, and the wall of a block is as steep as its own angle to the light says. (The first version eased the *place* by the steepness of *its* face, up to four texels more, which left the floor lit for up to five texels, a third of a voxel in the nearest cascade and more in the next, beside the wall of a block under a low sun: the owner saw gaps, 2026-10-28. The contact test `under_a_low_sun_the_shadow_of_a_block_begins_at_its_foot...` fails on it. The tests fail with the caster's bias at half a unit and pass from one, and fail with the place's at a quarter of a texel and pass from a half; the values are a unit and a half and a unit.) The shadow is still blurred over about a texel's footprint along the ground, which is a texel over the sine of the sun's height: seven times as long at 8 degrees up. (A first version also moved the place a texel along its normal. The mutation experiments showed that nothing needed it, and it was taken out.) A face whose normal looks away from the sun is in the shade of its own body and is not looked up. At the ground of a planet the *turned* normal of the sun's rule is the one looked up with (a planet of voxels has faces that look away from the sun and are lit by the turn).
- **Depth** is measured from the far side of the sphere round the camera that a cascade is for, as a fraction of the distance from there to the highest caster, so a place in the sphere has a depth near 0, where a float has the most precision, and a mountain a million voxels toward the sun is at 1 where a float has none to spare and needs none. The range is fitted to the casters each time a map is drawn and is at most 2^40 voxels (`HIGHEST_CASTER`). **[run]** (below) a mountain half a million voxels high shades floor voxels in front of it to within a voxel of where its ridge puts the shadow.
- **What is drawn into a map** (`Cascade::wants_caster`): what is over the square the map covers (with a texel and its own radius to spare), not wholly below the map's back, not higher than 2^40, and not in a sphere smaller than two texels: a cascade that covers a mountain range has no use for a tree, nor for the bricks of the ground round the camera (the sphere of a brick is 28 voxels: it is drawn into the cascades whose texel is under 14). Things that move (players, boxes, ships) are drawn only into the maps of a texel of a voxel or less.
- **The sun's light and the sky's.** The terrain's shadow takes the sun's light as it is. It takes the sky's light only in part: a place in the shadow has `sky_light_in_shadow` of 255 of it (Cave: 70, a little over a quarter), because the sky is scattered light and a shadow is not black; and a face turned from the sun has the same, fading in from edge on to a cosine of 0.15 so that a wall does not change in a moment as the sun crosses its plane. (This is a proxy for the sky being hidden, which only a cave knows: the cheap ambient term for caves, the next step, replaces it.)

## 3. Scale: why mountains do not break it

Everything that could depend on a size of world was made not to, and held by tests:

- **Camera-relative.** The renderer's positions already were; the cascades' middles are on a grid of the world in `f64` (a step of an eighth of the half width, a power of two) and are only ever used as a small offset from the camera.
- **Biases in texels** of the cascade, depth fitted to the casters, and a depth that is a fraction of the range measured from near the receivers (above).
- **Every size of cascade is a power of two**, so a texel's place in the world is exact.
- **A test draws the same scene at every scale**: a node of detail level `n` is the cells of a brick, each `2^n` voxels wide, and with the camera, the near plane and the fog scaled the picture is the same: a block and a roof at levels 0, 1, 4, 9, 15 and 20 (cells of a million voxels: the block is four million voxels high, in a node 33 million voxels wide), checked against where the sun puts the shadow, on four adapters. **[run]** The edge of the block's shadow on the floor, searched for in steps of a hundredth of a cell, starts at 11.1 cells where the block puts it at 11.0, at levels 0, 6, 12 and 20, the same on all four adapters (a tenth of a cell is the sun's slant and the bias; it is the same at every scale, which is the point).
- **A mountain 524,288 voxels high** (a node of cells of 32,768) 524,288 voxels away, shading a floor of voxels near the camera: the shadow edge is at x = 17.0 where the ridge puts it at 17.0, on four adapters. **[run]**
- **Past the cascades' reach** (scenes of nodes of cells of 2^24, 2^40, 2^60 and 2^90 voxels) the picture is exactly the one with no shadows, and nothing is not a number.

## 4. Keeping the maps (what the cost is)

Drawing all the terrain of a picture into each cascade in every frame is not affordable: **[run]** the first version, which did, took 32 ms in a frame that took 4 ms (RTX 3090, Cave at 1280 x 720; 20 million quads in the maps against 2 million in the picture). A map is therefore **kept** while it is right (`plan_shadows`):

- A map is **wrong**, and drawn again at once, when it has none, or the camera has moved more than an eighth of its half width from where it was (`SLACK`: the middle of a map is on a grid of that size, so it is at most half that from the camera when drawn, and what a place looks up in a kept map is found by moving the camera's place, not by drawing again), or the sun has turned more than ten times 0.005 of sine since (`SUN_DRIFT`).
- It is **out of date**, and drawn again when it has waited and there is room, if the sun has turned more than 0.005 (a second of Cave's day, a shadow of something a quarter as high as the map is wide moving about a texel), or terrain has changed (a brick came or went or was edited) or something that moves has, for the fine maps. A map of terrain that changed waits 1, 1, 1, 2, 4, 8, ... frames, finest first (a block placed has its shadow in the next picture; a far map follows within a second); and each map waits for as many frames as it has times 1.5 million quads (and 300 for each caster, for the calls) to draw, with one map always allowed, so a big map is drawn across several frames and no frame draws them all.
- A cascade that nothing in the picture is looked up in is not drawn.

**[run]** Cave, 1280 x 720, release, `--benchmark` (average of 200 frames, the camera walking at 5 voxels a second and the sun at the pace of Cave's day, shadows on against off): RTX 3090 4.33 against 4.44 ms; the integrated AMD GPU 16.6 against 15.8 ms (95th percentile 17.9 against 17.0; one frame in 200 took 40 ms). With the camera still: 4.06 against 4.13 and 15.9 against 15.7 ms. With the camera flying at 30 voxels a second and the sun six times as fast (a hard case): 5.8 against 4.7 ms and 22.2 against 18.0 ms. The cost of a drawing of the maps is in the calls, not in the quads (about 11,000 calls for the biggest map of Cave): a pool of bricks in one buffer with indirect drawing would take most of it away, and is a change of the renderer's whole way of holding bricks.

## 5. For the player

- `.shadows` says whether the terrain's shadows are on; `.shadows off` and `.shadows on` change it for this player (the client's own setting, nothing in a recording). On by default; a slow GPU may want them off.
- `--shadows on|off` does the same on the command line, for a window, a screenshot or a recording being watched.
- A game says, per level and in its `Sun`, how much of the sky's light a shadow has (`sky_light_in_shadow`), and how much reaches a place with the terrain over it (`sky_light_under_cover`, section 8; Cave 0). A level with no sun has no shadows. `.shadows off` turns off both.

## 6. Tests

`shadow.rs` unit tests (19): the frame of the light (orthonormal, right-handed, continuous through a day), the cascades' sizes and reach, the middle of a map on a grid, the matrix, what is wanted and what is not (however far, however big, not numbers), the depth range at a hill, a mountain, ten million and 2^40 voxels (places a hundredth of a texel apart are told apart), the lookup's bias in texels at every scale, the move of a kept map. In `voxel_renderer.rs` (5): the order of a mesh's quads by face, which faces a shadow map is drawn from (turned by a ship's pose too), and the policy of when a map is drawn again, as a function (a map that is wrong, or none, whatever it costs; one out of date waits its time, its cost in frames, and room in the frame; the first in a frame is let in). Shell (4): `.shadows` and `--shadows`, and the atmosphere a session gives the renderer. Renderer on every adapter (`gpu_rendering.rs`, 13 new): the block and the roof at six scales; the mountain; a floor nothing stands on is as bright with the shadows on at every distance (five cascades and their seams) and at every sun to a grazing one; beyond the reach; a map is kept while the camera moves a little and is then where the sun puts it for the camera's new place, and is drawn again for a camera twenty cells away; a block placed or taken away, a sun that moves a little, a little more and jumps, and a player who stands and walks, each change the next picture as they should; a face turned from the sun has the shadowed share of the sky and gains all of it by a cosine of 0.15; a face of a planet's ground that looks away from the sun by its own normal and is lit by the turn is not shaded by its own body; shadows on with no sun are no change and cost nothing; the maps are drawn only while the sun can light something.

**Found by the tests, and not by looking:** the interpolated distance chose a cascade too coarse across a big quad of a coarse node (a roof edge was smeared over a cell and a half at two scales of nine); a sphere that holds a node and the camera makes every cascade look as if it serves, so a test that wants a map kept has the camera outside the node; the frame of the light, made from whichever axis of the world the sun is least along, jumped a quarter turn twice a day (the test of continuity through Cave's day); and the shadows of a scene of cells of 2^15 and 2^20 voxels were lost when I first made the cascades grow fourfold only (the cascades now grow eightfold beyond 2,048, to 59 million).

**Mutation experiments** (`PROPTEST_DISABLE_FAILURE_PERSISTENCE=1`, files restored from copies and compared by hash): 35 mutants of the shader, the geometry, the policy of keeping maps and the shell's wiring (the sun not shadowed, the sky not, the vertices' distance, no mix with the next cascade, a place beyond the last in shadow, no bias, no slope, a cascade not used chosen, the map's middle not on the grid, the camera's move not in the matrix, every face drawn, a policy that does not wait, night over flat ground, ...), **all killed**. The first run left four alive: one was a test of mine that the harness's filter did not name (killed once it did), and three were the normal offset, which no test needed (it was taken out, and the tests stayed green).

## 7. Limits, known and not hidden

- **The sky's light is occluded only near the camera and by what is over a place** (section 8; until the same day's next step it was not at all, and a cave in the shade had a quarter of the sky's light, as a hill's lee has). The hill's lee still has its quarter of the sky (the proxy), since taps 8 voxels across cannot see the dome behind a hill. The cover is **a heightfield** (the highest thing over each column): light that comes into a room through a window at the side, or into a cave through a mouth, is not counted beyond the rings of 8 voxels, and a place under two decks is told nothing of the lower; the cover counts no figure, ship or box; it reaches 205 voxels from the camera (farther the sky is open); and on a planet the way up is the camera's, so a place 200 voxels off on a small moon has a way up that is a few degrees wrong.
- **A map that is out of date lags**: a shadow of something tall follows the sun a second late at the coarsest, and a far map of terrain that was edited follows within a second or two.
- **Things that move cast shadows only into the fine maps** (a texel of a voxel or less, out to about 500 voxels), and a ship's pieces the same: the shadow of a ship on terrain far away is not drawn.
- **A caster smaller than two texels** of a cascade is not drawn into it: the trees and bricks of the ground round the camera do not shade the far terrain.
- **Only the shadows of the sun**: no moon, and the planets' shadows (a terminator, an eclipse) are still the ball's rule in `sun.rs`, which the terrain's shadow is multiplied with.
- **The cost is in draw calls** (section 4). A frame in which a big map is drawn is the slow one on a slow GPU.
- **A face nearly edge on to the sun that is lit only by a planet's turn** (the faces of a cube-sphere's steps, which look away from the sun by their own normal and are lit by the turned one) has a few texels of the filter deeper than the bias: in the test (a face 73 degrees from the sun's direction) it is 0.96 of the light, never dark. A steeper one has more of its texels in its own shadow.
- **The fog still uses the distance the vertices interpolate**, as it always did, which is too long in the middle of a quad that is wide for how far it is (a quad of a coarse node near the camera): the shadow lookup does not (section 2), the fog was left as it was so that no picture without a sun changes. **[run]** (seen in a test with a fog of 27 voxels over a floor of 32.)
- **Not tested in a window**: every look was a headless picture; the windowed path draws the same function each frame (`light_the_scene`).

## 8. The sky's cover (the ambient term for caves; built the same day, at the owner's "Continue")

**What it is for.** The owner's direction was a cave in the shade that is darker than a hill's lee. The sun's maps cannot say it: the sky's light has no direction, and a place in the shade of a hill and a place under it have the same shadow. So the sky has maps of its own. **[run]** unless marked.

- **The maps.** `SKY_CASCADES` (4) more cascades in the same texture (layers 11 to 14, `MAP_LAYERS` 15), of half widths 32, 64, 128 and 256 voxels (`sky_half_widths`), each a picture of **the terrain seen from straight above**: the highest thing over each column, kept by the same depth test as the sun's (depth grows toward the light). *Which way is up* is the camera's own: +y on a level of flat ground, the way out of the ball whose surface is nearest on a planet (`sun::zenith`, in the ball's own radii, as the turn of a face is), and none in empty space, where there are no sky maps. What is drawn into one is the **front** faces (the ones that look up), where the sun's maps are drawn from the backs: a solid hill has no face that looks down over a place beside a tunnel, so the rock beside it would read as open sky from the backs. Only terrain: a figure, a box or a ship is no roof. They do not depend on the sun, so a map is kept while the camera stays within an eighth of its half width of where it was and the terrain does not change (the same policy and budget as the sun's, `plan_maps`), whatever the sun does: **a test** turns the sun, walks a figure in, moves the camera a voxel and then twenty-four, and changes the terrain, and counts what is drawn again.
- **The lookup** (`open_in_sky_cascade` in `voxel.wgsl`; `Cascade::lookup_tap` and `sky_place` are the same arithmetic on the CPU). A place looks at **13 columns**: its own, six at 3 voxels from it across the way up and six at 8 (`SKY_TAPS`; the two rings turned a twelfth of a turn from each other), each a comparison lookup (the hardware's filter) of the place's own height, eased toward the sky by the texel, against the highest thing over that column. The share of the thirteen that find nothing higher is **how open the sky is** over the place. The sky's light of a pixel is multiplied by `mix(sky_light_under_cover, 1, open)`: the sun's light is not touched (a floor the sun reaches under a roof's edge has the sun's light and not the sky's: a test), and the proxy of a shadow (`sky_light_in_shadow`) is still there and multiplies with it: a hill's lee has no cover and keeps its quarter of the sky, because the dome is partly hidden by a hill that taps of 8 voxels cannot see. The finest cascade that holds the place is looked in and mixed with the next over the last 15% of its reach, and **beyond the last the sky is open**. A cascade's reach is that of the sun's less the farthest tap and a wall's push (17 voxels): **11, 39, 94 and 205 voxels** from the camera.
- **A wall is judged by the air in front of it.** A place on a face that looks sideways is moved 9 voxels (`SKY_WALL_PUSH`: the farthest tap and a voxel more) out along the part of its normal that is across the way up before it is looked up; a floor and a ceiling are not moved. **Found by looking**: a picture of a tunnel through a hill showed the hill's wall in the open a few percent darker with the cover on, since half of the columns round a place on it are the hill's. By the test: 0.505 of the sky at no push, 0.84 at 4 voxels, 0.96 at 8 (the farthest tap is on the wall's plane, where the filter takes some of the hill), 1.0 at 9. The way up is read in the shader from the third row of the cascade's matrix (a test holds that row to the way to the light over the range).
- **For the game and the player.** `Atmosphere::sky_light_under_cover` (renderer; 0 to 255, the share of the sky's light that reaches a place with the terrain over it; **255, the default, counts no cover and draws no sky map, and pictures are as they were**), `Sun::sky_light_under_cover` (the game's, per level; **Cave 0: a cave is black by day**, and lit by its lamps). `.shadows off` and `--shadows off` turn it off with the shadows. Chosen by me, the owner's to overrule: four maps, thirteen columns, the rings of 3 and 8 voxels.
- **Cost.** `cave --benchmark --size 1280x720` at the pole, dev profile (optimized), average of 200 frames, the camera walking at 5 voxels a second (0.083 a frame) or flying at 30 (0.5 a frame), with the cover counted and not, **in the same build** (the only difference is the cover): **RTX 3090** 5.48 against 5.40 ms walking and 6.18 against 6.06 flying; **integrated AMD** 18.63 against 17.32 ms walking (+7.6%) and 22.35 against 20.63 flying (+8%); the worst frames the same (59.9 against 57.7 ms). With the camera still on the integrated GPU: 18.3 against 17.2 ms with the lookup left out of the shader (the maps still drawn once) and 16.5 with no shadows at all. **Five columns cost the same as thirteen** (17.96 and 18.48 ms): the cost is in the lookup's overhead for each pixel (choosing the cascade, the matrix, the way up), not in the samples, so thirteen were kept. (The run-to-run spread on this machine is about 0.4 ms on the RTX and 0.5 on the integrated GPU, so what is above is about a millisecond on the weak one.)
- **Looked at.** A throwaway scene through the renderer, 640 x 360: a hill with a tunnel through it, the sun 53 degrees up, with and without the cover. The tunnel is black inside with a soft gradient over the first voxels from the mouth; the hill's wall and the floor in the open are as they were. Cave at its start, 1280 x 720 (`cave --screenshot`, dev profile): the open ground is as it was (no stain on a slope, no acne), and the shade under the ledges at the left is a little darker. *Not looked at:* a cave of Cave's own (the headless start cannot reach one: a camera off the pole sees no planet) and a window.
- **Tests (first, then the code).** Renderer unit tests (8): the zenith (flat ground, the nearest ball, the middle of a ball, the empty space, a billion billion voxels off), the sky cascades and their margins, the thirteen taps (one at the place, two balanced rings), a tap looks up the column that far across at the same depth, a wall's push (and a floor's and ceiling's none), the depth row of the matrix, the faces a sky map is drawn from (turned by a placement), and the shader's constants held to Rust's (the taps, the layer, the push). GPU, on four adapters (11, in `gpu_rendering.rs`): a hall with a roof (none of the sky under it, all of it in the open, a gradient of the rings' size at the mouth, the roof's underside dark and its top bright), the sun and the sky told apart (a floor in the sun under a roof's edge keeps the sun and loses the sky), a share of the sky that is what the atmosphere says (at 64, 128 and 200 of 255), a floor with nothing over it as bright with the cover counted at every distance to the end of the sky maps and beyond (no acne in any of the four, no seam), a wall of a hill and the walls, floor and roof of a tunnel, the same on a planet (a ball of a million voxels) and on one seen from the side (the way out 37 degrees from up: the floor beside a hill is 0.92 of the sky with the way out straight up and 0.38 with it over the hill), coarse cells (a node of cells of 4 voxels) and far ones (cells of 512 and of a million: the picture is exactly the one with no cover), the maps kept while the sun moves, a figure walks in and the camera moves a voxel, and drawn again for the camera's walk and the terrain's change, and no maps without cover counted, without a ground to say which way is up, without sky light and without shadows. Shell and Cave: the game's share reaches the picture (`sun_view_tests`, Cave's `daylight`).
- **Found by the tests and not by looking**, all of them mine: a pixel on a floor seen at a slant is one to three voxels long, so a probe a voxel from an edge catches another tap (three tests measured 1/13 and 12/13 of the sky and looked like bugs; they now aim a narrow camera at each probe from a high eye: see the memory note); the second coordinate of a tap is along world x when the way up is y (the basis's `right` is z); a camera in the sphere that holds a big node makes every sky map look as if it serves, as the sun's maps do (so the far scenes assert the picture and not an absence of maps).
- **Mutation experiments** (`PROPTEST_DISABLE_FAILURE_PERSISTENCE=1`, files restored from copies and compared by hash): 42 mutants of the shader, the plan, the pipelines, the zenith and the shell's wiring (the cover not applied, its share always none or all, one tap, a wrong divisor, the taps half as far, no push of a wall, the push along the whole normal or into the wall, no bias or the bias the wrong way, the sun's layers read, a sky map chosen by the sun's reach, the sky beyond the last map covered, the next of the last covered, no mix of a map with the next, the cover darkening the sun, the maps drawn from the sun, in the sun's layers, with the backs kept, with the fronts taken for the backs, holding things that move, counted with no sky light or always, the zenith straight up, the statistic counting the sun's maps, a wrong share, half the width not told, no slots for the maps, the reach not reduced, a tap the other way up, the push along the whole normal on the CPU, the zenith of flat ground and its way into the ball, and the shell's and Cave's shares). **The first run killed 34 and left 8**: two were gaps in my tests, which I closed (nothing probed the floor 5 to 7 voxels outside the mouth, where the outer ring is partly covered; and the band where the last map gives way to the open sky is less than a row of pixels of a 64-pixel picture, so a narrow view is aimed into it), and closing the second showed **the test was blind**: its sun, 255 and high, fills the screen blend (sun 0.97 and the sky 0.38 make 0.98, and half the sky makes 0.98 too), so a floor of the open-floor test was as bright whatever the sky did; it and the probes now use the sky alone. **Six survive, each looked at and none observable in a scene I can build**: the way up read as straight up in the shader (the same on flat ground, and on the tilted planet the push moves the place to open air either way; the premise of the matrix row is held by a CPU test), a sky map chosen by the sun's reach and no mix of one map with the next (a map that is clamped to its edge repeats the last column, and two maps agree to a texel on a clean edge), the pixel's distance taken from the vertices (it changes which map is looked in, and every map agrees on the scenes with big quads), a map that is not used given its uniform (nothing seen is in its range, by the definition of used), and the count of sky maps taken from the sun's (the shader's array reads are clamped, and the counts are four and four or five).
