# The sun's exact short ray (contact trace)

Status: **a design; phases 0 and 1 (the CPU side, section 10) and phase 2 (the renderer, section 11) are built** (2026-11-09); **the cost of phase 2 in real Cave on the integrated GPU is over the budget (section 11), a decision for the owner.** The spike that justifies it is `contact-shadow-spike.md` (read its tables first); the owner's wishes it serves are in `future-goals.md` ("Shaders", 2026-11-09): a default shader that is robust, fast and has **no gap at the base of a shadow**, 60 fps on the integrated GPU, and shaders that players can swap. Where this says "measured" it is the spike's; where it says "I believe" nothing was run.

## 1. The rule

A shadow map decides by comparing depths one texel wide, and the bias that keeps a thin floor from shading itself leaves a lit strip of one to two texels at the foot of every wall (the spike: 0.07 to 0.57 voxel by cascade, the same on all four adapters; and the same strip as a leak into closed caves). The strip is a **contact error**: it only matters where the occluder is within a few cells of the place. So:

> For a place on a face, walk the sun's ray through the voxels for up to `K` cells, starting in the cell in front of the face. If a cell that blocks the sun whole is entered, the place is in shadow. Otherwise the shadow map decides. `light = trace_clear ? map : 0`.

The start in the cell in front of the face means nothing is eased toward or away from anything: a wall beside the place is in the way or it is not, which is a fact about the lattice and not about floating point (spike, measured again after a bug in its first shader was fixed, `contact-shadow-spike.md`: zero lit pixels at a wall's foot at every sun from 5 to 88 degrees, on all four adapters, with 6 cells, along the middle and the edge rows of a block). What "no gaps" then means, as a test: **for every place on a face of a whole opaque cube, within `K` cells of the sun-ward occluder, the shader's light equals a CPU reference tracer's.** Beyond `K` cells the map's own softness (a texel, at a silhouette far from the place) remains, and is not a gap.

## 2. What the code already gives (verified by reading)

- The renderer never sees voxels: `MeshUpdate::Upload { coordinate, mesh }` (`engine/world_view/src/mesher.rs`) carries the mesh only. The client's `WorldMesher` meshes a brick on a worker thread **only when the brick and its six neighbours are present**; `GridMesher` and `ShipTerrainMesher` do the same for ships and for planets, under the grid's pose (planets are ships with a generator: `planet-spec.md`; the screenshots of 2026-11-09 are at y of about 63,700 voxels, over a pole, which is where a planet's surface is and not where a flat level's would be; I did not check Cave's world file).
- `MeshedBrick` already holds a `ModelId` for every voxel of the brick (`models`), and `BlockTable` answers `is_solid`, `is_opaque_cube`, `is_sight_through`, `is_light_through`, `transparency`. A shape is a **bitmask of 8 by 8 by 8 sub-cells** (`Shape::occupied`), so partial blocks fit an exact walk too.
- **A trap:** `mesh_brick` returns an empty mesh for a brick that is all air *and* for one that is solid and buried (the fast paths), and the renderer then holds nothing for either. The spike's volume had every voxel; the real one must say **Full** for a buried brick, or the ray walks through the rock of a cave and the leak is back. So occupancy is not a by-product of the mesh: it is sent for every brick that is meshed, empty ones included.
- A brick's mesh and its occupancy can leave the same job together, so they arrive in the same `apply_updates` and neither is ahead of the other.

## 3. The data

### 3.1 Per brick (CPU, `voxel_meshing`)

`BrickOccupancy`: `Empty` (all air or nothing that blocks), `Full` (every voxel blocks), or `Mixed(Box<[u32; 1024]>)` (one bit per voxel, x fastest, in words of 32: the layout the spike uses, 4 KB). The bit is set when the voxel's model **blocks the sun whole**: for phase 1, `is_opaque_cube`. Everything else is clear (phase 3 adds the rest, 5.3). It is computed in the same worker job as the mesh, from `models`, and costs one pass over 32,768 voxels that the mesher makes anyway. It is emitted for **every** meshed brick, so the renderer's table is as complete as the mesher's view. (As built it is a stream of its own and not new `MeshUpdate` variants: section 10.)

### 3.2 On the GPU, per grid

One **window** per grid that has bricks in view (the level's root grid; each planet; later each ship): a toroidal table of 64 by 64 by 64 `u32` entries (1 MB), indexed by the brick coordinate in the grid's own frame modulo 64 (the view radius is at most 16, which is 33 bricks across: a table of 32 would alias; as built, section 11). An entry says `UNKNOWN` (0: nothing known, which is clear), `EMPTY`, `FULL`, or a slot of the **pool**, a storage buffer of 4 KB slots (a free list on the CPU). When the window moves with the camera the CPU clears the entries that left it before reusing them (a stale entry would be a wrong shadow); a brick's pool slot is freed when its entry goes. Per window, per frame, a small uniform: the window's origin brick relative to the camera in world axes (as `occupancy_origin` in the spike), the grid's world-to-local rotation (a ship's `placement.rotation` transposed; the identity for the root grid), and the brick coordinate of the window's centre.

### 3.3 Memory (measured in phase 0, 2026-11-09)

A headless Cave on the RTX 3090 (`VOX_REPORT_OCCUPANCY=1`, below), the planet's bricks the mesher told of:

| Where | Empty | Full | Mixed | Pool (4 KB each) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| radius 12, the default start at the pole | 4,262 | 1 | 4,289 | 17.6 MB |
| radius 12, in a cave (the position of the owner's screenshot 3) | 3,787 | 1 | 4,764 | 19.5 MB |
| radius 16, the default start | 9,376 | 6 | 10,186 | 41.7 MB |

The terrain of the level's own root grid is **all empty** in Cave (8,552 empty bricks at radius 12): the ground is the planet, a ship (`GridId::FIRST_ALLOCATED`), so the windows that matter are planets'. **The ground below the surface is nearly all `Mixed`, not `Full`** (one brick in thousands is all rock), so the count grows with the cube of the radius (10,186 against 4,289 is 2.37, and 16 over 12 cubed is 2.37) and not with the area of the surface: caves and whatever else is not an opaque cube are everywhere under it. (I did not look at why: how dense the mixed bricks are, which says whether sub-bricks would shrink the pool, is not measured.) So the pool is **about 20 MB at the default radius and 42 MB at the largest**, one buffer well inside the 128 MiB `wgpu` allows by default; **sub-bricks are not needed for memory** (they may still speed the walk). Half the bricks are `Empty`, so the empty-brick jump (4.2) is used and a `Full` jump hardly is. Measured at the pole of one planet; the surface elsewhere has the same bricks turned.

## 4. The shader

### 4.1 Which volume, in which frame

The fragment knows its placement (the vertex stage has it), so the placement carries a **window number** as a flat varying; a face is traced in the window of **its own grid**. The ray is turned into the grid's frame (`to_local * sun_direction`, and the place relative to the window's origin turned too), so a planet that spins under the camera is walked in its own lattice. Grid-local positions near the window are a few hundred voxels from its origin, where `f32` is good to about 3e-5 of a voxel; **the exactness does not depend on that**, because the start cell comes from the face's normal and its own plane (`floor(place + normal / 2)`), not from the position alone.

### 4.2 The walk

A 3D DDA as in the spike, with a **second level** (not built: section 11): when the brick entered is `EMPTY` the walk jumps to where the ray leaves it (one step instead of up to 32); `FULL` is shadow; `UNKNOWN` ends the walk clear. A budget of steps and a longest distance bound the work, so the cost depends on how far the walk is allowed and not on the world. The ties of a ray along a lattice plane break in a fixed order (x, then y, then z), which the CPU reference repeats; tests keep probes off ties.

### 4.3 How far (`K`)

Enough to cover the map's strip at the place's cascade. The map's strip is `T * (cos(el) + 1.5 sin(el))` across (`T` the texel of the cascade that is looked up), and a steep ray climbs about `tan(el)` layers for each unit of it. Measured (fixed shader): with `T` from 1/16 to about 1/4 voxel, 1 cell settles every sun up to 60 degrees, 4 settle all but one case and 6 settle every sun tried (to 88 degrees, a wall 4 high). The rule to build is **`K` from the cascade index and the sun's elevation, bounded** (my first guess: the strip's length times `tan(el)` plus 3, between 3 and 24 cells); the test matrix of 6 fits it, the build does not trust the guess (the rule as built is in section 11). A place whose cascade is beyond what level 0 covers uses phase 5.

### 4.4 Not in the default shader

**The walk's binding must not be in the shader that is used while no volume exists.** The spike's fragment shader used one storage buffer for the walk, and two shadow tests (`a_block_and_a_roof_shade_the_floor_...` among them) began to fail on the integrated AMD GPU on DX12: 2 of 12 runs alone against 0 of 12 before the spike and 0 of 12 with the call taken out (`contact-shadow-spike.md`, "Why it is not in the tree"; `test-flakes.md`, which has the same story for the pictures of 2026-11-07). So phase 2 builds the walk as **a shader variant, built and chosen only while some window holds bricks** (as `pictures_on.wgsl` and `pictures_off.wgsl` are chosen), and its tests take a picture once two frames agree. The binding is then in the pipeline layout only for the variant that uses it.

### 4.5 Cost

Measured on the spike's flat 36 KB volume, 1280 by 720, about **0.08 to 0.09 ms per cell searched on the integrated GPU** and under 0.1 ms in all on the RTX: 6 cells are about 0.5 ms of a 16.7 ms frame. A real pool will not fit in cache, so this is a best case (I believe); the empty-brick jump should reduce the steps for long walks (I believe, unmeasured). Budget for the build: **at most 1 ms on the integrated GPU at 720p** in a scene with many contact edges, measured, or the step budget comes down. **Measured as built, in real Cave: over it (section 11).**

## 5. What is traced, and when

1. **Phase 1 (whole opaque cubes).** What the spike does, for the root grid and for planets.
2. **Ships and boxes as occluders (later).** Each ship near the camera has its own window; a face is traced against its own grid's window and then against the few nearest other grids (the ray turned into each pose; at most 4 to 8, by distance); an entity's box is an analytic ray-box test against a short list in the frame uniform. A ship's foot on a planet is a contact like any other.
3. **Partial blocks and cut-outs (phase 3).** A second bit plane marks voxels that are not whole opaque cubes (`complex`); a hit in one asks the block's **shape** (the 512-bit mask, a table indexed by `ModelId`, about 64 bytes a model) with a walk through the eighths of the voxel, and for leaves and glass the **picture's alpha at the entry point**, the rule the cut-out shadows already have (`shadow_cutout_fragment`). Until then these keep today's strip. (This is the part with most room for being wrong: the voxel's model id must reach the GPU for complex voxels, and a model's state or orientation changes its shape.)
4. **Coarse levels (phase 5).** The map's strip is about as wide as a cell of the level that is drawn there (texels follow pixels and cells follow pixels), so the same short walk in the **cells of the node's own level** (`CoarseCell::fill` thresholded the way the coarse mesh is drawn) removes it, with a constant `K`. Not designed further here.

## 6. Tests, in the order they are written (tests first, as the project works)

1. **`BrickOccupancy` (unit and property):** a bit is set iff the voxel's model is an opaque cube, for random bricks and tables; `Empty` and `Full` equal "every voxel is" (and the uniform fast paths agree); a buried brick has occupancy and no mesh; the occupancy of a brick is the same however the neighbours are.
2. **The reference tracer (CPU, `world_view::sun_blocked`, built in phase 1):** in exact integer cells with the tie order of the shader (x, then y, then z), checked against the slab method for each cell alone on random soups of voxels, with rays that have a component of exactly zero (the case the first spike's shader got wrong) and rays through a corner.
3. **Window and pool (CPU):** moving the window clears what left; a slot is freed once; unknown is clear; full is shadow; the toroidal index never aliases two bricks in the window.
4. **The shader against the reference, on every adapter (`VOX_REQUIRE_GPU=1`):** the spike's block scene and closed room become permanent tests with the whole matrix (suns 5 to 88 degrees, cascades by camera height, strip and holes, room leaks); then a **random scene test**: random voxel soups in a few bricks, probes at the centres and offsets of faces, aimed narrow cameras as `pixel_straight_through` does, every probe's light against the reference tracer, with a debug output that shows the trace's result alone. A planet scene: the same under a turned pose.
5. **No false shadows:** the existing "floor nothing stands on is as bright" test, and the 15 shadow tests, pass unchanged with the volume on.
6. **Mutation experiments** (`PROPTEST_DISABLE_FAILURE_PERSISTENCE=1`, one harness at a time): start in the voxel behind the face; the wrong axis at a tie; `K` one fewer; unknown solid; `Full` taken as clear; the empty-brick jump one brick short; the window not cleared on a move; the grid's turn not applied; the occupancy not sent for a buried brick; the mesh and occupancy not in the same update. All must be killed.

## 7. Phases (each ends with tests, clippy and fmt, as `CLAUDE.md` says)

- **0. Measure (done):** counters by kind of brick, printed by a screenshot run; the numbers are in 3.3. Decided: the pool is one buffer of 4 KB slots, sized by the radius (about 42 MB at 16), and the whole view radius is traced.
- **1. The CPU side (done):** 6.1, 6.2 and the occupancy stream from the three meshers; nothing on the GPU. Exit: tests pass, no picture changes (section 10).
- **2. The renderer (done: section 11):** windows, pool, per-grid uniform, the shader walk with the empty-brick jump, `K` by cascade, 6.3 to 6.6, **as a shader variant chosen only while a volume is in use (4.4)**; the spike's tests come back from `e166faf` as tests of the real walk (the spike itself is out of the tree). Exit: the matrix, the room and the planet scene at zero holes and zero leaks on four adapters; cost within the budget.
- **3. Partial blocks and cut-outs;** 4. **ships and boxes;** 5. **coarse levels;** each with its own tests and a measurement.
- **Optional: sub-bricks** of 8 by 8 by 8 (64 bytes) with an empty/full flag in a table, if phase 0 says the pool is too big (a mixed surface brick may need a third of its 4 KB; I believe, unmeasured). It also speeds the walk.

## 8. For swappable shaders

The engine owns the volume and the answer; a pack gets **`sun_visibility(place, normal) -> f32`** (map and trace together, as `light` above) and, later, a bounded **ray query** `trace(origin, direction, longest) -> hit`, and writes only how a surface is lit with them. A pack therefore cannot bring the strip back by choosing its own bias, which is what packs of other games do each for themselves. The volume would also give exact sky cover (replacing the heightfield of `shadow-spec.md` 8, whose approximations the spec lists) and cheap ambient occlusion to a pack, at no new data cost; neither is designed here. A pack's loops can still hang a GPU: the engine's queries are bounded, a pack's own are its author's (to be thought about with the pack format).

## 9. Open decisions (my recommendation first)

1. **Composition: `min(map, trace)`**, not the trace alone within range. The map has no false shadows by design (the bias is toward lit), so the minimum is exact where the trace reaches and the map's own where it does not.
2. **Radius of the volume: the whole view radius,** if phase 0 says the pool is affordable; else a smaller radius with the strip returning beyond it (one to two pixels, and the owner has said none).
3. **Unknown bricks are clear** (the map decides): the cost is a leak at the edge of the loaded view, where streaming is still arriving; the alternative, unknown as solid, would darken the edge of the world.
4. **Phase 1 includes planets,** since Cave's ground is one; a version for the root grid only would not show in Cave.
5. **Hard shadows only.** The owner has no preference; a few rays across the sun's disc would give a penumbra that is zero at contact, at several times the cost.

## 10. As built (phases 0 and 1, 2026-11-09)

- **`voxel_meshing::BrickOccupancy`** (`occupancy.rs`): `Empty`, `Full`, `Mixed(Box<[u32; 1024]>)` as 3.1 says, canonical (`Mixed` is never all zeros or all ones, so equal bricks are equal); `BrickOccupancy::of(&BrickChannels, &BlockTable)` and `blocks(voxel)`. The bit of voxel `i` (`LocalVoxelIndex::index`) is bit `i % 32` of word `i / 32`, which is the layout of the spike. Tests: seven (every class of uniform brick; one voxel sets exactly its bit in the word `z * 32 + y`; one voxel short of a wall is not `Full`; canonical form; independent of light and neighbours; **a buried brick has no mesh and is `Full`, a brick of sky has none and is `Empty`**; a property test of every bit against the table asked one voxel at a time, over random bricks with states).
- **A stream of its own, not `MeshUpdate` variants.** Every program that matches on `MeshUpdate` does so without a wildcard (about eighty places in tests and the shell), a brick with nothing to draw has nothing to show a `MeshUpdate::Upload`, and `WorldMesher::show` hands out one update. So each mesher has `enable_occupancy()` (before the first update; off costs nothing: the job does not compute it) and `take_occupancy_updates()`, which returns `(brick, OccupancyChange::Set(occupancy) | Clear)`: `WorldMesher` for bricks of a level, `ShipTerrainMesher` for a planet (the same, in the ship's coordinates; `with_occupancy()`), `GridMesher` for the stored bricks of ships (keyed by ship and brick), and `Session::enable_occupancy` / `take_occupancy_updates` / `occupancy_statistics` over all of them (the root grid is `GridId::root(level)`). A brick is told of **when it is meshed, whether or not it has anything to draw**, and again only when its occupancy changes (a 64-bit digest of what was sent, 2^-64 a pair of missing a change; meshing again to the same thing, for a neighbour's edit or a change of light, says nothing); it is cleared when it goes out of range, when the mesher is reset, or (a ship) when it turns out not to be there; a forgotten ship says nothing brick by brick. It does not follow what is shown: in the held-back mode of the levels of detail (`WorldMesher::show` and `hide`) the occupancy is told when the brick is meshed.
- **`world_view::sun_blocked`** (`sun_ray.rs`): the reference walk, in exact integer cells, ties broken x, then y, then z, a cell that is not known ends the walk clear, the start cell never tested. Tests: nine, among them the slab method for each cell alone as the oracle on random soups of voxels, with rays that have components of exactly zero and rays through a corner.
- **Tests of the meshers:** seven in `world_view/tests/occupancy.rs` (off by default; every meshed brick told of, the buried and the sky ones too, a mixed one against `is_opaque_at` for each of its 32,768 voxels; changed only when it changes, one voxel dug out changes one bit and tells of no neighbour; a brick edited to sky is `Empty` and one filled with stone `Full`; moving away clears and tells; reset clears in order; a planet tells as the terrain of a level does), three in `GridMesher`, four in the log.
- **Mutation experiments** (`PROPTEST_DISABLE_FAILURE_PERSISTENCE=1`, one at a time, the file restored from a copy of its bytes): 27 mutants of the occupancy, the log, the three meshers and the reference walk (a slab taken for a cube, the bit order, uniform `Full` and `Empty` swapped, `Mixed` not canonical, the wrong bit read, states ignored; the log sending every time, not forgetting a clear, unsorted, ignoring its switch; the mesher never clearing, keeping occupancy on reset, telling only bricks with a mesh, not asking for it; a ship forgotten and a brick missing; the walk breaking a tie y or z first, testing the start cell, one cell too few, a negative step wrong, the boundary from the far side, an unknown cell blocking, a zero component walking first). **Killed: 27 of 27**; the first run left one alive (a log that always said it was enabled, which no test could see because `set` has its own guard, and which would have had the meshers make the occupancy of every brick for nothing): a test of the switch itself kills it. (Two of the walk's mutants were skipped by the harness at first because rustfmt had reflowed the text it matches; run again, killed.)
- **The spike is out of the tree** (`contact-shadow-spike.md`, "Why it is not in the tree"): its binding in the default shader made a shadow test fail on the integrated GPU, so the renderer is as it was before it, and the spike is in `3f6ffb1` and `e166faf`.
- **A bug found on the way, in the spike's shader** (`contact-shadow-spike.md`, "Correction"): a sun direction with a component of exactly zero made the walk step sideways first. The CPU reference has the case as a test (a ray along an axis stays in its row), and **phase 2's shader must be tested with suns that have zero components**, and with places on the edge rows of a block, where the spike's scenes (everything extruded along z) could not show it.
- **For phase 2, learned here:** the root grid of Cave is empty, so the windows are planets'; faces of **coarse nodes must not be traced** with the fine volume (their cells are not the voxels the volume holds: a placement with a scale above 1 is not traced); the bricks of the keep shell beyond the view radius are not meshed and so not told of, so the walk meets `UNKNOWN` there and ends clear; the volume is told of bricks in the order they are meshed, nearest first.

## 11. As built (phase 2, 2026-11-09)

**What is in the tree.** The renderer holds the voxels that block the sun and a shader variant walks them; nothing else draws differently, and no brick that was never told of changes a picture.

- **`engine/renderer/src/occupancy_volume.rs`** (the books, on the CPU; tests in `occupancy_volume/tests.rs`): a **window** for each grid (the terrain of the level, a planet, later a ship), at most 4: a table of **64 by 64 by 64** `u32` entries (1 MB; not 32: a view radius of 16 is 33 bricks across and would alias) indexed by the brick's coordinates modulo 64, an entry being unknown (0), empty (1), full (2) or a slot of the pool plus 3. The pool is slots of 4 KB, handed out lowest first, given back, never more than the device's binding limit (128 MiB by default: 32,768 slots). A brick a whole window from one that stands at its place **pushes it out** (it is unknown, which is clear, and its slot is free), and clearing a brick that was pushed out does nothing to the one that took its place. Changes are collected (rows of a table that differ, in runs; the bits of the slots that were set) and handed over by `drain`. A window with nothing that blocks the sun in it is not traced; a grid that finds no window or no slot is counted in `VolumeStatistics::rejected` and drawn as the map alone.
- **`engine/renderer/src/contact_volume.rs`** (the graphics card's side): the tables of all windows in one buffer (4 MB), the pool (256 slots to begin with, doubling with a copy of what it holds), a uniform that says where each window is, and the pipelines of the shader variant, made when first wanted. **Made when the first brick that blocks the sun is told of**, so a renderer never told of one is exactly as before (4.4).
- **`contact_off.wgsl` / `contact_on.wgsl`**: the hooks of `voxel.wgsl`, concatenated as the pictures' are (`voxel_shader(device, pictures, contact)`): `contact_light(in, distance)` (0 or 1) and `contact_debug(in)`. The walk is in `contact_on.wgsl`, in the third bind group, which only the variant's pipeline layout has. `voxel.wgsl` itself gained `Placement::volume` (a placement is **80 bytes now, not 64**: the number of the window its faces are traced in, 0 for none: coarse nodes, avatars and boxes), one more flat varying, and two calls in `fragment_main`: the debug answer, and `terrain = terrain_light(...)`, times `contact_light` where that is not already 0.
- **Renderer API:** `set_occupancy` / `clear_occupancy` (the terrain), `set_grid_occupancy` / `clear_grid_occupancy` (a ship or planet; `remove_grid` takes its window), `occupancy_statistics`, `set_contact_debug(ContactDebug::Answer { cells })` (the walk's answer alone, white or black: the tool the tests of the walk use), and `RenderStatistics::traced_windows` (0 when the picture was drawn with the shader that has no walk).
- **The shell:** `view::apply_occupancy` after `apply_updates`, in the window, the headless screenshot and the replay; `Session` enables occupancy at the start. **Ships are not traced yet** (their updates are left out in `apply_occupancy`; phase 4). `VOX_WITHOUT_SUN_RAY=1` makes a screenshot or a benchmark run without the walk, to compare.

**Deviations from the design above, and why.**
1. **Window of 64, not 32** (above).
2. **No empty-brick jump.** The walk is at most 24 cells and keeps the table entry of the brick it is in; the measurement below says the cost is the arithmetic of each fragment's setup and not reads, so a jump would not help. Not built until something measured asks for it.
3. **The start cell** is the one the place is in, moved **a sixteenth of a voxel out of the face** (`floor(place + looks / 16 * axis)`), which is `floor(place + normal / 2)` for a whole face and is also right for a face at an eighth (a post, a fence): the walk starts in the voxel the face is in, not past it.
4. **`K`, the cells searched**, by the rule `ceil(strip * (1.5 + climb)) + 2`, at most 24, with `strip = T * (cosine + 1.5 * sine + 0.5)` for the texel `T` of the cascade the place is looked up in (the next cascade's where the two are mixed), `sine` the sine of the sun over the face's plane and `climb = sine / max(cosine, 0.05)`. Every term is held to account by a mutant (below): without the climb, with the finest texel everywhere, and without the two cells of slack, the matrix fails. 0 beyond the last cascade.
5. **Faces that look away from the sun are not walked** (`sine` at most 0.001), even where a planet's turned normal lights them: the walk from such a face would enter its own block at once. A test with a planet holds this.
6. **A range check:** a brick more than 32 bricks from the window's origin brick (the one the camera is in) is unknown, so a drawn brick that was never told of cannot read the entry of one a window nearer.
7. **The walk is skipped where the map already says shadow.**

**Tests.** `tests/gpu_rendering/contact_trace.rs`, 12 tests on every adapter and one measurement (`#[ignore]`): the floor is dark from the foot of a wall in a matrix of nine suns (5 to 88 degrees) by three heights of camera (three cascades) **with a control that the strip is there without the volume**, along the edge rows of the block, in a ship turned half way round, and in a closed room of rock (**with its control**); nothing is darkened that should not be (a floor nothing stands on; the volume never makes a pixel brighter); the walk against `world_view::sun_blocked` on random soups (12 suns, among them parts of exactly zero and diagonals, 1 to 8 cells; every kind of table entry and an unknown brick; the terrain, and grids turned by a half turn about y and x, a quarter turn, and a slanted axis); two grids in view at once; the pool growing with what it holds kept (400 bricks); a brick far from the camera not read as its alias; a planet's turned normal; the walk used only while a volume holds something and there are shadows. And 15 CPU tests of the books (one a property test against a plain map, with random sets, clears, grids taken whole and bricks a window apart).

**Mutation experiments** (`PROPTEST_DISABLE_FAILURE_PERSISTENCE=1`, the harness restoring files from a copy of their bytes): 36 mutants of the shader, the renderer and the books. **34 killed.** Two survive: **the order of an exact tie** (`x` before `y` before `z`, as the reference): a picture cannot tell, because the place of a pixel is never exactly on an edge of cells, so only the reference's own tests hold the order. Two more mutants were found to be **dead code and the code removed**: a snap of the place to an eighth of a voxel, and a least number of cells (the rule never gives fewer than 3). Two mutants **survived the first run and showed gaps in my tests**, and were killed once a test was added (a face that looks away from the sun on a planet; a window's count of solid bricks not lowered when a brick is cleared, which only the first of two ways of digging a brick out had tested).

**Flake check (the lesson of the spike).** The two shadow tests that failed 2 of 12 with the spike: **0 of 12 and 0 of 12 alone and 0 of 3 as the whole binary**, on the first shader and again on the final one (the default fragment shader gained a varying that it does not use, and a call that is a constant 1 there). The full gate passed on the final code (fmt, clippy, doc, all tests).

**The picture.** Cave at the pole, 09:00, 1280 by 720, with and without the walk (`VOX_WITHOUT_SUN_RAY=1`): 21,935 of 921,600 pixels differ (2.4 percent), **none brighter**, none on the open grass; the difference is the shadows of the blocks and trees growing out of their feet. (The owner's picture: the shadows no longer float off their blocks.)

**The cost, measured, and it is over the budget of 4.5.** On the integrated AMD GPU at 1280 by 720 (medians, Vulkan / DX12):
- The synthetic scene (a floor with pillars, every floor pixel walks): **0.78 / 0.77 ms without the walk, 1.64 / 2.00 ms with it**: +0.9 / +1.2 ms. On the RTX 3090 +0.03 / +0.01 ms.
- **Real Cave** (`cave --adapter integrated --benchmark --time 09:00 --pitch -35`, still, the dev profile, view radius 12): **19.10 ms (52 fps) without, 22.93 ms (44 fps) with: +3.8 ms, 20 percent.** Where it goes (each by a temporary edit, then undone): the setup arithmetic of each fragment is about +1.9 ms (a walk that returns after it, with no reads at all, is 21.0 ms; one that returns at its first line is 19.4, so it is the work done and not the registers the code takes), the cells about +1.9 ms (about 0.4 ms a cell); a walk only within 128 voxels is 21.08 ms, so **about half the cost is the far terrain**. Tried: reading the table entry once for each brick crossed and skipping the walk where the map already says shadow (both kept; each about 0.05 ms, **measured in the synthetic scene only**); the sun's direction in the grid's axes and its reciprocals computed once a frame (0.03 ms in the synthetic scene, **not measured in Cave**, taken out for simplicity); four cells at a time so that the reads overlap (**worse** in Cave, 24.5 ms: it always does the whole chunk); a constant index of the window (no change in Cave); passing the distance that `fragment_main` already has (no change in Cave). **Corrected 2026-11-09, measured:** real Cave shades 1.61 fragments for each pixel (an occlusion query: 1,486,525 pass the depth test for 921,600 pixels, the bricks drawn nearest first), not several times as many as I believed here, so overshading is at most 38 percent of the fragments' work, and a pre-pass does not pay (below).

**Not done, and the owner's to decide.** Options for the cost: (a) accept it; (b) a player setting like `.shadows` (the walk off for a slow GPU; a small change; **the owner, 2026-11-09: hold off on the command**); (c) walk only within some distance (loses the fix beyond it: the owner said no gaps); (d) a depth pre-pass, so that each pixel is shaded once; (e) cheaper tests of whether a fragment can be near an occluder at all; (f) the owner's second idea, to paint the contact into the shadow map; (g) found while measuring (d): draw only the faces of a brick that can look at the camera. Not built: partial blocks and cut-outs (phase 3), ships and boxes (4), the far levels (5), the pack interface, an empty-brick jump, sub-bricks.

**The options measured (2026-11-09; temporary edits, undone; the same view and adapter as the cost above; the build log has the numbers in full).**
- **(d) is a loss.** A pass of its own with no colour (the picture's vertex shaders, no fragment stage), then the picture compared with `GreaterEqual` and writing no depth: each pixel is shaded once (921,604 fragments for 921,600 pixels), and the frame is 30.08 ms without the walk (19.22 before) and 32.19 with it (23.04): **+10.9 and +9.2 ms.** The walk's own cost falls from 3.8 to 2.1 ms. It buys back 1.7 ms and costs five times that: the frame is bound by the vertices and set-up of 2.8 million quads, not by shading.
- **(f) costs nothing per frame and is partial.** The bottom edge of each back face pushed toward the sun by 2 texels of its cascade in the map's vertex shader (a wedge up the quad, no new draw): 19.11 ms against 19.18. It narrows the strip where the cascade is fine and the sun is under about 55 degrees (0.07 to 0.11 voxel to 0.004 to 0.03), does nothing at 75 and 85 degrees, is uneven in coarser cascades, and stops at the map's own filter, a texel across. More than 2 texels falsely shadows the sun's side of a thin wall (4 texels: 0.45 of the floor in front of a one-voxel wall lit, at 400 voxels with the sun at 15 degrees). It needs the foot of every quad known (whole and detailed quads, a planet's up, ships), and it can never put a shadow where the gap is narrower than a texel. The numbers are of one scene, from a throwaway edit, on the RTX 3090.
- **(g) pays for the walk and more.** The picture draws all six faces of every brick, though no camera sees more than three of a brick it is outside of; the rasteriser throws the others away after shading their six vertices. The quads of a brick are already in the order of their faces (the shadow maps draw ranges of them), and a face can look at the camera from somewhere in the brick only if the camera is on its side of the brick's far end. Drawing only those ranges: **23.05 to 17.19 ms with the walk (43 to 58 fps), 19.22 to 13.40 without it; at pitch 0 17.21 to 13.64; at pitch -80 16.91 to 11.20; the picture is the same pixel for pixel** (0 of 921,600 differ, at pitch -35 and at 0). Up to three draws of a brick in place of one. Integrated GPU only.

**My opinion.** Keep the walk (it is exact; painting cannot be) and build (g) first: with it the frame with the walk (17.19 ms) is shorter than today's frame without the walk (19.22). Confidence: high in (d) and (g) (measured in three views, the pictures compared pixel for pixel in two, on one adapter), medium in (f) (one scene, one adapter), and I have not measured (g) on a discrete GPU.
