# Viewpoints: the camera as something that asks for terrain (a draft, 2026-11-21)

Status: **a draft; the owner's first answers are in (section 8, 2026-11-21) and the interview goes on; nothing is built.** It extends `lod-spec.md` (which decides what one viewer draws) to several viewers at once: the ship you fly, a map, a free camera, a preview of an
orbit. Read with `far-nodes-spec.md` (far ships and the server's nodes), `planet-spec.md`, `protocol-and-replay.md` and `design-decisions.md`. Marks as in `lod-spec.md`: **[code]** is what I read in the code,
**[run]** what a program measured (the programs are in `history/spikes/viewpoints/`, written against the code of commit `aa419ee`, and are not kept compiling), **[belief]** what I did not check. The owner's decisions are in section 8; every other choice here is mine and marked as a recommendation until the owner has answered what is open there.

## 1. What the owner asked for (2026-11-21)

1. Switch back and forth between the up-close ship view and a zoomed-out map view **without regenerating, re-downloading or re-meshing any node that stays the same size between the two**.
2. Switching is **instantaneous**: no moment where the up-close terrain is too low in detail, at least not after the first time.
3. **No noticeable frame-rate cost** for keeping more high-detail nodes in memory.
4. Review the idea of keeping several detail levels of the same terrain in memory and drawing only the ones the active view needs; investigate alternatives (sections 4 and 5).
5. Leave room for what comes next: maps from extremely far away, **a free camera with no ship** (creative, spectator), and **a camera that zooms ahead along an orbit** to show a trajectory in first person without the ship.
6. Then interview the owner to close the gaps (section 8).

## 2. What is true today

- **[code] A view keeps nothing but its current cut.** `LodView` works out the cubes to draw from one viewer (`coarse_cells::select_around_brick`), and a node's record exists only while the node is wanted or shown: `CubeStates::want` drops every
  record that is neither (`cube_states.rs`), `hide_node` drops the picture of a node that goes, and `WorldMesher::hide` drops a brick's picture ("the brick has none until it is made again", `lod-spec.md` 3.6a). Only the yes/no answers of
  `may_show` outlast a cut, for two rounds (`MayShowMemo`). So leaving a view throws all it built away, and coming back makes it again.
- **[code] A picture goes on the screen at most 64 to an update** (`SHOWN_PER_UPDATE`, `lod.rs`), each a new GPU buffer (`VoxelRenderer::gpu_brick`: one `wgpu::Buffer` for the whole-voxel quads and one for the detailed ones). 12,000 nodes need about 190 updates: 3.1 s at 60 frames a second even when every picture is ready. **[run, 2026-11-21] The cap is not what limits a cold build** (section 9a): with caps of 64, 128 and 256 a view from 264 km back to the surface took 3.05, 2.96 and 2.94 s, and counting only the pictures that have something in them instead of every cube changed nothing; it stays at 64. It will matter when a switch shows pictures that are already held (a move, not an upload).
- **[code, run in phase 0] A node is replaced only when everything that replaces it is ready.** `plan_swaps` makes a group of a cube and the cubes of the other set it overlaps, and a group changes only when every cube that comes in is ready. After a big move (a teleport, a switch, a fast flight) the old picture is a few huge nodes, each holding thousands of the new cubes, so
  **the old picture stays until the whole new cut under it is built**: from 264 km up back to the surface the view wanted 15,147 cubes, showed the 24 old nodes for 3.8 s (a few seconds, 2.5 s at least, with nothing changing), and then 12,449 meshes came in a few bursts. This is not coarse first. It is what makes the owner's 0.25 s unreachable by speed alone, and it was a change of the planner (6.2, item 11, **built 2026-11-21**: from 264 km up back to the surface the picture is of nodes of level 7 and finer from 0.26 s on, and the finest level on the screen goes down a level at a time; the build is still 3.6 s and costs 23% more uploads).
- **[code] The renderer draws everything it holds and does not choose** (`upload_coarse_node` says so). Every frame `VoxelRenderer::gather` walks `bricks`, `coarse_nodes`, `ship_bricks` and `ship_coarse_nodes`, makes a `Drawable` of each (an exact offset from the camera
  in 128-bit integers), and the culling and the shadow planning loop over all of them. Every upload and removal also counts up `terrain_version`, which the shadow maps watch. **Changed in phase 1 (2026-11-21):** the four maps are `MeshStore`s with a list that is drawn and a list that is hidden (held on the graphics card and nothing else); `gather` walks the drawn list only, and `terrain_version` counts a change of the draw list only. The text above is what the renderer was.
- **[code] One `LodView` for each grid, all driven by one camera.** The level's root terrain is a view; each planet is a `ShipTerrainMesher` with a `LodView` and its own worker threads (`Session::planets`); each far ship has one with no fine ball. Each takes the
  camera's position in its grid's axes (`Session::update_view`, `ship_updates`).
- **[code] One region of interest, one level.** The client sends `SetInterest { level, region }`, a box round the camera's brick, again only when the camera's brick changes (`Session::update_view`). The server sends the edited bricks inside it. On a new region **the client forgets
  every edited brick outside it, and of every other level** (`client_world.rs`, `InterestChanged`), and a brick outside the region is an error (`BrickOutsideWatchedRegion`): a second region is not possible today. So going to a map and back **does re-download** the edited bricks of the ship's region and its
  generator assignments.
- **[code] What the server is asked about coarse nodes is asked once and answered for one viewer.** `SetDetail` carries the pixel size and the rate; the server picks the cubes with the same function as the client, from the **player's** pose (`coarse_streaming::server::plan`, `presence.pose_of`), not from the client's camera. Edits the server sent are kept by the session (`coarse_overrides`) and given to any view made later, so those are never
  downloaded twice; the server's own record of what it sent stays too.
- **[code] Per-brick GPU tables are local to the camera.** The occupancy volume (`set_occupancy`) holds a window of 64 bricks round the camera, "bricks told of must be near each other: a brick 64 bricks from another one in every direction shares its place in the tables, and the one told of last is kept".
- **[code] The fine ball is never pruned.** `select_around_brick` keeps every brick of the fine region whatever `may_hold` says (a player builds in the air), so any viewer, in empty space too, has 8,552 brick cubes at the default radius of 12 bricks.

## 4. The idea reviewed: several detail levels resident, one drawn

**What it is.** Keep the pictures of every cut that matters (the ship's, the map's) resident, and draw only the cut of the active view. The pictures for a cube do not depend on the viewer (a node's mesh is made from its cells with no neighbours, `mesh_coarse_node(&node, &[None; 6])`; a brick's from its voxels and its six neighbours; **[belief]**
nothing in a picture depends on the camera: textures near the viewer, `lod-spec.md` decision 6, are not built, and if they are chosen when the picture is made they would split the pictures by viewer), so one store of pictures keyed `(grid, cube)` can serve any number of views.

**Verdict: yes, with one correction to the shape.** The data say the idea is cheaper than it sounds and a literal reading of it is not enough:

1. **It is cheap in memory.** Keeping the ship's cut costs at most 23 MB (about 8 MB high up). A map's own cut is up to about 21 MB (at 1 km, mostly the ship's nodes) and under 3 MB beyond 100 km; what a map adds to the ship's cut is the map-only column of section 3: 2,000 to 2,700 nodes, about 4 to 10 MB. The union of the ship's cut and any map is about 32 MB at the most at Cave's planet, shared nodes counted once. Memory is not what decides.
2. **It is not free in the frame as the renderer is built**: 2 ms at 20,000 meshes, because the renderer walks what it holds. Resident must be **separate from drawn**: a store that nothing walks every frame, and a draw list that is only the active cut. That split is the change that makes the idea work, and nothing else in this document is as valuable.
3. **It is not instant unless a switch is O(1) per picture.** A switch that creates buffers is capped at 64 a frame and costs 12 to 22 microseconds each: 3 s for the ship's cut. A switch has to flip the draw list (a key moving between two maps, or one pointer), with the GPU buffers already made.
4. **"Several levels of the same area" is not the shape of the saving.** A pyramid with every level over an area holds more than two cuts; the two cuts share only the far field, and the map's near-ship nodes are ancestors that no cut of the ship's holds (section 3). What is kept is not a pyramid but **the union of the cuts of the viewpoints that are kept warm**.
5. **Keeping a cut warm is not keeping it still.** A ship that flies while you look at a map moves on: the cut you will return to is the cut at the ship's place **then**. So a warm viewpoint has to be tracked (its cut worked out again as the ship moves, built in the background), at the cost the active view pays now in worker time and none in frame time. At orbital speeds in empty space the cut is nearly nothing (air is not asked for, `ship-terrain-cost.md`); near a planet's face it is 570 nodes a second at 100 m/s, 5,749 at 1 km/s and 15,468 at 7.8 km/s (section 3: the build rate is 3,400, so a warm cut keeps up to about 600 m/s near a surface and at any speed from 20 km up). The order of building (coarsest first) makes the worst case a coarse picture on return and never a hole.
6. **The server has to keep a warm viewpoint current too**: the edits near the ship while you look at the map, and the coarse nodes the server sends. Today the server knows one region and one viewer for a client (section 2), so this is a protocol change, not only a client change.
7. **Things that are camera-local must be told when a switch makes a cut the drawn one**: the occupancy window of 64 bricks, the contact-trace windows, the shadow maps (they are drawn for the active camera anyway, and `terrain_version` must not count up for a picture that goes into the store without being drawn). The cost of filling the occupancy of the bricks a switch shows was not measured.

## 5. Alternatives

| | what it is | instant after the first time? | frame cost | memory | work | verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | today: a view keeps its cut only | no: 3 s to 0.05 s by size | of what is drawn | the cut | none | the baseline |
| **1** | **warm viewpoints: one store of pictures shared by all views, pinned by the views that want them, resident apart from drawn** | **yes** | **of what is drawn** | **the union of the cuts** | **large (renderer, view, protocol)** | **recommended** |
| 2 | a resident pyramid: every level over an area round the ship, any cut chosen from it | yes, inside the area | of what is drawn | more than the union | medium on top of 1 | no as the base; as a **prefetch rule** for a zoom (section 6.5) yes |
| 3 | an LRU of pictures behind today's view (dropped nodes go to a cache under a byte budget) | usually; not guaranteed | of what is drawn if the cache is outside the draw maps | the budget | small | a stepping stone: it is 1 without pins, tracking, or the server |
| 4 | make a cold build fast (more threads, meshing on the GPU, one arena buffer for uploads) | no: 12,000 nodes in 100 ms needs 35 times today's rate | none | none | large | not an alternative; **the arena is part of 1** (12,000 buffers is a bad shape) |
| 5 | one continuous dolly with the cut built ahead along the path | smooth, not a cut | of what is drawn | small | small | the right way to **zoom** a map; not a switch |
| 6 | a baked map layer (a cube map or tiles of the planet, drawn instead of the voxels) | yes | tiny | tiny | large: a second picture of everything | no: it breaks "LOD works on ships as on terrain" (`lod-spec.md` decision 1) and edits need baking; **[belief]** the one possible use is beyond the largest node (stars, galaxies) |
| 7 | GPU-driven selection over a resident arena (a compute pass picks the cut of each view each frame) | yes, for any number of views | about none | the resident set | very large | the end state; 1 with the arena is its first half; decision 15 of `lod-spec.md` asks to wait for a measured limit, and **0.1 ms a thousand is that measurement** once resident sets pass 20,000 |

**What is kept** (choices inside 1): (a) CPU mesh and GPU buffer both, instant and the most memory; (b) CPU mesh only, uploaded into one arena buffer on a switch, a bulk copy that I **believe** is a few milliseconds for 23 MB and did not measure, half the memory; (c) the node's cells, re-meshed on a switch: about 1.3 ms for a mixed node, so no;
(d) nothing: today. Recommendation: (a) for the active and warm cuts, (b) for what an LRU keeps beyond them.

## 6. The design I recommend (a draft)

### 6.1 Terms

- **Viewpoint**: what asks for terrain. A place (a level and a position), an orientation, a **projection**, detail settings (`cell_pixels`, the pixel size, the farthest distance, an optional zoom cone), a **fine radius** (the ball of bricks), a **time offset** (0 now; for an orbit preview, ahead), and a **role**:
  *body* (the player's eyes: a fine ball, bricks and edits, follows the body or ship it is aboard); *free* (no body, so the fine ball is as small as the altitude needs and pruned by what may show, since nobody builds from it), with two ways of being steered: **orbit**, locked on a *focus* (the map, and the
  third-person camera at a short distance), and **fly**, the spectator's camera, which looks where the player points; *predicted* (a free viewpoint at another time).
- **Focus**: the thing an orbit camera is locked on: a ship, a planet, a star (decision 7). The viewpoint's place is the focus's place plus a distance along two angles, and it follows the focus, so a camera locked on a planet keeps its place in the planet's own axes and the planet's cut
  does not change while the planet moves on its rail, and a camera locked on a ship moves over the planet as the ship does.
- **Projection** (owner, 2026-11-21): *perspective* everywhere; in the levels with planets in them (`mini_cosmos` and the levels to come) perspective only; in the overworld *orthographic* is available and is the default.
- **Active** viewpoints are drawn this frame (one per window; a minimap would be a second). **Warm** ones are not drawn and are kept in one of two ways, and there are at most three warm and the first design uses two: *tracked* (the cut is worked out again as the viewpoint moves and building goes on at once, as for the active one) and
  *lazy* (worked out again about once a second, coarsest first, with a share of the workers). The body's view is **tracked whenever it is not the active one** and the body exists ("quite warm", owner). The map's is tracked for a window after it was last active, lazy for a longer one, then released (my defaults: 600 client ticks, 30 s at
  20 ticks a second, then 12,000, 10 minutes; open question e), which is what makes switching back and forth fast and lets the map take a second or two to catch up after the ship has moved a lot.
- **Picture store**: the pictures (a CPU mesh, a GPU buffer, a version) keyed `(grid, cube)`, with the viewpoints that want each; unwanted ones go to an LRU under a byte budget; a picture whose data version changed is made again when it is next wanted, not before. **Draw list**: the cubes of the active viewpoints' cuts, the only thing
  the renderer walks. Building goes by priority: the active viewpoint's, then a tracked one's, then a lazy one's, each coarsest first.

### 6.2 What must hold (each is a test, apart from 9)

1. A picture is the same bytes whichever viewpoint asked for it (made through two viewpoints, compared).
2. **A frame costs what is drawn, not what is held**: counted visits of `gather` equal the draw list (a counter, not a clock), with 0 and with 100,000 pictures in the store. *(Built, phase 1: `tests/gpu_rendering/resident.rs`, `a_frame_visits_what_is_drawn_and_not_what_is_held`, with 8,000 meshes of the four kinds hidden and then shown, on every adapter; 100,000 was not tried.)*
3. **The second switch does no work**: from the ship's view to a map and back, counted generator calls, mesh jobs and buffer creations are all 0, and the draw list changes in one frame. *(Built for the nodes of one view, phase 2a, 9c: on the ground and 400 bricks up and back, 645 nodes were made of the 9,056 the ground took, 92 pictures uploaded and 6,696 shown from hold; bricks and a second view are not held yet, so the counts are not 0.)* The first switch builds only the cubes the other view has not (section 3's "map-only").
4. No hole and no double: at every frame the draw list tiles the active cut (the swap planner's property, `plan_swaps`, now across a switch), and a cut still being built shows the coarser picture it replaces and never nothing.
5. The cubes of a viewpoint are the same on both ends of the connection: client and server run the selection from the same inputs, **per viewpoint** (built in 4b: the player's and each place's, 9k).
6. Memory: the store is within its budget plus what is pinned or drawn; an eviction never takes a drawn or pinned picture; the order of eviction does not depend on a clock.
7. **No wall clock** (the hard rule on client cosmetics): the windows of warmth, the LRU and the order of building are counted in client ticks or frames, never in seconds.
8. Replay: a recording plus the viewpoints the client had draws what the live client drew (viewpoints are recorded as client input, section 6.3).
9. **The owner's times (2026-11-21)**, as targets the probes measure in seconds and the tests check as counted work: switching to the **ship's view**: a coarse picture with no hole in the first frame and the draw list equal to the wanted cut **within 0.25 s** (about 15 frames); to the **map**: coarse first, the whole of it **within 4 s at worst**
   (the first time, or after the ship has moved a lot), and **within 0.25 s when switching back and forth** (inside the tracked window). A wall clock may not decide a test, so a test counts jobs, flips and updates and waits for a condition with a deadline (`tick_until`).
10. **A viewpoint changes no state of the server's world**: with a free viewpoint watching anywhere for any number of ticks, the world's saved bytes, the generator assignments, which ships are awake or parked and the contents of the generated-brick cache that simulation uses are the same as with no such viewpoint (section 6.3).
11. **A refinement goes through intermediate cuts** (found in phase 0; **built 2026-11-21**, `coarse_cells::plan_progressive_swaps`, the tests are in `coarse_cells/tests/progressive_swaps.rs` and `world_view/tests/lod.rs`): when the wanted cut is far finer than what is shown, the view builds and shows it **level by level**, each step a cut that tiles the area (the wanted cut with every cube above a level L replaced by its ancestor), L going down, so that what is on the screen is never more than one level coarser than what has been built. The extra cost is the ancestors, about a third more nodes of a surface (a quarter, then a sixteenth...), and many of them are already in the store (the map's). Test: a view jumped from a far cut to a near one has, after every update, a picture that tiles the cut and is finer than the last.

### 6.3 Network, and what "never affects the simulation" asks of the server

`SetInterest` and `SetDetail` become **one message that holds a list of viewpoints** (protocol 13; the owner allows breaks before the first release, `CLAUDE.md`): for each an id, a level, a centre brick, a radius for edited bricks, the projection and detail settings, a priority. The server watches the union of the regions, runs the coarse
plan once per viewpoint from **the viewpoint** and not the player's pose, sends coarsest first within one byte credit shared by all of a client's viewpoints (the active one first), and refuses a list whose regions together pass a cap (`maximum_watched_bricks` today for one). The client keeps the edited bricks of every region in the list and forgets those in
none. Far-ship tiering already works from the region and keeps working from the union.

**A viewpoint with no body is read-only on the server (owner: "never affect simulation").** What I found that it would touch today **[code]**:
- *The saved world.* A watch is answered by `Server::assignments_covering`, which **assigns** the level's current default generator to every cube round the region that has none (`assign_unassigned`, `server.rs`), and the assignments are saved in the world file. A body that watches does that today by design ("assigned at the first need"). A free camera that flies must not: it would write
  the places it looked at into the world. For a free viewpoint the server answers with the default for an unassigned cube and writes nothing. Whether the client then keeps those answers is its own memory (the client's tree grew by about 43,000 nodes for each megametre flown, 400 MB at 444 Mm, measured 2026-11-20; the client can forget default answers outside every region).
- *The cache simulation uses.* `generated_brick` and the cache of 256 generated bricks are shared with the colliders and the walkers; a camera that asks for bricks and hints all over a level would push out what the tick needs. A free viewpoint's server work (coarse plans, overrides) is on its own workers and its own bounded cache.
- *Which ships are awake.* Awake, frozen and parked follow the players' bodies, not their cameras (`grid_lifecycle`): a free camera wakes nothing, brings no ship in from its file, and so sees a ship that is **frozen in memory** as it is (near, with its bricks, if it meets the region) and a ship that is **parked in a file** only as a far ship, from the nodes of its sidecar (`far-nodes-spec.md`),
  at any distance: **not up close** (decision 9).
- *Everything else* a viewpoint causes is replication (messages to that client): edited bricks, nodes, far ships. Those change nothing in the world.

### 6.4 What belongs to the camera and what to the player

*Camera (viewpoint)*: the region and detail asked for; the coarse-node plan; the far-ship tier; the zoom cone; the cut and what is drawn; the projection; the atmosphere, fog distance and sky (set for the active camera: the fog radius is the viewpoint's farthest distance; none in an orthographic map); the sun and the sky's brightness as seen
from it (a time offset moves them); the up direction and frame; the picking ray for *looking* (the ray for *placing* a block is the body's); the listener, when there is sound.
*Player or ship (not the camera)*: collision, the prefetch of bricks for a body, simulation (which ships are awake, section 6.3), edits and reach, which entity the client controls (`set_client_focus`), the saved place.

### 6.5 The uses

- **The map** is an orbit viewpoint locked on a focus. Perspective in a level with planets: the rule of `lod-spec.md` 3.3 as built, with a fine radius of 0 (the ball of 8,552 bricks that every viewer has today is pure waste for a camera nowhere near ground), and the cuts of section 3: nearly free to keep beyond 30 km. A zoom is a dolly: the cuts on the way are built ahead, coarsest first.
  **Orthographic (the overworld's default) needs a rule of its own** **[belief, not built, not run]**: in an orthographic view a pixel is the same size in voxels at every distance, so "fine enough" cannot depend on the distance from the viewer, and the cut is the cubes of **one level**, `2^n` voxels across being about `cell_pixels` pixels, over the box the picture sees and that may hold something. A picture of
  1920 by 1080 pixels at 3 pixels a cell is 20 by 11 nodes across the ground: **a few hundred nodes at any zoom**, against 15,000 for the perspective view near the ground. Two consequences: a zoom changes the level of the whole picture at once (a pop, not the local, gradual change of a perspective cut), so the next level is built ahead while zooming and the swap is one group (or faded); and the cost per
  node of the overworld's generator decides whether the 4 s hold (section 6.6, A).
- **The free (fly) camera.** A free viewpoint steered by the player, with no focus and no body, anywhere in any level (decision 8): the plan from the viewpoint (6.3), the server's read-only rule, and the pruned fine ball. It is the worst case for the assignment tree of the client and, unfixed, of the server (6.3).
- **Ahead along an orbit.** A *predicted* viewpoint: a time offset, and the ghost of the ship (or none, open question d). It needs the client to place every grid at the other time, which needs the rails of the bodies on the client (`wide_kepler::predicted_path` exists on the server; **[belief]** the client has no rails and only the poses the server sends). Out of scope for the first phases; the
  viewpoint carries the offset from the first.

## 9a. What limits a cold build: the job feed, not the cap (2026-11-21)

The owner asked for the cap of 64 pictures an update to be raised. It was measured first, **[run]** in a release build with the probe's updates paced at 60 a second (`PROBE_FRAME_MS`; the first probe called `update_view` as fast as it could, which is not what a game does), a view from 264 km up back to the surface, on the client's 11 worker threads:

| the cap (counted in cubes added, as built / in pictures uploaded) | 64 / - | - / 64 | - / 256 | - / 1,024 | none |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| seconds until the last change | 7.49 | 7.42 | 7.41 | 7.41 | 7.58 |

The cap was reached in 132 of 944 updates (counted in cubes) and 79 (in pictures), and removing it changed nothing. **What took 7.4 s, where the unpaced probe had taken 3.6, was how many nodes the threads were handed:** `LodView::start_jobs` hands out work only when the view is updated, and held 4 nodes a thread, so 11 threads were given 44 nodes a frame: 2,600 a second at 60 frames a second where they can do about 5,000. At 4 / 8 / 16 / 32 / 128 nodes a thread: 7.54 / 4.16 / 3.20 / 3.25 / 3.21 s.

**Built: `job_feed::jobs_to_hold`**, not a bigger constant. A count of nodes is the wrong unit when a node's cost varies by a factor of 200 (a node of the planet about 1 ms, of hills 18 to 43 ms, of hills with caves 94 to 201 ms): 32 queued for each thread is seconds of work for the slow ones, which cannot be taken back or put in another order when the view moves. The rule holds twice what the threads finished since the last update, and one a thread, between 4 and 64 a thread: it doubles while the threads run dry and stays at the old 4 for slow nodes, by counting alone (no clock). **Result: 3.10 s at 60 frames a second, 3.12 s at 30, 3.06 s unpaced** (7.4, -, 3.6 before), uploads 13,362 where there were 15,271 and removals 1,141 where there were 3,050 (a build that goes faster shows fewer pictures on the way). With the cap at 64 / 128 / 256 on the new rule: 3.05 / 2.96 / 2.94 s: **the cap stays at 64** (a hitch of 1.4 ms in the worst frame, 12 to 22 us a picture) until a switch shows pictures that are held already, which are moves and not uploads.

**Corrections to what this document and the build log said before.** (1) The probe's client has **11 worker threads** (`Session::new`: the cores less one), not three as I wrote in sections 3 and 6.6. (2) The "3.4 thousand nodes a second" was **the job feed's rate** (about 44 nodes an update at the probe's update rate) and not the threads' own: it is about 4.9 thousand with 11 threads, which moves the speed to which a ship's cut can be kept complete from 600 to about 850 m/s (6.6 C), and makes the overworld's map (6.6 A) borderline on this machine and missed on one with few cores. (3) What the 3.1 s is bound by is answered in 9b.

## 9b. What bounds the 3.1 s: the CPU, not the code around it (2026-11-21)

**[run]** with counters in the node threads (`docs/history/spikes/viewpoints/probe_job_bound.rs` and `job_bound_counters.patch`), release, updates paced at 60 a second, the view from 264 km up back to the surface, on a machine with 6 cores and 12 logical processors:

| node threads | seconds | speed-up over one | a node costs (generate + mesh) | threads busy |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 19.7 | 1.0 | 1.07 ms (0.69 + 0.38) | 1.0 |
| 3 | 6.9 | 2.9 | 1.12 ms | 2.9 |
| 6 | 4.3 | 4.6 | 1.29 ms | 5.5 |
| 11 (the client's) | 3.25 | 6.1 | 1.81 ms (1.14 + 0.67) | 10.0 |

- **The threads are the limit, and they are not short of work.** With 11 they waited for a job for 0.04 s in the whole build, and were busy 10.0 threads of 11: 32 s of processor time in 3.2 s. The job feed, the cap and the one lock the threads take jobs from are not what bounds it (the lock's wait is that 0.04 s). The main thread is not either: `update_view` took 1.7 s of the 5.8 s of the run, a mean of 5.2 ms a call at 16.7 ms a frame, and the threads never ran dry while it did.
- **Why 11 threads are 6 times one and not 11:** the cost of one node rises with the number of threads, in generating and meshing alike (1.65 times for generating and 1.76 for meshing, from 1 thread to 11), which is what a machine with 6 cores whose second hardware thread adds a part of a core, and a clock that falls when all cores are busy, would do. **This is read from the curve, not shown:** a cost that rises evenly in two different phases is not a lock's or the allocator's signature, which would show in the phase that allocates most, but no test here told processor sharing from memory bandwidth or the allocator.
- **What the nodes are:** 18,112 were made on the way back, of which 12,449 are the cut at the surface (69 percent), 2,841 came out empty (16 percent, asked and found to have nothing), and 2,822 were steps on the way (16 percent: the progressive swap of 3716f27 makes the nodes between a giant and what replaces it, and these were drawn and replaced). By level, made / empty: 1: 4,090 / 906; 2: 3,948 / 664; 3: 3,316 / 531; 4: 2,824 / 358; 5: 2,348 / 219; 6: 1,150 / 131; 7: 336 / 32; 8: 100 / 0. The steps are the price of showing a coarse cut at 0.26 s instead of the old giants for 3.8 s: about a sixth of the 3.1 s (about 0.5 s) bought that.
- **What this means.** The 3.1 s is the cost of making 18,000 nodes again, and the 12,449 of them that are the cut at the surface **were made, drawn and thrown away a moment before, on the way up**. The cost is not reduced by tuning the feed (it is at the machine's rate: about 5,600 nodes a second, empty ones included, on 6 cores) but by making fewer nodes, which is what a warm view (phase 2: nodes that leave the cut are hidden, not dropped) is: coming back to the cut that was left would be swaps of nodes held, the 0.25 s of 8. A cut made cold, the first time or after a far jump, stays a cost of about 1 ms of a core for each node (generating 0.69 of it): half again as much for each node and more with more threads on this machine, and a machine with fewer cores takes longer (one thread: 19.7 s).
- **Not measured:** what a game that renders and runs a server on the same 12 logical processors loses to 11 busy node threads for 3 s (6 threads took 4.3 s against 3.25, so leaving half of them free costs a third more time to the cut; which is better for the frame rate is not known without the renderer in the loop); how much of the 1.07 ms a node could be saved by cheaper generating (0.69 ms of it, 64 percent); whether the empty nodes (16 percent) could be known to be empty without generating them.

## 9c. Phase 2a: the nodes of a view are held, and the 3.1 s is 0.27 s (2026-11-21)

**Built:** `LodView::hold_hidden_pictures(budget)` (off, 0, by default, so every view that does not ask is as it was); `CubeStates` has a fourth state of a node's picture, **held** (not shown, ready, charged to the budget by the bytes of its mesh and at least 64 so that the records are bounded, with an order number that counts up and never repeats: no clock); `MeshUpdate::{HideCoarse, ShowCoarse, HideGridCoarse, ShowGridCoarse}` (the renderer's `hide_mesh` and `show_mesh` of phase 1); `Session::hold_hidden_pictures`, which the windowed and headless runs call with `HELD_PICTURE_BUDGET` = 64 MiB for each view of terrain (the level's, each planet's).

**The rules**, each a test: a node is held only if its picture is of the data now and no job is making another (otherwise it is dropped as before); a held picture of data that has changed since (the server said something of the node while it was held) is not shown and is not touched until the node is wanted, and then it is dropped from the renderer and the node is made again (so the old picture is never put up); past the budget the **oldest** are let go of, never one that is wanted (those are the pin of a single view: what it is about to show); a node with nothing to see is held too, without a mesh (so the generator is not asked to find that out again: 16 percent of the nodes made); a reset takes down held meshes with the rest; showing a held picture does not count toward the cap of 64 pictures put on the screen in an update (it is a move), and a refinement does not make steps for a part of the cut whose pictures are all ready already.

**Measured [run]** (release, updates paced at 60 a second, the probe of 9a, `docs/history/spikes/viewpoints/probe_two_views.rs` with the screen kept as the renderer keeps it): from 264 km up back to the surface, **3.44 s without holding, 0.27 s with it** (both measured from the end of the first update after the move, which is not in them: it took 70 to 170 ms, so from the move it is about 0.34 s with holding, and 9d says what that is made of); 12,449 nodes shown from hold and 100 pictures uploaded where 13,711 were; 3,651 updates in the first call. Going up held 13,878 nodes (24.8 MB of meshes), and 2.7 MB was still held at the end. The view test: the ground (7,564 nodes drawn, 9,056 made for it, in a view that reaches 40,000 voxels) went up 400 bricks and came back with 645 nodes made and 92 pictures uploaded and 6,696 shown from hold; the 645 are probably the steps toward the bricks near the viewer (bricks are not held), which the next piece should look at.

**Not measured / not built:** the memory of 12,000 hidden GPU buffers beyond their bytes (the probe counted meshes' bytes, not the adapter's); bricks (2b); a second view's pins; what a game's frame costs with the budget on (phase 1 measured the hidden meshes at 0.12 to 0.29 ms for 20,000, and nothing here has run in a window).

## 9d. What the rest of a switch is made of (2026-11-21)

Asked: what is the 0.27 s? **[run]** with timers in `LodView::update`, a line for each update that changes something, and a count of the wanted nodes by level and by whether each is shown, held, waiting or not ready (`docs/history/spikes/viewpoints/probe_timeline.rs` and `switch_timing.patch`), the same trip back from 264 km up, held pictures on, updates at 60 a second:

- **The measure left out the first update.** The probe stamps the arrival after `update` returns, and that first update is the one that chooses the cut (`reselect`: 15,147 cubes): **70 to 170 ms** in the runs (138 ms in one), a single frame that long. From the move to the last change the trip is **about 0.34 s** (0.27 s after the first update), and 0.44 to 0.66 s in two earlier runs of the same trip, so the run-to-run spread is wide. 9c's "0.27 s" is corrected in place.
- **All 15,147 wanted nodes were held and ready at the start.** Yet the view made about **2,200 intermediate nodes** (the queue at the reselect was 17,138 cubes: 14,915 wanted nodes, which needed nothing, and the steps) and took the swap in **three waves** of a few updates each, with 80 ms in which nothing changed between them (the threads making the next level's steps): the shown count went 718, 2,478, 4,821, 6,884, 8,862, 10,957, 14,945, 15,147 over about 25 updates.
- **Why:** the 232 wanted **bricks** of the fine ball round the viewer are not ready (the mesher makes them), and they are inside the giant node that holds the viewer, which holds nearly all of the cut. A group changes only when everything that comes is ready, so that giant waits; the progressive swap (11 of 6.2) then replaces it level by level, and each level's nodes (the ancestors of everything below it) must be made first. 9c made the groups with no unready cube change at once (the first update's 585 shows) and no more.
- **The floor, measured by a hack** (bricks counted as ready, which no real view may do): everything is shown in **three updates, 0.095 s from the move**, in two runs, with 0 uploads. So **about three quarters of the 0.34 s is the planner waiting on 232 bricks**, and the rest is the first update.
- **A second cost, to the frame:** after the first update each update spent **5 to 7 ms in the swap planning with nothing to change, and 12 to 47 ms now and then**, for the 25 updates until the view settled (`plan_progressive_swaps` over 15,000 cubes, with hash sets); a 47 ms update is three frames. Holding does not change this: a switch costs the frame rate about 25 frames of 5 to 47 ms and one frame of 70 to 170 ms. **[belief]** the owner's "no noticeable frame-rate cost" is for holding, and a switch's hitch is not what was asked, but it is a cost.

**What would fix it (not built; each is a change to the planner, which has property tests, and the owner's call on order):** (1) **a partial refinement**: where some of what comes is ready and some is not, change the giant for the cubes that are ready and cover what is not ready by the biggest ancestors that hold no ready cube (about tens of nodes for a ball of bricks, where the steps are 2,200), so that the switch shows everything that is held in one update and the cover is replaced as the bricks come; the cold case, where nothing is ready, keeps the levels one at a time. The floor is then the first update plus one. (2) **Keep the cut of a warm view** (phase 3): the first update's choosing (70 to 170 ms) is the work the tracked warm view does ahead of the switch. (3) **A cheaper plan**: `plan_progressive_swaps` and `refinements` per update, which are hash sets of 15,000 cubes; they could be worked out once at a reselect and kept. **Not measured:** what (1) saves in a real switch (the hack is its floor, not its result); whether the 232 bricks take long to be ready in a game (here they were ready within the 0.3 s).

## 9e. The partial refinement, built (2026-11-21)

**Built** in `coarse_cells::swap`: a refinement that cannot change whole (some of what comes is not ready) now puts up **what is ready of it at once and covers the rest**. Each half of the cube that goes that holds a cube that comes is replaced by the cubes that come in it if all of those are ready, and otherwise by the half itself, which has to be ready (a cube made between); the swap is made only if everything that comes in is ready, so there is never a hole. `intermediate_cubes` takes the readiness and returns only the ancestors of the cubes that come and are **not** ready, so a view back from far away over held nodes makes the cubes that hold the bricks and not the ancestors of 15,000 nodes; `LodView::reselect` passes it its own `is_ready`. A cold view, where nothing is ready, plans as it did.

**Tests first:** a fixture of a node over 64 nodes with a brick in one corner (the cubes to make between: 3 where it was 10); what is ready is shown at once and the corner is covered a level at a time; a half whose cubes are all ready comes in as those cubes though it is not ready itself; and a property over random tilings and random readiness (the cubes between ready, those of the cut by a share): no overlap, the same bricks covered, only ready cubes come in, they are wanted or between, rest within as many rounds as levels, **and at rest a cube is shown and not wanted only for a reason** (a wanted half that is not ready, or what comes in it is its halves only). ## 9f. Phase 2b: the bricks of a view are held (2026-11-22)

**Built:** the pictures of bricks are held as those of nodes are (9c). `WorldMesher::hold_hidden_pictures(budget)` (`LodView::hold_hidden_pictures` calls it with the same number, so bricks have **a budget of their own of the size of the nodes'**, up to twice the number in all; 64 MiB each in a game); a brick that is taken down while its picture is current is **held**: the renderer hides its mesh (`MeshUpdate::{HideBrick, ShowBrick, HideGridBrick, ShowGridBrick}`, the renderer's `HeldMesh::Brick` and `GridBrick` of phase 1) and the record keeps the size, and showing it again is a move that makes nothing, **even after the brick went out of range and its voxels were let go of** (a held picture needs none). A brick with nothing to see is held without a mesh (no meshing to find that out again). Held bricks are charged their mesh's bytes and their occupancy's, and at least 256 (about what a record takes); the oldest go first (`LodView` lets go of them after the swap, never a cube that is wanted or a step); showing one does not count toward the 64 pictures an update may put up.

**Two traps, found while building:** (1) **What the sun is told of a brick (`BrickOccupancy`, the contact trace) is cleared at the renderer when the brick goes out of range and is made only when the brick is meshed**, so a brick shown from hold would have had none and the strip of light at a wall's foot would be back. A held picture therefore keeps its occupancy (4 KB for a mixed brick, kept only when pictures are held) and the mesher tells the renderer again when the brick is shown (nothing is sent if it was never cleared). (2) A brick that is shown goes out of range **before** the viewer takes it down, and `unload` threw its picture away, so nothing was ever held; with a budget a current picture is kept at an unload. Without a budget everything is as it was.

**Staleness:** an edit (`brick_changed`) drops the held pictures of the brick and its six neighbours, and a change of what is drawn from (`set_channels`) drops them all; the renderer's hidden meshes are taken down by a `Remove` that is the **first** thing the next update says (or by a reset, which says them too). This relies on the client saying `BrickChanged` for every change to a brick it has, in range or not, which the loaded bricks needed already. **Not handled, as for nodes:** a generator assigned to a region while its pictures are held (the held picture is of the old one). One check was relaxed: a brick whose picture was given out may have no voxels (it is shown from hold before they are loaded again); a picture that is waiting still has to have them.

**Tests first:** 14 for the records (`brick_states`: held and not held, empty bricks, shown again, out of range, edit, every picture, the order of eviction and what is kept, clearing, a picture being made again, the occupancy and its cost, the check), and in `tests/lod.rs`: up and back (no brick uploaded or meshed again, the same pictures, **all 9,288 cubes shown after the first update**), the sun told again (equal to the ground's after the round trip), an edit at a brick's edge while held (pictures of the brick and its neighbour changed, equal to a fresh view's, occupancy too), a budget of a third (exactly the bricks let go of are meshed again), a change of light, a reset (also with pictures out of date and not yet taken down), no budget (no hide or show), and the cap with a ball of over 100 bricks; the path test with pictures held now compares the bricks and the occupancy with those of a fresh view at each stop. **13 mutants** (never hold, an unload that drops the picture, a show that does not tell the sun, an edit that leaves the hidden mesh or the neighbours, a change of channels or a reset that forgets them, nothing or everything let go of, the newest first, a cost without the occupancy, a hide that removes, a show counted toward the cap): **12 caught; the cap one only after a test with a ball of over 100 bricks was added** (the first ball had 44); **not caught: the view letting go of wanted bricks** (the rule is tested in the records, and no test of the view has a wanted brick held while a newer one pushes it over the budget). ## 9h. Phase 3b: a viewpoint is kept warm (2026-11-22)

## 9i. Phase 3c: the warm cut off the update's thread, what applying hide and show costs, and what a map costs that is not used (2026-11-22)

### The cubes of the warm viewpoint on a thread of their own (built)

`LodView` hands the working out of the cubes of the warm viewpoint to a thread of its own (`warm_cut.rs`: `WarmWorker`, made when there is first something for it to do, so a view that keeps nothing warm has no such thread), from **a copy of what the terrain says may show** (`CoarseTerrainSource::detached_may_show`, a `MayShowOracle`, taken when the work is handed over; the default is `None`, and then the cubes are worked out on the update's thread as before: every test source that does not say otherwise, and nothing in the program). What is done with what comes back:
- **One working out at a time**, started at most once in 60 updates counted from its start (as in 9h), and not while another is under way. What comes in is used if it is for the same detail settings, the same version of the terrain and the same count of edits as now: **the place may be a little behind** where the warm viewpoint is by then (a place that moves is what it is: about a second behind), and the cut that is drawn, or the one that was just left, is used instead when it is exactly the place (no working out then, as in 9h). What is not for those is thrown away and another is started at that update.
- **A life of the view** (`warm_epoch`): a reset, or taking the warm viewpoint away, makes what the thread is at, and what it learnt of the terrain (its own `MayShowMemo`, which goes with the job and comes back with the result), of no use: it is thrown away when it comes in.
- **`is_settled` waits for it** (`warm_selection_pending`), and a bug in what the terrain says is a panic of the update that takes the result in, as for the nodes' threads.
- **What the thread is given is a copy**: the edited cubes, the core, the viewer, and the terrain's answers. For a **level** that copies its `WorldTerrain`, which holds the tree of which generator makes which region; copying that was **1.8 to 6.8 ms for 109,025 nodes** on the update's thread (the Cave's client has about 7,500: 0.1 to 0.19 ms; the client's tree grows about 43,000 nodes for each megametre flown (section 6.3), so **[belief, from the 17 ns a node that was measured]** a copy would have been about 75 ms after 100 Mm), so **`RegionOctree` now shares what is not changed** (`Arc` branches, copied when one of two trees is assigned to: `Arc::make_mut`): a copy of the 109,025-node terrain takes **0.25 to 0.7 ms**, and a planet (`ShipTerrainSource`) copies only its generator.
- **The queue of nodes to make is made by the thread too** (`warm_queue_of`): it was sorted on arrival by a key worked out at every comparison (a sum of squares of 128-bit numbers: 15,000 cubes cost several milliseconds), and is sorted once for each cube now (`sort_by_cached_key`) and on the thread.
- **A fault found by measuring a map that follows a ship** (below): a brick that only the warm ball wants, made and held, put `ready_serial` up, which makes the planner of the swaps look at every cube drawn and wanted (about 12,000) at the next update, whatever was drawn: 0.52 ms of the update against 0.29 without a warm viewpoint; it does not any more (a viewer that goes to the brick says so by moving).

| `update_view` with the map ready, the player standing | 0.145 ms | 0.25 ms | 0.22 ms |
| the same, CPU | 0.00 cores | 0.00 to 0.01 | 0.00 |
| 20 s flying, `update_view` mean (median) | 1.41 and 1.49 (0.17) | 1.67 and 1.75 (0.30 and 0.32) | 1.99 (0.28), with the map moved |

**What the map costs the player who does not use it, then:** a map that is high up costs next to nothing (+0.1 to +0.3 ms in an update that is 1.4 ms in flight, +10 MB, and 88 pictures); **a map at the ground costs a one-off burst of CPU that is the whole machine for two seconds (and 2 to 6 ms of uploads in a frame on DX12), 13 MB, +0.08 ms a frame while it stands still, and while it follows a ship +0.4 cores and the uploads of the places it goes over**. The first is the hitch that nobody sees if the map is made while the game loads and the second is the one to watch (a map that follows should take a share of the workers, as 6.1 says of the lazy window; **the share is built since: section 9l**).

**The longest update while the ground map was made was 9.7 to 59 ms in six runs, before the thread (35.6 and 59.1) and after it (9.7, 10.5, 49.4 and 55.5)** (the 11 node threads against the update's: the machine, not the select), so a frame in which the map is made can be a long one whatever is done in the view, and the cure is the share of the workers (**built, 9l: it did not shorten the longest update in one run of each**).

**Unused and the map is not there (the server says no map): nothing.** `LodView::update` at rest took **40.15 us before the warm viewpoint was made (e2dcfab) and 40.40 us after it, with none set** (the median of the medians of 8 rounds of 20,000 calls each; `probe_idle_update.rs`), and walking a brick an update (15,000 cubes worked out at each) 15.83 and 15.89 ms: the code is there and costs a branch. No thread is made (`WarmWorker` is made at the first working out), no cube is pinned, no brick is made for a second ball, and the budget of held pictures is the player's own view's. **What a server that says no costs the wire is nothing today, because nothing is sent for the map** (the server sends the camera's region only: phase 4 puts viewpoints in the protocol, and a viewpoint that is not in the list is none).

**When the map is taken away after it was kept (the server says no later): the cost stops, and the memory is not given back.** After `keep_ready(None)` the CPU is 0.00 cores and `update_view` is where it was, and the pictures that were held for it (15,447 meshes, 26.4 MB of quads, in the ground case, the player's own among them) **are all still there** 15 s later: unpinned, they are held pictures like any other, under the 64 MB budget (`HELD_PICTURE_BUDGET`), and are let go of only when others push them out. For the 88 of the high map it does not matter; for 9,267 pictures it is 18.7 MB of quads, and on DX12 600 MB. **The owner said (2026-11-23) that it should let go of them at once, as a client setting that is on by default: built, see the end of this section.**

**Not measured:** a real frame of the game with the map's pictures (this is the renderer alone, and the session alone, apart); the player at 1 km/s (the speed of the owner's targets); the map in the overworld, orthographic (not built); the server's side of all of it (phase 4); memory of the voxels of the warm ball for a place at the ground (its working set is in the table: 13 MB, of which the bricks of the ball are most); a mesh arena.

### A map that is taken away lets go of its pictures (built 2026-11-23; owner: "make this a client setting, default to release")

**What it does.** When the warm viewpoint is taken away (`LodView::set_warm_viewpoint(None)`, `Session::keep_ready(grid, None)`), the pictures that **were made for it alone** are let go of at that update: the node pictures and the brick pictures that were held hidden without ever being shown (`park`), and that nothing wants now (not wanted, not a step on the way, not pinned: a cube the viewer is going to draw keeps its picture and is shown by a move, as in 9h). The records of those pictures say so (`Picture::Held { parked }`, in `cube_states.rs` and `brick_states.rs`): a picture that was shown and taken down is the view's own history, held like any other under the budget, and **stays**; so does a parked one that was shown and taken down again. `CubeStates::release_parked` and `BrickStates::release_parked` say what the renderer is to take down (`RemoveCoarse`, `Remove`; the planet's the same, through `ShipTerrainMesher`).

**It is a client setting**, as the others of the player's window are: `ClientSettings::release_map_pictures` (**on** by default), `.maprelease [on|off]` while playing (`.maprelease` alone says which), `--map-release on|off` at the start (a recording can be watched either way), `Session::set_release_map_pictures`, which the session gives to the view of the level and of each planet at every update (`LodView::set_release_warm_pictures`, default on as well). Off leaves them to the budget as 9i said (the oldest going first).

**What it does not do:** a warm viewpoint that **moves** leaves what was made for the place it left to the budget (a map that goes back and forth is not made again; a test says so, `a_warm_viewpoint_that_moves_on_leaves...`). Memory of the process does not visibly go down when pictures are let go of (the allocator keeps it; measured with the probe below: the working set was 57 MB after, 53 before), and the memory of the graphics card was not measured.

**Measured [run]** (the planet's map 5 km along the ground, 9,267 pictures kept ready and taken away after a 20 s flight over it with the map following; `probe_map_cost.rs` with `PROBE_MAP=warm_then_off`): with the setting on, **8,925 pictures were taken down in the update that took the map away** and the held pictures went from 15,447 to 6,522 (the player's own, from the flight); with it off, 15,447 before and after. The update that took it away was in the noise of the others (0.21 ms mean over the 15 s after, 0.03 CPU seconds).

**A fault this found, and fixed** (it had been there since 9h): when the viewer went to the warm place and the warm viewpoint was taken away in the same update, the drawn cut took the kept cut (the switch of 9h), and the pins of the warm cut were never taken off: the cubes stayed pinned for good, and the budget could not let go of their pictures when the viewer left again (360 KB held of a 64 KB budget in the test). The pins are taken off whenever any are left, and not only when a warm cut is (`has_pins`). A mutant that the first test of this section did not catch led to it.

**Tests first:** 3 of the node records and 2 of the brick records (`release_parked`: the shown-and-taken-down is not let go of; what is wanted or pinned or kept is not), 4 of the view (let go of and the view's own kept; the setting off; the viewer going there; a warm viewpoint that moves on), 1 of the stale pins, 1 of the commands, 1 of the options, 1 of the session (the level's view, on and off) and 2 of the Cave (a planet's map, on and off: the pictures taken down are exactly those that were put up hidden). The only existing test that changed is the one of the budget path of 9h, which now says it wants the pictures left to the budget (`set_release_warm_pictures(false)`). **16 mutants, all caught** (`mutate_release.py`; two were not at first and led to a stronger test of the viewer going there, which found the fault above).

## 9j. Phase 4a: free regions in the interest (built 2026-11-23, protocol 13)

**What was built.** `SetInterest { level, region, free }` and `InterestChanged { level, region, free, assignments }` (`engine/protocol`, version 13): the region round the body, as it was, and up to eight **free regions** (`MAXIMUM_FREE_REGIONS`) of the same level, the places of viewpoints that have no body. The server (`engine/server`: `interest.rs`, `server.rs`) sends what is new to the client in **any** of the boxes (`newly_covered`: the pieces of the boxes now that none held before, each brick in one piece however many boxes it is in) and keeps it up to date, and the client (`ClientWorld::watched_free`) holds the edited bricks of every region and forgets those in none. A server limits a client's free regions in number and in bricks (`ServerConfig::maximum_free_regions`, 2, and `maximum_free_bricks`, twice the body's by default; the host sets it from the view radius) and sends away a client that asks for more. The session asks for a free region round every place it is told to keep ready in the level's own terrain (`Session::keep_ready(GridId::root(level), ..)`), as big as its own, and takes it back when the place is not kept ready any more.

**Free means read-only (test 10 of section 6.2, for the part that is built).** A cube of a free region that has no generator yet is **not** given one: the server tells the client the generator it would have (the level's current default), which is what the client needs to make the bricks there, and writes nothing in the world's assignments. The region round the body is given generators as it always was, for the part of it that the body did not watch; so a body that comes to a place that was free-watched is given the generator it was told it would have (a test says so). A free region is **terrain only**: the entities, the ships and the messages of the game that reach a place go to the region round the body (`Watch::body`), so a ship that is near a map and far from the camera is not told of by it. A **planet** needs no free region: its edited bricks are in the picture of the ship, which a client has whole wherever it is in the planet's level (**[code]** `grid_replication::grid_messages`, a planet is wanted from anywhere in its level while the client's ships fit under `maximum_ship_bricks`; not tested for a map).

**What I chose that the spec did not say** (the owner may change any of these): (1) the coarse part of the viewpoints (`SetDetail`) stays a message of the coarse plugin and gets its list in phase 4b, not one message with the interest: the protocol crate knows nothing of nodes, and the dependency runs the other way; (2) the free regions are of **the one level** of the interest (a map of another level is not asked for yet); (3) a free region is as big as the camera's own, because the warm viewpoint reads the same settings (its ball of bricks is the same); (4) no priority or role is in the message yet: the body's region is the first and the others are free.

**Not built, and what it leaves wrong:** the coarse nodes that an edit changes far from the camera (the overrides of `coarse_streaming`) are still sent for the camera's pose only, so a map shows the edited **bricks** near its place (its ball) and the unedited **nodes** beyond (4b, **built since: section 9k**); a free region is told to the server whenever the session keeps a place ready, whether or not the map is used (the cost: one more box of edited bricks, which is the edited bricks only).

**Tests first** (all before the code that made them pass): the layout of both messages pinned and damaged frames refused (a count over the most, a box the wrong way round); the server's six (a free region is sent its edited bricks and the edits made in it afterwards and no one else is; a brick in two regions is sent once and its edits once; a free region that moves is sent only what is new; **a free region changes nothing in the world and still says which generator would make it**; the limits; what reaches a place does not reach a free region), the exhaustive test of `newly_covered` over every pair of lists of boxes of a lattice (573,049 cases), the client's four, and the session's (`the_edited_bricks_of_a_place_that_is_kept_ready_are_held_while_it_is_and_forgotten_when_it_is_not_any_more`, which has the control of a place that is not kept ready). **One existing test moved, as it says it will:** `the_standard_session_makes_the_same_recording_on_every_platform` (the recording of a session pins its length and checksum): 84,923 to 84,925 bytes, two more for the count of free regions in each of the two `InterestChanged`, and the checksum for that and the version in the welcome; **checked by taking the recording apart** (the count taken out of those two frames and the version put back to 12 gave the old length and the old checksum). The whole workspace (`--no-fail-fast`): 4,021 tests passed and one GPU test of the renderer failed once (`facing_cull`, nothing of 4a touches the renderer; it passed alone and as its whole binary, and is in `test-flakes.md`). **20 mutants** of the server, the client, the session and the wire (`mutate_free.py`): two survived at first, both for a weak test (the client forgetting the free region's bricks was never tested with a body that moves and a free region that stays; a place kept ready in another level was never tried), and both are caught now.

## 9k. Phase 4b: the coarse nodes of the viewpoints (built 2026-11-23)

**What was built.** `SetDetail` (`engine/coarse_streaming`, a game message, so the protocol stays at 13) carries `places`: up to eight (`MAXIMUM_PLACES`), each a grid and a brick in that grid's own coordinates, written after the zoom as a count and the places. The server's `plan` selects the cubes of each place with the same function and the same settings as for the player (`select_leaves`, or the far-ship pair for a ship told of as far away), **after** the player's: `from` is the player's bricks followed by the places', so a cube that both want is sent once (`pending_updates` keeps the first naming), and coarsest first across all of them, the player's first within a level. The session names the places it keeps ready: in the level's root grid when the level is drawn in nodes, and in a planet or a far ship it knows (`Session::detail_places`); a place in another level, in a grid it does not have, or in a level drawn in bricks only is not asked for. `SetDetail` is no longer `Copy` (it holds a list).

**Test 5 of section 6.2 (the cut is the same on both ends, per viewpoint).** The server's half: for each viewpoint the cubes sent are exactly what the shared selection says from that viewpoint's brick (`select_around_brick`, `far_ship_nodes_to_send`, `far_ship_empty_cubes`, which the client runs too), among the cubes that have an override. The client's half is checked end to end, not by comparing two lists: a watcher 1,000 voxels from a crater, with a place kept ready 350 voxels from it, holds hidden pictures of the nodes over the crater that are the same as those a player standing at the place draws (`the_nodes_an_edit_changes_round_a_place_kept_ready_are_held_as_a_player_there_draws_them`; it fails if the session names no places).

**What I chose that the spec did not say** (the owner may change any of these): (1) the places share the player's detail settings (the one `SetDetail`), because the warm viewpoint reads the same settings today; a map with its own (orthographic, phase 5) needs a settings block for each place, which is a change of this message and a bump of nothing else; (2) a place is a brick in one grid, not a level and a radius (the radius is the settings'); (3) a client with no pose on the server (no body) is still sent nothing, as before: the places are selected for a player who has a pose; (4) the server takes any grid that keeps nodes, in any level, and does not check that it is the player's level (the session asks only for its own).

**Not built, and what it leaves wrong.** **[belief, not measured]** what a map's nodes cost in bytes and in server time in a real game (the places add one more selection and, at most, the overrides round it, which are few where little was edited). A place in a ship that is neither a planet nor far is not asked for by the session (the server would answer it as a near ship; no test of it). The overrides sent for a place that goes away stay on the client, as those of the camera do. Still not built: roles and a priority in the message (the share of the node workers for a warm map was built next: 9l), a game that calls `keep_ready` (phase 5), ships and entities near a free region, free regions in another level.

**Tests first.** The layout of the places pinned and a count over the limit, a count that is short and one that leaves a place over refused; at the server, seven (the cut of a place and of the player, once each and coarsest first; the player's before a place's within a level, with a guard that a level has a cube of each; a place that moves sends only what is new, and one that is dropped or comes back sends nothing; a cube both want is sent once; a grid with no nodes is asked of nothing; a client that wants none is sent none for its places; a place in a far ship), the unit test of the dedupe, at the session two (`the_places_the_server_is_asked_the_nodes_of_are_those_kept_ready_in_terrain_the_player_draws_in_nodes`, and the end-to-end one above). **10 mutants** of the server, the wire and the session, all caught (two had patterns that matched twice and were run again with unique ones). Gate: format, clippy and `cargo doc -D warnings` clean; the whole workspace (`--no-fail-fast`): 4,033 tests passed and one failed, **not by this change: `wide_float`'s `a_number_has_one_encoding`, a proptest that found by chance an encoding its decoder accepts and its encoder would not write** (found 2026-11-23 in the full run; it fails on every run now that the seed is saved in `engine/wide_float/tests/codec.proptest-regressions`; `wide_float` depends on none of the crates this phase touched; **not fixed in this phase; fixed the same day, in its own commit: the cause was in `binary_codec`, whose `read_varint` accepted a longer form of a varint, see `build-log.md`, "Varints have one encoding"**). The golden sessions did not move: none of them sends a `SetDetail`.

## 9l. A share of the threads for the warm viewpoint (built 2026-11-24, the probe and the first tests the evening before)

**Asked for** by 9i (a map at the ground "costs a one-off burst of CPU that is the whole machine for two seconds"; "the cure is the share of the workers") and by 6.1 (a lazy viewpoint is worked out "with a share of the workers"). The owner chose it as the next piece after 4b.

**Measured first [run]** (release, `docs/history/spikes/viewpoints/probe_map_cost.rs`, `PROBE_MAP=warm PROBE_MAP_HEIGHT=0 PROBE_MAP_ALONG=5000 PROBE_FOLLOW=0`, a place at the ground 5 km along that nothing had looked at, the probe's client with 11 node threads, a machine of 6 cores and 12 logical processors, one run of each, nothing else running):

| keeping the ground map ready | before | with the share |
|---|---|---|
| the map is ready after | 1.7 s | 3.1 s |
| CPU used while it is made | 17.1 s | 15.2 s |
| the same, in cores on average | **10.25** | **4.86** |
| worst `update_view` in that time | 6.35 ms | 8.07 ms |
| 20 s flying (the map not moved) | 0.40 cores, worst 23.8 ms | 0.44 cores, worst 38.1 ms |

So **the burst takes half the machine for twice as long** and costs the same CPU in all (15 to 17 s; the difference is not a gain). **The worst update did not fall**: 8.07 against 6.35 ms in the burst, and 38.1 against 23.8 ms in the flight, where the share does nothing (the map is not moved, and no warm node is made). **[belief, not run]** that is the spread between runs of this probe, which 9i measured as 9.7 to 59 ms in six runs; one run of each cannot say it is not the change. What the share does for the frame rate of the game is **not measured**: it leaves cores free, which is what it is for, and a frame that waited for a core is a frame this test does not have.

A second measure on the old design (a throwaway test with a node that took 3 ms, 8 threads, 2,584 cubes drawn and the ground warm): the warm nodes ran on **8 of 8 threads at once**, and what a moved viewer needed was drawn in 205 ms alone and 215 ms with the warm nodes being made. **The queue in front of the drawn nodes was not the harm** at this cost per node (the view holds only 4 nodes for each thread when the updates come every millisecond, 32 in all, and up to 64 each when they run dry): a drawn node waits for as many warm nodes as the view has handed out, and for slow nodes (caves, 94 to 201 ms) that is seconds, which the share bounds as well.

**What was built.** `world_view::job_queue::JobQueue`, in place of the channel the threads took jobs from: two queues, **drawn** and **warm**; a thread takes a drawn job before any warm one that waits, whichever was pushed first, and takes a warm one only while fewer than `warm_threads` threads are making warm jobs. **Half of the threads, at least one, by default** (`default_warm_workers`; 11 threads: 5), `LodView::set_warm_worker_count` to change it. `LodView` counts what it handed out for the warm viewpoint apart (`warm_nodes_in_flight`, `warm_node_cubes`, `warm_finished_last_update`) and sizes it by the job feed (`jobs_to_hold`) for the share of the threads, so what is held for the warm viewpoint does not take room from what is drawn, nor the other way. **A warm node that waits in the queue when the viewer goes to its place is promoted**: it is made as a drawn one, before the warm nodes that wait (`JobQueue::promote`, called when the cubes are chosen again). A view that is dropped drops what waits in the queue (it used to finish all that had been handed out before it ended). `LodView::warm_nodes_being_made` and `warm_worker_count` say what it does.

**One test changed its meaning, and I changed it, not weakened it.** `what_the_drawn_viewpoint_needs_is_made_before_anything_for_the_warm_one` pinned the order in which nodes were **handed out** (none for the warm viewpoint until all of what was drawn was handed out); the warm nodes are handed out under a limit of their own now, so that order is not the rule. Its replacement, `what_the_drawn_viewpoint_needs_is_made_before_the_warm_nodes_that_wait_though_they_were_handed_out_first`, holds the threads at a gate, hands the warm nodes out first and the move's after, opens the gate and asserts that at least 9 of the first nodes the threads start are the move's (the 3 threads write what they start a moment after they take it, so up to 3 may be written out of order). It fails if the queue is first in, first out.

**What the share does not cover** (not built): the **bricks** of the warm viewpoint's ball (the mesher's own threads: 343 bricks or so, the part of the 15 s of CPU that is not nodes was **not measured apart**); the thread that works the cubes of the warm viewpoint out (one thread, `WarmWorker`); a share that depends on whether the map is followed or made for the first time, or on the player's frame time (a counted rule, not a clock); the setting for the player (`.mapworkers` or such: the view has the call, the session does not use it, and it is not a client setting yet).

**Tests first.** The queue's seven (drawn before warm and each in its order; the cap and the next waiting until one is done, a drawn job never waiting for warm ones; a share of none is one; a waiting warm job made a drawn one and one that is taken or absent not; a closed queue drops what waited; threads that never run more warm jobs than the share, and run every job; a drawn job made at once by a free thread while warm jobs are held), and the view's three (nodes of the warm place made on half the threads at most, 3 of 6, and all of them made; what is held for each is apart, 8 and 4 for 2 threads; a waiting warm node of the place the viewer goes to is made as a drawn one, a reset forgets what was held) and the replacement above. **14 mutants** of the queue and the view, all caught (one, the warm limit counted against the drawn count, was caught by a failure that named no test). Whole workspace (`--no-fail-fast`): 4,047 tests passed, none failed.
