# Joints and cars: what was decided (owner, 2026-11-01; the solver changed 2026-11-09; being built on the branch `worktree-dev-ball-joint`)

A joint lets one ship have parts that move against each other. The first one is a prototype, the **dev ball joint**, a block that couples two cars like a ball hitch.
This file records the talk that came before any code; the measurements behind the physics choices are in `joint-probe.md`. Status key as in `design-decisions.md`.

## Words (owner)
- **Ship** is the whole vehicle; a **car** is the blocks of one grid of it. (The code's `Ship` is one grid; it is to be renamed to say so, a mechanical change, not yet made.)
- **Dock / undock** are used only for docking ports, and **never** for anything else. **Lock / unlock** are the same act for joints. **Dock and lock merge grids; nothing else does.**
  Undock and unlock split them.
- **Couple / decouple**: two joint blocks come together and hold, or are parted. (Not "connect": that word is the network's, `ServerMessage::Disconnected`.)
  Coupling is automatic when the blocks meet; decoupling is a command. **Mobilize / immobilize**: let the joint move, or make it rigid at its present angle, without merging.
- "Hinge" is reserved for a joint with one axis. "Port" is not shortened from "docking port", because it may name fluid attachments later.
- The command is `/joint lock|unlock|mobilize|immobilize|decouple`. **Decouple is always available**: on an immobilized joint it mobilizes and decouples in one step, on a locked one it
  unlocks and decouples in one step.

## The block
- Id `dev_ball_joint` (creative only, like the dev docking port, which stays as it is). 0.75 x 0.5 x 0.5 m. It is put on the face that was clicked, facing away from it, one small
  end flush with the face and centred, the other end 0.25 m short of the next cell. It has **its own face property, `engine:joint_face`**, and its own finder, so that `ports_of`,
  `docked_pairs` and `dock` never see a joint as a docking port. The geometry the two share (`Port`, `PortView`, `approach`) gets a neutral name. A block may later have both properties
  (a joint that is also a docking port).
- **Built 2026-11-09 (step 2):** the block is a data file, `games/voxunami_cave/data/blocks/dev_ball_joint.json`, the last block of Cave (number 13, so no number moved). As made it is the box
  `[2,0,2]-[6,6,6]` in eighths of a voxel (a voxel is a metre: 0.5 by 0.75 by 0.5), gunmetal gray, with its ball end up and that face a flat blue; `engine:axis_face` is up, so it is placed like a
  log, with the ball end away from the face that was clicked; `engine:joint_face` is up too. It has no pictures yet. The finder is `Docking::joint_ends_of` (the same scan of a brick that finds
  ports and mass, `BrickScan`); it never returns a port, and `ports_of` never returns a joint end. The shared geometry is `BlockFace`, `BlockFaceView` and `approach` (`Port` and `PortView` were
  renamed; the docking code and its tests are as they were). The code does not keep the ends of joints yet: that is the coupling logic (step 3).
- Two such blocks facing across a cell boundary, 0.5 m apart at the tips, are **coupled**; a ball of 0.5 m diameter is drawn in the gap, and only then. The ball is two hemispheres, one in
  each cell: bent, the join shows; that is accepted for the prototype.
- States of a pair: apart, coupled (mobile), coupled and immobilized, locked. A joint is **derived from block states and positions and never saved** (since 2026-11-11 the *pair* is saved with the world: item 12).

## Coupling, lock, unlock
- Couple when the pivots are within 0.3 m and the faces within 45 degrees; until then pull with 10 kN each (as for docking, independent of mass) until the pivots are within 0.05 m, then the joint.
  Coupling applies positional forces only, no torque toward 180 degrees. **Agreed 2026-11-09:** as for docking, the two cars must also be close in velocity when the joint is made (a limit on their relative
  speed, **1 m/s of linear speed and 0.2 rad/s of angular speed between the two cars at the pivots, agreed 2026-11-09**), because a joint made with a relative velocity begins with a collision that loses energy and leaves a gap (found building the first tests, `build-log.md`).
- **Lock** is the only thing that turns the cars toward alignment (180 degrees, or the nearest turn that lines the grids up cube for cube). Within the docking snap (0.3 m, 10 degrees) it snaps
  and merges the grids. A lock request lasts until it succeeds or the joint is mobilized; on an immobilized joint it is refused with "mobilize first".
- **Unlock** splits the grids and lands in *coupled (mobile)*. Lock and unlock act on the **pair of cars**: unlock cuts every joint between them at once (the flood fill in `undock` returns
  `Welded` if the halves touch anywhere else).
- Cars collide with each other, and the two cars of a coupling too: **contacts between them stay on (owner, 2026-11-09).** Coupling across physics frames is refused for now.
- **The dev ball joint lets one car pitch, yaw or roll against the other (owner, 2026-11-09):** the ball turns freely about all three axes (what a spherical joint is). No angle limit has been chosen; the
  hulls stop a bend where they meet (the cars are 2 m apart at the pivot's ends at rest).
- **A joint never breaks, for starters (owner, 2026-11-09).**
- **Built 2026-11-09 (step 3, `engine/grid_bodies/src/joints.rs` and `plugin/couplings.rs`), and what the tests found, which is where it differs from the words above:**
  - *The pull is a controller, mine, and not a constant force.* A constant 10 kN toward the other end arrives at its greatest speed exactly where the coupling is made, so no pair could meet the speed limit (worked out, not
    measured: a pair of 500 kg cars from 6 m arrives at 15 m/s). The pull is **at most 10 kN on each of the two cars** (the owner's number, equal and opposite, so the lighter car moves more), and it asks the pair for the greatest speed it can be
    stopped from in the distance left (a deceleration of 2 m/s/s, or half of what 10 kN does to the pair if that is less), taking up the difference over 0.1 s. A longer response (0.5 s) let light ends overshoot each other and
    jam with their tips in contact; the lag of the speed behind the one asked is the deceleration times the response, 0.2 m/s. From 3 to 6 metres, at rest, cars of 1.2 to 30 tonnes were coupled after 1.5 to 4.6 seconds.
  - *The pull is at the end, as docking's is at the port (owner, 2026-11-09: "don't differ from docking").* I had put it on the centre of mass first, because a pull at an end away from the centre swings the car, and two ends side by side
    swung the two cars in opposite ways, 0.6 rad/s each (1.2 rad/s of one against the other, measured), which nothing took away and which is six times the 0.2 rad/s a coupling allows, so such a pair never coupled. With the pull at the
    end, **a torque slows the cars' spin against each other** (equal and opposite, at most 10 kN m, docking's twist torque; the inertia is a third of the trace of each car's tensor, an approximation): the two-ends pair then couples, and
    without the torque it does not (checked by taking it out). It only slows their turning against each other: nothing turns the ends toward each other, so the 45 degrees is for the players to aim. **This is a torque, which the first words of this
    section said coupling would not apply; it is not toward alignment.**
  - *What is pulled is a group.* Cars joined by couplings are one mass under a pull, and **a group is pulled by one other group at a time, the nearest**. A chain of three cars (the middle one pulled from both sides) overshot by
    half a metre and jammed with the tips of the bars in contact; with this rule it couples. Two cars of one group are not pulled or coupled to each other (a loop is for later).
  - *When the joint is made:* in the first tick the ends are within 0.3 m, the faces within 45 degrees of exactly opposite, the pivots going past each other at 1 m/s or less and the cars turning against each other at 0.2 rad/s or less.
    That is how I read "until the pivots are within 0.05 m, then the joint": the pull does not go on to 0.05 m, the solver closes what is left. Measured: the pivots were 0.12 to 0.28 m apart and closing at 0.5 to 1.0 m/s, and the gap
    was closed in the tick (by the solver's position correction, which adds no speed): the two cars stop against each other at the pivot, an inelastic joining that loses at most half the reduced mass times the speed squared
    (worked out, not measured: 750 J for two 3.2 tonne cars at 0.97 m/s; both cars' speeds were 0.000 in the tick after, and the gap 0), and nothing is gained. **The owner agreed (2026-11-09) to keep the snap of up to 0.3 m.**
  - *The pivot is the middle of the end's face* (a quarter of a metre beyond the bar's tip), so two coupled ends have their tips half a metre apart, as this file says the ball is drawn.
  - *Coupled cars keep 4 extra substeps and may not sleep* (set when coupled, undone when the car is in no coupling): a coupled pair given 5 cm/s kept going for ten seconds; without the switch it went to sleep after half a second (since 2026-11-11 no body sleeps unless it touches something, so in space the switch is not what keeps them awake, and on the ground it still is: item 7)
    and stopped dead, 2 cm in ten seconds.
  - *One ball for two cars, in one frame:* the closest pair of ends of two cars makes the coupling and the other ends of the same two cars are left (the integer rule is for later); a coupling is let go when either car or end is gone, and
    otherwise not (a joint never breaks).
  - *A group is always in one frame (step 4a, built the same day).* A car leaves its frame at 32 km from the frame's origin and its joint died with its body; one car of a pair crosses first (by my arithmetic, not measured: about one crossing in a hundred for a pair four metres
    long at 6 km/s, a tick being 300 m of flight; more for a long train). Now the cars coupled to one that leaves leave with it and are put in a frame together (a car goes to its coupled partner's frame if it is near enough), and the joint is made again in the
    new frame as it was (`couplings_moved`, not `couplings_made`). Tested with the edge of a frame run between two coupled cars, and with one car just inside another frame's radius and its partner just outside (each test fails when its
    half of the fix is taken out). **The 8 km size of a group is what lets a whole group be near one frame's origin, and nothing enforces it yet**; a train longer than a frame would lose its joints again.
  - *A group is kept as one by the lifecycle (step 4b, the owner chose the server holding the groups).* The server world holds the pairs of ships that are coupled (`ServerWorld::coupled_ships`; the bodies plugin writes it each tick after it couples and lets go); the lifecycle
    (`engine/grid_lifecycle`, `groups.rs`) takes the squared distance of the nearest ship of a group, in memory or in a file, as the distance of every ship in it. So a group is frozen when every ship of it is beyond the stop distance, let go when its nearest ship is within the
    simulation distance, put in files when every ship is frozen and the nearest is beyond the unload distance, and brought in when the nearest is within the simulation distance (a ship that waits for its pilot stays in its file, but its place counts). A group with a ship awake and a
    ship frozen (a ship brought in alone from its file) is awake: the frozen ships are let go as long as the nearest is within the stop distance. **The pair is remembered while one of its ships is in a file** (the joint went with the body); when both are in memory the joint is made
    again by the coupling rule at once, the ends being together and a frozen ship having no speed (so the 0.2 rad/s spin limit is not in the way; the centimetre waiver was made for this). The pair is forgotten when a ship is gone or an end is. Not saved: a world loaded from a file has
    the pairs forgotten, and its ships come back by their own distances until the bodies couple them again (step 4d).
  - *Not done, and a problem seen:* **A coupling is derived and never saved**, as decided; but the rule asks for relative spin of
    0.2 rad/s or less, so a swinging group that is saved and loaded would not be coupled again until it turned slowly. The joint only cares about the speed of the pivots (a ball turns however it is), so **the spin limit is waived for ends that are
    already within a centimetre (owner, 2026-11-09: yes, a centimetre; `spin_waived_within`)**; the speed limit is not.

## Physics (decided for the prototype; the reasoning is in `joint-probe.md`)
- **Rapier works in f64** (done 2026-11-01, before any joint was built): the joint error floor was f32 rounding of the distance from the frame's origin (about 1 mm at the frame's edge), and it is now rounding of an `f64`.
  The measurements above were taken in `f32` and are to be repeated in `f64` when the joint is built.
- **Decided by the owner, 2026-11-09: the first solver is Rapier's impulse joints, one spherical joint for each pivot pair of a coupling.** This replaces the multibody tree this file first chose (2026-11-01):
  `loop-probe.md` found impulse joints conserve momentum to rounding and cost about linearly in the cars, and a multibody neither. So a second coupling on the same pair and a coupling that closes a loop
  are not refused by the solver (the integer consistency rule below still applies to a second coupling of one pair), the root car and the heaviest-car rule are gone, and an immobilized joint is not a
  fixed multibody joint (how to immobilize is decided when it is built). The substeps are no longer fixed at 16 (that was measured in f32, `joint-probe.md`): the joint gap at the game's settings is 0.85 mm
  and falls about four times for each doubling of the substeps (`loop-probe.md`, section 14), so the substeps and passes are dials (Rapier keeps them per body: `additional_solver_iterations`,
  `additional_pgs_iterations`). **A coupled car gets extra substeps by default (owner, 2026-11-09: yes; the number proposed is 4 extra):** a car a thousand times lighter than its partner, swinging
  fast, left a gap of 6.0 mm at the game's settings and 0.16 mm with 12 extra (`build-log.md`, "Joints, step 1").
- **Planned, not built: the owner will set the kind of joint per ship with commands.** The options named (2026-11-09): impulse joints for everything (the first, above); a multibody with impulse joints for
  the loops; a multibody with an MLCP solver for the loops. This is why the physics layer is given a **coupling = two cars and a list of pivot pairs**, never one joint per block: the choice of solver is
  inside that one box, and nothing outside it (the blocks, the coupling logic, the commands) may know which kind it is.
- The **integer consistency rule** applies when a second joint couples the same two cars: the squared distance between the pivots must be equal in both cars' grids, in half-voxel integers
  (and, from four joints, the handedness). Without it the cars are permanently strained by the mismatch (measured: 1.44 times it). It does not apply to the closing link of a loop of
  three or more cars; that depends on the pose.
- Future, not scheduled: several couplings per pair reduced to one equivalent constraint (two on a line are a hinge, three not on a line are rigid), then a direct solver for loops
  that sits beside Rapier. Not a fork of Rapier.

## Bounds and lifecycle
- For choosing which bricks need colliders, a ship's bounds are the sum of its cars' longest dimensions used as a diameter, per car (a to-do is to improve this, `future-goals.md`).
- A coupled group **freezes and is parked together**, by the nearest of its cars, so that a joint never anchors an active car to a frozen one. **A mechanism moves only while a player is within 1,024 voxels
  of the group (owner, 2026-11-09: "let's start with 1 km", then "1024 voxels for both of them for now")**; a group with no player that near is frozen, and parked when none is near enough to unload it, and its rail goes on all the same
  (see "Promises"). **The 1,024 is the simulation distance of every ship, jointed or not** (`ShipSettings::DEFAULT_SIMULATION_DISTANCE`, was 256, changed 2026-11-09); the unload distance was 1,024 already, so a ship is put away as soon as it stops moving.
- **A coupled group is at most 8 km across (owner, 2026-11-09, "for now")**, so that it fits in one physics frame (a frame is 16 km from its origin to its edge).
- Later, a joint between a planet and a car: lock and unlock to a planet reuse `dock_into_planet` and `undock_from_planet`; the open problems are in `future-goals.md`.

## Under review (2026-11-06; **still open except the choice of solver, decided 2026-11-09 above**): what measuring the plan above found
`loop-probe.md` ran the plan in Rapier 0.36.1 (f64) and `BodyWorld`. The owner wants to keep both ways of closing a loop open and to **choose per ship**, with the solver's dials (Rapier has them per body:
`additional_solver_iterations`, `additional_pgs_iterations`) as ship data. Numbers are in that document; in short:
- Rapier's multibody costs about the cube of the cars: 24 cars 8.4 ms of a 50 ms tick, 56 cars 71 ms; impulse joints grow about linearly (86 and 175 us).
- A multibody does not conserve linear momentum, with or without a loop (first order in the step: a spinning rig of 16 cars strays 2.7 m in 40 s at a 1 ms step); impulse joints conserve it to rounding. The loop method
  (Rapier's own impulse closing joint between two links, which it supports; or a direct 3 x 3 solve beside Rapier) changes the loop's gap (9e-6 m or 1e-14) and little else. So the choice that matters is multibody against
  impulse joints, not the loop method; a multibody for small articulated assemblies and impulse joints with the dials for everything else is what the numbers favour (opinion).
- Overlapping colliders joined by a joint make energy (1.2 MJ from a 0.5 m overlap) and, with a multibody joint, momentum, unless the joint turns contacts off between its bodies (then exactly nothing).
  **A rule to add whichever solver is chosen:** a joint disables contacts between its two cars, and so does every pair of cars of one rigid group.
- A multibody puts a child where its joint coordinates say, so its joint frames must be chosen from the poses the cars have when it is built (a ring built without them was straightened and exploded).


## Promises (owner, 2026-11-09; the rules below are the owner's answers to the first round of questions, the way to build them is still to be designed)
The owner's words, condensed:
- **(a) A mechanism works the same everywhere.** Joint physics work the same at all speeds, in an atmosphere or out of it. A player never meets a mechanism that works in one situation and fails in
  another for a reason that is not obvious, or for a bug. It is fine for a custom landing gear or a Star Trek turbolift to stop working because air rushes through the interior (a hole), because of extreme-G
  manoeuvres, or because the ship landed on a planet of high gravity. It is **never** fine for a mechanism to stop working or work differently because of what the coordinates look like, which ships are
  near, how many players are on the server, or how fast the ship goes in a vacuum. The places where the game's intended behaviour breaks down are few, simple to memorize and not arcane.
- **(b) Ships with joints go on rails.** The ship is kept at the centre of a physics frame and the frame is moved along the rail. The motion of joints, motorized or not, cannot affect the rail. Engines
  affect the rail. If two ships cross paths while one is on a rail they may not both be at the centre of a frame, but whatever error that brings must not affect the rail. The only thing that takes a ship
  off its rail is a collision with another ship or with a player on the outside of the ship. A player on the inside (in the pressurized area) changes the ship's course by nothing, bumping into it or using
  suit thrusters.
- **The goal:** players can make and keep extremely precise trajectories to extremely distant places and still have fun inside the ship and use motorized parts.

**The owner's answers (2026-11-09, second round):**
1. **"The same" is bit-identical**, wherever the ship is and however fast it goes. (Means, **[mine]**: the group's frame is centred on the group's centre of mass, so the numbers a mechanism works
   with are the same whatever the place; tests that the same ship gives the same bits at the origin, a long way off and at speed, alone and among a thousand ships.)
2. A ship's frame is **in free fall**: no tidal pull on a ship inside its frame (accepted).
3. **Every turning ship, jointed or not, rides a rail**, and its turning is worked out in the frame. (This reverses what `coasting.rs` does today: a rail holds a ship's place and not its turn.)
4. **A ship is on a rail whenever it is not in an atmosphere.** For a ship landed on a body that has no atmosphere the owner left the choice to me: "whatever keeps complexity low and is most likely to be
   bug-free". **[mine, not built]** It is off its rail while anything is near it, as today (`NEAR_MARGIN`), an ordinary body of the contact pass; it goes on a rail again when it has lifted off and been quiet for
   `QUIET_SECONDS`. No new landing rail. (No atmosphere exists in the game yet; today a ship is off its rail near anything, in a level with a pull of its own, and when it turns.)
5. **A collision with the outside of a ship gives its rail a kick and also changes its spin**, and the rest of the blow goes into the cars' motion against each other: the owner expects this to need
   careful design. **[mine, to design before the lifecycle phase]** The kick on the rail must be the exact impulse, and so must the change of angular momentum; how the impulse is shared between the
   ship's motion, its spin and its joints is for the joint solver to say (an impulse put on the struck car in the frame, at the point of contact), but the *size* of the impulse comes from a contact pass
   that sees one body (`loop-probe.md`, section 14, which found the whole ship as one contact body works and a car by car one does not), and a ship hit through a loose joint has a smaller effective mass
   at the point of impact than the whole ship. Which of the two sizes to use is the open part.
6. **Two railed ships that pass close both stay on their rails**; their collision is worked out in a temporary shared frame in which the rails are the authority, and only a real hit changes either.
7. **Inside** is the ship's *mostly enclosed* space, and any space that has life support air. Players, cargo and loose items there never move the rail by moving or by colliding with the ship. A player
   on EVA **does** move it (nudging an out-of-fuel ship with suit thrusters is wanted). A **deflector shield block**, a **tractor beam block** or some similar advanced block can give a ship the
   property that EVA and collisions with other ships do not change its rail: a forcefield round the ship inside which they have no effect on the rail and which nothing outside can enter (a player or
   ship is deflected), paid for with a stored resource (energy, mana) of the ship that projects it, and with **no reaction force on that ship**. Both blocks are in `future-goals.md`. **"Mostly enclosed" (owner, 2026-11-09, for now): more than 95 percent enclosed, measured by casting rays from the place to find the sky.** Later, perhaps: a flood of
   starlight through the cells, measured by how bright it is where the player stands.
8. **A mechanism moves only while a player is within 1 km** (see "Bounds and lifecycle").
9. **Time warp:** for starters mechanisms pause while the rail goes on. **Later** (`future-goals.md`): what is aboard goes on at normal speed while the outside universe and the ship's place on its
   rail go on at a high speed, so that an hour aboard is an hour of ordinary play (crops grow, fire spreads) in a year of travel.
10. **No sleeping state between active and parked.** A group nobody is near is parked and its rail goes on, even with nobody within light years. **Read in the source, 2026-11-09, not run now:** a
    rail is a function of the world's clock (`coasting.rs`, header), so "observed or not, a ship on a rail is where the clock says, and one saved on a rail and brought back is where the clock says
    too". A parked ship that is *not* on a rail is where it was left, not moving; that is what today's rule that a turning ship is not put on a rail would have meant for a group with a motor, and what
    answer 3 removes.
11. **A thrusting group flies free until burns on rails exist (owner, 2026-11-09: "yes").** Engines on a rail are step C of `orbits-and-thrust-plan.md`, not built: today any thrust takes a ship off
    its rail. Until step C, a jointed group that is thrusting is an ordinary body of the contact pass, its frame following its own body, with no promise about the precision of its path; only a
    coasting group rides a rail. Its mechanisms behave the same either way (promise (a)): the frame is centred on the group in both. "Engines affect the rail" (promise (b)) waits for step C.

**What the code did on 2026-11-09 that these would forbid (read in the source, 2026-11-09, not run). Since 2026-11-10 item 3 no longer holds for a ship alone (4c-2: a turning ship rides a rail), nor, since 2026-11-11, for a group (4c-3: a rail holds its centre of mass) and item 4 no longer holds (since 2026-11-11 a body sleeps only while it touches something); the rest stand until their steps:**
1. `BodyWorld::steps_for_this_tick` cuts a tick into 3 to 24 steps by the speed of the **fastest dynamic body in the frame**, so a mechanism's solver steps depend on its own speed and on every other ship in its frame.
2. A frame is one `BodyWorld` for all the ships of a place; its origin is the brick of the first ship that made it, and a ship moves to another frame (its joints would die) at 32 km from the origin.
3. A rail is per grid, holds a place and not a turn (`coasting_candidate`: a ship that turns is not put on one), and any force or torque takes the ship off it; a joint motor turns cars.
4. Bodies can sleep: a body alone in space slower than about 0.09 m/s slept after 0.5 s and stopped dead in the probe (`loop-probe.md`, not measured again through `BodyWorld`), which would stop a slow mechanism.
5. A ship far from every player is frozen (`ServerWorld::set_grid_frozen`, `ShipSettings::simulation_distance`, 256 voxels then, 1,024 since 2026-11-09), so a mechanism stops when no player is near.
6. Positions in a frame are `f64` from the frame's origin, so a mechanism's numbers differ in the last bits with where in the frame it is (the rounding floor of an `f64` is about 5e-13 m at 4 km, as worked out in `loop-probe.md`; a joint's actual gap is millions of times that).

## Step 4c plan: a frame that goes with a ship's centre of mass (2026-11-09; **a plan, nothing built; the questions at the end wait for the owner**)
**What was read (the source, not run).** `Follow` (`grid_bodies/src/plugin.rs`) is already a frame that goes with a *planet on a rail*: places in it are the world's less how far the planet has gone, velocities are
the world's less the planet's, the ships in it are ordinary dynamic bodies, and `write_back` puts them in the world through `follow.world(..)` and `velocity_from(..)`. So a ship that rests on a planet, or orbits it,
is worked out as if the planet stood still. 4c is that mechanism with *a ship's centre of mass on a rail* in the planet's place. On the world's side `put_ships_on_their_rails` already places the **centre of mass** (the origin is put
at the rail's place less the centre of mass turned as the ship is), and `has_to_leave_its_rail`/`coasting_candidate` refuse a turning ship. Two things stop a turning ship on a rail today, and both have to change together:
1. a rail with no `spin` **overwrites** `ship.angular_velocity` with zero on every tick (`world.rs`, end of `put_ships_on_their_rails_remembering`) and keeps the orientation it finds, so the turn would be lost;
2. a ship on a rail is a `BodyKind::Fixed` body (`body_kind`), which Rapier does not turn.
The existing `Spin` of a rail is a steady turn of a *planet* about an axis (its +y is carried onto the axis); a torque-free ship is not that (it precesses unless it turns about a principal axis), so the rail is not given the ship's turn:
**the rail holds the centre of mass and nothing else, and the turn is the bodies' work in the frame**, as answer 3 says ("its turning is worked out in the frame"). Rapier turns a free body with its own gyroscopic step (kept
angular momentum, explicit; `loop-probe.md`, section 8 has its drift: an exact torque-free rotor step is an open question there, not asked again here).

**The design.**
- **A rail frame**: a frame whose `Follow` is not a planet but a *rail point* (the centre of mass of a ship or group, `rail.state(time)` of the carrier's rail). Its origin is made at the point; the ship is a **dynamic**
  body in it, at rest at the origin, with gravity zero (free fall; the owner's answer 2). Because its numbers are the ship's own, small and relative, they are the same bits at the origin, a long way off and at speed
  (answer 1): the speed of the frame does not enter, and neither does `steps_for_this_tick`, which counts the fastest body of the frame (a rail frame has only the ship).
- **Each tick, before the bodies step**, the ship's world pose is moved by the rail's displacement (as `apply_rails` moves a ship carried by a planet, and `Follow.shift` follows it). **After the step**, the centre of
  mass in the frame is put back on the frame's point and its velocity back to zero (re-centring: the drift is rounding, because joints and the turn keep momentum to rounding; a real blow takes the ship off its rail first).
  The rail is the authority for the place: nothing the bodies do moves it. That is promise (b).
- **Who carries the rail.** One ship (the anchor) holds the rail, as now. For a single ship the anchor is the ship. For a **group** the rail is the group's centre of mass (total mass, centre of mass of the cars), kept on the
  car that names the group (lowest id); the other cars carry no rail but are listed as riders by the pairs the server already holds (`ServerWorld::coupled_ships`).
- **Frame and rail are separate.** A frame centred on the group is wanted for every jointed group, on a rail or off it (promise (a): a mechanism must not change because a ship is near and the group came off its rail).
  Off a rail, the frame's point is the group's own centre of mass, re-anchored every tick from the bodies (the same re-centring, with the velocity taken from the group). The rail decides only whether the *path* is exact.
- **What does not change.** A ship that does not turn and has no joint stays a fixed body on a rail in an ordinary frame, as today (no frame per ship; the cost of one `BodyWorld` per coasting ship near a player is unmeasured).
  The near rule (`NEAR_MARGIN`) and every push still take a ship off its rail, and the ship then keeps its frame; the temporary shared frame of two railed ships (answer 6) and the kick of a blow (answer 5) are later.

## Rails and the lifecycle, after the ship audit (2026-11-10)
Two seams that groups on rails (4c) would have made much worse were fixed first (build log, "Ship audit follow-up"): **a ship taken off its rail by a neighbour keeps its speed** (it used to go on from rest), and **a ship on a rail is never put in a file by distance** (it is frozen, its rail goes on; one in a file with a rail is brought in whatever the distance). For 4c this means a group on a rail is always in memory, which is also what "parked groups keep their rail" needs: they are not parked. The other way, a rail in the parked record evaluated each tick (with the hand-overs between spheres), costs the same time per ship and saves only memory; it stays open until the memory of many ships on rails matters.

- **(3) Realistic microgravity.** A player aboard a ship in orbit floats, unless the ship spins, when the centrifugal pull lets them walk.

This also ends the flip with the rail (the rule no longer asks whether there is a rail). The Coriolis term has to be an exact turn of the speed, not an Euler step (an Euler step grows the speed by 0.1 percent a tick at 1 rad/s and 60 steps a second: 1.4 times in ten seconds, worked out, not run). **Decided (owner, 2026-11-10): a floating player moves with suit thrusters**, in a life-support atmosphere (ducted fans, no fuel) and in a vacuum (EVA thruster fuel may come later; - **(4) A change of a ship's centre of mass. Decided (owner, 2026-11-10, second reply): the present behaviour stays for now**: a block placed or broken steps the whole ship by the shift of its centre of mass, which stays on its rail. Fitting the rail at the edit, deferring the fit with a saved offset and ramping the mass of blocks under construction are written up, with the numbers below and the worst cases, in `ship-centre-of-mass-edit-options.md`, and are on the unscheduled list (`future-goals.md`). What was checked: the owner leaned to an offset kept on the ship: if the centre moves 0.5 m forward the ship is placed 0.5 m farther forward on the rail from then on, to be removed when nobody is near.
  *Checked (ran, `docs/history/spikes/rails/offset_vs_refit.py`, a circle of 70,000 m at 761.5 m/s, a 0.5 m shift, the new centre of mass keeping the old velocity):* the sign and size are right (the offset is the change of the centre in the ship's axes, summed over the edits, turned with the ship; no block moves). But a rail that carries a point that is not the centre of mass is a displaced copy of an orbit, and it is not an orbit: a shift **along the path** stays exactly on the true path (0.001 m after 20 orbits), a shift **across it** (radial) leaves it by 9.4 m an orbit (47 m in 0.8 h, 188 m in 3.2 h; 6 pi times the shift an orbit). So an offset can only be held for seconds or minutes, and the removal at the end is a jump only if the rail is not fitted again. **The alternative with no jump:** fit the rail again from the true state at the edit (position: the rail's place plus the turned shift; velocity: the rail's plus the turn of the ship crossed with that shift, which is nothing for a ship that does not turn). It is continuous to the bit, the centre of mass keeps to a true orbit, nothing is kept or removed, and it costs one fit (114 to 145 us) in the tick of an edit. A merge and a split are the same act and already work so. *Read, two traps:* (a) the world learns the centre of mass in one place, `ServerWorld::set_grid_mass`, called from the bodies after the ship is placed for the tick, so the move of the rail has to be made there and before the second `apply_rails`; (b) a ship brought in from a file has a centre of mass of 0 until it is weighed, so the first weighing must not be taken for an edit (the file must keep the centre of mass, or the first weighing be known for what it is). - **(8) Collisions between ships on rails, and when frames are combined.** *Reasoned, not run.* Rapier is needed for the contact itself (many-point contacts of voxel hulls, friction, resting, stacking, a docking by contact); an impulse at one point is right for one instantaneous hit and for nothing that lasts, and a pair of ships in different frames has no solver at all (the audit ran this: two close ships in two frames go through each other).   **Built 2026-11-11** (build log, "Joints, the pairs saved"): `GridStore` holds the pairs (`coupled()`, `set_coupled()`; a pair names ships the store knows, in memory or in files), `ServerWorld::coupled_ships` reads them, `engine:grids` has them at its end (only if there are any, so a world without is byte for byte what it was; a section from before reads as having none), and a load refuses a pair that is out of order, twice, with the higher id first, with itself, or that names a ship the section does not list. - **The planet's unchanged identifier.** *Read:* a planet keeps its id because it cannot be copied (its blocks are a generator and a sparse set of edits): docking writes a ship into it as edits (`dock_into`) and undocking cuts the built blocks out as a new ship (`detach_blocks`), while a ship that merges or splits is always new, with a tombstone (`merge_grids`, `split_grid`). So the asymmetry is the terrain's and not a policy, and renumbering a planet today would make every client forget it and be sent its stored (edited) bricks again, the rest being generated by the client (`GridTerrainState`), and would break every `Centre::Grid` that names it. Opinion: keep it. The bugs of the other kind are real and are all on ships (`Seated.ship`, a walker's reference, the pairs of `coupled_ships`, a rail that goes round a *ship* and is left behind when that ship is merged: `world.rs:373`, the ship stays where it was). What would find them: a test-only `renumber` that gives a ship a new id and changes nothing else, run in the middle of the aboard, seat, rail and coupling scenarios.
  *Telling clients, and not moving data (owner's questions, 2026-11-10).* *Read:* the protocol has `GridsSpawned` (followed by every brick of the ship, "nothing generates a ship's"), `GridsChanged` and `GridsDespawned`, and no message for a change of id, so a merge or a split is, to a client, ships that go and ships that come with all their bricks. **A rename is possible** for a planet or a ship: a new message (`GridRenamed { from, to }`, a protocol version) that a client answers by changing the key of the grid in its stores, and on the server `GridStore` moves the grid to the new key (nothing is copied) and leaves a tombstone from the old id to the new with the identity, so every stale reference still resolves. The work is not the message: it is every map the server keeps by id (the bodies of each frame are `BodyId(id)`, the rails' `Centre::Grid`, `Follow::planet`, the carriers, `coupled_ships`, the lifecycle's failures, the seat) and what a rule keys its chance by; these are the places that break on a merge or a split now, so one event that tells each of them is the cure of both. **For a large ship:** yes, the part that goes on can be re-keyed and not copied (`split_grid` copies every voxel of every part into new grids, and `merge_grids` clones the larger ship; with a rename only the voxels that leave or arrive are touched, and a client is sent the small part's bricks and the rename), with the tombstone still naming both parts so that nothing stale is silently wrong. The other way, the part that goes on keeps its id with no rename, needs no message but leaves a reference to a block that left pointing at the wrong ship. **A client that does the undocking itself** (a message `GridSplit { from, parts: [(id, transform)], ...}` and the client moves the voxels into new bricks): a client has every brick of every ship it knows, and a split keeps the parts' coordinates (identity), so it can move voxels for a split, and for a merge with the lattice transform the server already holds (`voxel_space`, which the client has). What it costs: the rule that decides the parts has to be code the client has too (or the message lists the voxels of the small part, as runs: a few hundred bytes for a small part, not its bricks); a protocol version; recordings stay valid since they are the message stream; the digest of a brick that `VoxelEdits` already carries can be sent for each part, so that a client that differs asks for the bricks (the fall back is today's way); and the client's meshes of the bricks that changed are made again either way, so the saving is the network and the server's serialising, not the client's work. **To keep the option open at no cost now:** make `split_grid`, `merge_grids`, `dock_into` and `detach_blocks` give one record of what they did (old id, new ids, the transform of each, the voxels that moved), and let the replication read that record and not find the change by comparing the sets of ships; today's replication would send spawn and despawn from it, a later one a rename or a split. I have not read the replication code in this session, so how it finds changes today is not checked. **Decided (owner, 2026-11-10, second reply): planet ids stay as they are for now, and the options (a rename message, a re-key of the part that goes on, a client-side split) are kept open**: the record of what a split, merge, dock or detach did, and the test-only `renumber`, are in the plan, last.
  **Built 2026-11-11** (build log, "Joints, the change record and the test-only renumber"). *The record:* `StructureChange` (`engine/server/src/structure_change.rs`): `Split { from, parts }`, `Merge { into, base, carried }`, `Dock { carried }`, `Detach { carried }`, each `VoxelsCarried { from, to, transform, voxels }` with the voxels in the coordinates of `from` and the transform the one the tombstone holds (for a detach, the shift of the lowest corner). `ServerWorld::take_structure_changes` hands them out in the order they ran, an operation that was refused leaves none, and the server hands them on at the end of every tick in `StepReport::structure_changes` (so they never pile up). **Replication does not read it, and cannot replace its comparison with it**: a ship that comes into a client's region, or leaves it, has no record at all, so the comparison of what the client knows with what there is stays; the record is what a rename message or a client-side split would read, and what it says about a ship that a client knows is the extra. *The renumber:* `ServerWorld::renumber_ship` (feature `test-support`; `GridStore::renumber` under it) gives a ship in memory the next id and changes nothing else it can reach: the tombstone, the pairs, what is aboard, the carrier, the quiet of a free ship. It refuses a planet, a ship in a file, and **a ship that another ship goes round**: a rail names a ship with a lower number than the body that goes round it, and a renumbered ship has the highest. (A rename that is not test-only would have to keep the order of the numbers or give that rule up.) *Found, by running it in the middle of the scenarios (`games/voxunami_cave/tests/renumbering.rs`):* a chain of coupled cars swings as if nothing had happened (4e-14 voxels from the same run without it, for a middle or last car, in a frame of its own and on a rail; 4e-5 voxels for the first car, which is the anchor of the group, so the group's rail is taken and another car is the anchor); a ship on a rail goes on along it to the bit; a walker aboard a held cabin is aboard the new id in the same place and walks on. **What does not survive it, and is not pinned by a test because it may be a defect to fix, not a rule:** (1) a walker in the air in a ship that burns, renumbered two ticks into a jump, rises 0.44 m instead of 0.62 (renumbered a tick into it, 0.63 and then 0.58): `agree_with` (`movement.rs`, "in or out of a ship") re-places the body in the frame the record names **with its speed in the ship set to zero**, as for a ship taken from under them; a walker standing still shows nothing. (2) A seated pilot is no longer seated (measured: the next `/ship sit` sits them in "ship 1"), which is what `Seated.ship` says it is for (a ship cut apart or made one is not the one they sat in), so a rename would drop the seat too. (3) *Read, not measured:* `Motion.ship`, `Departure.ship` and the carrier hold ids, and a body whose `Motion` names another ship has no acceleration to measure for one tick (the "first tick aboard" rule). All of these resolve through the tombstone if a rename is ever done for real: the id of the ship, a tombstone with the identity transform and one successor, is a rename; a split or a merge is not. **Fixed 2026-11-12** (`docs/motion-in-frames-options.md`, option 2): `crate::renaming::successor` (`games/voxunami_cave/src/renaming.rs`) says what a retired id became, from `GridStore::resolve`, and every holder asks it: the body (`follow_renamed_ships` in `movement.rs`: the frame, `buckled`, `Motion.ship`, `Departure`, with the speed in the ship turned by the transform's rotation, `LatticeTransform::apply_direction`, and not zeroed) and the pilot's seat (`Seated::follow`, which also carries the seat voxel). ## Step 4c-5: the contact frame (2026-11-13): what was measured, two designs, and a question for the owner
**What is left of promise (a).** A group alone in space has a frame of its own (4c-4); a group with a stranger "in the way" (`ServerWorld::group_is_alone` false) is in the frame everything is in, where its joints are solved with a number of steps that follows the fastest body there (3 to 24 a tick) and with the pull of the world as a force on each car. Nothing in a mechanism has changed, but its numbers have. Nothing of this is built yet; the tests are (`games/voxunami_cave/tests/contact_frame.rs`).

**Measured (ran, 2026-11-13).** The chain of three cars of `group_off_a_rail.rs`, set swinging in orbit, with a stranger of one stone 100 voxels beyond its east end going the same way (the stranger is in the way by the room of `near`, so the chain is in the shared frame); the greatest difference between the facing and the turn of each car and those of the same chain alone, by the distance from the planet's middle:

The 200-tick row is the swing turning back (about the 130th tick), which makes the rounding of a few ticks before it large (`group_off_a_rail.rs` says the same of the alone chain); it is not the size of the difference, and the tests compare at 100. **Reading:** where the speed and the pull are small (the last column) the shared frame and the group's own agree to rounding, so what differs is what the shared frame does at speed and in a pull. *Believed, not isolated by an experiment:* the steps that follow the fastest body, and the pull put on the cars as a force. The first is why a stranger cannot simply be put into the group's frame: a stranger that closes fast would change the steps of the joints.

**Three things found on the way.**
1. *The room is made of reach spheres.* A stranger takes the group out of its frame when the gap between their reach spheres is less than `factor` (2 to become alone, 1 to stay) times 64 voxels plus the way they close in a second. The reach of a ship is the sphere about its origin through the far corner of its bricks (`reach_of_bricks`: 55 voxels for a ship of one brick, whatever is in it), so two ships of a few stones are "in the way" with about 100 voxels between their hulls. There is no measure of a ship's hull finer than its bricks (`MovingGrid::brick_extent`).
2. *A slow stranger misses.* A stone that closed on the chain at 20 m/s from 150 voxels passed 20 m above it (ran: its height over the chain went from 0.6 to 21.6 m in 8 s): a ship at orbital speed in the frame everything is in climbs. *Believed:* the gain of energy that the steps are known to have (`future-goals.md`, "Forces over solver steps"); not isolated. The control uses a wall of 13 by 13 stones that a few metres of climb do not make miss.
3. *The control.* That wall, closing at 50, 150 and 300 m/s, hits the chain, slows, keeps momentum to a hundredth, and leaves the chain coupled with its joints within half a voxel. The chain takes about 58 percent of the share that a hit which stopped the wall dead would give it, at 50 m/s (the joints and the turn take the rest); the amount is not pinned, only that it hit and that momentum was kept.

**Two designs for what is left.**
- **A. Narrow the window.** Keep the shared frame for contact, and make a group leave its own frame only when a hit is about to happen: a measure of a ship's hull from its voxels (a sphere or box that the bodies work out where they weigh the ship, kept on the ship), and a room for the alone-group rule of its own (a few voxels and a few ticks of the speed two ships close at, not 64 voxels and a second). The mechanism's numbers then change only in the quarter of a second before a hit and while it lasts. It does not make them the same through a hit: the shared frame is still the shared frame. Cost: small to medium. The hull is new data, the tree search takes the room as a parameter, and every test that puts a stranger at 40, 100 or 205 voxels says what the old room was and is rewritten. Risk: a room too tight lets a fast stranger in a frame of its own go through the group (the control above is the guard; it can only be run at closing speeds under the 380 voxels a second at which a tick can no longer be cut finely enough, `reference/pack-format.md`).
- **B. Two passes: the joints in the group's frame always, the contact in the shared frame.** Three forms. The owner's question of 2026-11-06 (`history/loop-probe.md`, section 14, (a)): the contact pass sees the whole group as one rigid body whose change of velocity is given back as one rigid motion. The owner's variant of 2026-11-13: the same one body in the contact pass, but what is given back is the *impulse*, to the cars that were hit, at the points, in the group's own frame, where the joint solve spreads it. And a third, the **ghost**: the contact pass is a full copy of the shared-frame world (cars, joints, motors, colliders), synced from the groups' own joint worlds every step, and only its contact result is given back (as impulses, or as the difference of two ghosts, one with the contacts and one without). All three keep the joints in the group's frame, so a mechanism that touches nothing is bit for bit what it is alone, whatever is near (ran: zero difference with a stranger near and not touching, for both ghost forms and the rigid form; the whole-body impulse form was not bit-identical, see below). **Measured 2026-11-13 on landing gear, doors, a rover and a ship carrying a ship, with springs and motors under gravity (`history/loop-probe.md`, section 16; `spikes/loops`, `cargo run --release --bin contraptions`):** the whole-body forms, rigid and impulse, are wrong for anything that moves: a landing gear stops in one step (13 to 18 g where 2.5 is true; the struts do not compress or bottom out; the feet chatter at 1 to 3 m/s at rest, and a bystander 1.5 m away changes the whole run), a door on a spring motor is flung to its stop (1.71 rad where 0.29 is true), a rover cannot drive or coast (a wheel's rolling is invisible to a contact that sees one rigid body), a carried ship's struts and landing are wrong; they are right for a locked door and for a stiff column hit along its length (better than today's combined step); a body for each car fails whatever is carried through a joint (the foot holds up only itself; the hull sags onto the floor, 0.7 m for the gear), and 8 rounds of predicted joint steps are needed to bring the gear back, at 7.6 times the cost; **the ghost passes every one of them within a few percent of the converged answer**, because its contact answer is the combined step's. *This replaces what I wrote here earlier today, "a strut would not compress, a pushed-in car does not push back": in detail it was wrong (with the impulse form a strut does compress, too much, because the whole ship's impulse is given to the foot) and in substance right (the contact does not see how the parts of a mechanism move).* The ghost's price: a group with a stranger in range has a second, shared-frame copy and its own joint world (two solves, three for the difference form; in the toy 2.4 and 2.9 times the combined step, in the game less, because voxel contacts are most of a combined step), the bodies hold each group twice, and it has to exist *before* the contact (the same room as A, but a generous one costs only time). It inherits what the combined step gets wrong: a stranger that hits a stiff chain rebounds (exit speed -0.57 where -1.00 is true), where the whole body is exact. Given as an impulse, a hit arrives late in the step (overlap of 23, 93 and 281 mm at 5, 20 and 60 m/s where the combined step has none); the difference form does not have this. **Second round, same day (`history/loop-probe.md`, section 17), the owner's three questions: the ghost is the shared world with every car movable and its joints in place, and with it, part priorities in four forms and fixes of the failed methods:** *weld what is rigid* (fixed joints, and joints marked immobilized) into one body in the ghost and keep the mobile joints: a stranger on a stiff chain then gets the converged answer (exit -1.000 / -4.000 / -12.000 at 5 / 20 / 60 m/s where the combined step gives -0.571 / -2.283 / -7.047) at less than the plain ghost's cost, and nothing changes where nothing is rigid (the owner's whole-body idea kept where it is exact; welded wrongly, on a joint that moves, it is as wrong as the whole body); *solver work* as a priority cures the same rebound by passes (15 extra passes: -0.998 and -3.992), at about twice the island's cost, but only if it is set before the touch (by nearness): set by the touch it did nothing or made a chain bounce; *who is given the impulse* (a priority per car; the higher one is left out of the contact's impulses) keeps a crate from moving the ship it is in (the ship's speed exactly 0), as the owner's answer 7 wants, and works on the impulse forms; a *level of detail by distance* (far cars welded) saves a third to a half of the cost for a hit that stays local and gives the wrong answer for a shock along a column.
