# Phase 39: planets are ships (and the level `mini_cosmos`)

Status: started 2026-10-24. This builds `lod-spec.md` 3.8 (planets are ships: grids with terrain generators) and the part of 38j that a planet needs (a ship drawn with levels of detail), and uses them for one
planet in a new level. What I checked in the code is marked **[code]**; what I believe is marked **[belief]**.

## 1. What the owner asked for (2026-10-24)

A new world level `mini_cosmos` with one planet in it; the default level for now. The planet is **1% of the size of the Earth**, **a ship**, with a terrain generator **like the overworld's but producing a sphere**, its outside
shaped by **a 2D heightmap bent into a sphere**. **LODs are on for it.** Questions about big gaps, along the way.

Answers to my questions (the same day):

- **The bend:** a cube-sphere with the heightmaps blended where faces meet (no seams, little pinching).
- **Gravity:** 10 metres a second a second at the surface. (I had written that the overworld falls at 32; it falls at 5, `GravitySettings::default`, the owner's decision of 2026-10-22. **[code]** I had trusted an old doc.)
- **Caves:** in the bricks, as the overworld has them; **not in the levels of detail**, which are made from the ground without them (`lod-spike.md`: a far view with caves is 100 to 1,000 times slower to make).
- **Levels:** a new world has both, `mini_cosmos` first (where players start) and the overworld second. Saved worlds are unchanged.

## 2. My assumptions (say if wrong)

- **1% of the Earth is 1% of its radius**: 63,710 voxels (a voxel is a metre), 127 kilometres across. The generator is a version of its own, so the number is part of the version, not a parameter of the file.
- The planet is **held** (it does not move or spin), centred on the origin of the level, and the level is empty space around it. Moving and spinning planets are later ("let's decide later", owner). **The owner's stance of 2026-10-25:** a planet is a kind of ship with slightly different rules, sharing most of the code of ships; all planets
  will be **on rails** (so what happens when two come near each other is not a question); planets are **never docked to each other or to a moon** (node and brick alignment are not worth it); a ship that docks with a planet is **written into the planet's grid and the planet's identifier does not change** (not built yet). The code is to stay simple.
- The planet's **mass is declared**, not summed from blocks (it has none stored): the mass that gives 10 metres a second a second at the surface under the real G (6.07e20 kilograms). The level's own pull (`GravitySettings::base`) is **zero in `mini_cosmos`**
  (space), so gravity there is the planet's pull toward its centre. That needs a level's gravity to be its own setting, which today is one for the server (a small change, below).
- Players spawn **on the surface at the top of the planet** (+y) and are put aboard it when they stand on it, by the rule that puts a player aboard a ship.
- The sky stays as it is for now (a blue sky and fog): a planet in space wants a sky that goes black with height, which is a renderer job of its own.
- **Planet seed:** the world's; later planets get one from their ship's id.

## 3. The design

**The shape (`terrain::PlanetGround`, built in 39-1).** A voxel is ground when `radius + height(direction) - |p| > 0` where `p` is the voxel's middle from the planet's centre (the corner where eight voxels meet, so the planet is exactly
symmetric under the turns and mirrors of the cube). The value is about how many voxels the middle is under the surface. `height` is the blend of six 2D heightmaps, one for each side of a cube; a heightmap is the overworld's fractal noise (planar),
read at `radius` times the angle across the face (`atan` is the 9-term polynomial of Abramowitz and Stegun 4.4.49, 1e-5 off, and beyond 1 a straight line that touches it, so a face can be read a little past its edge), in whole voxels. A face counts in
a direction if its share of the distance is within 0.15 of the greatest; its weight is a smoothstep of how near it is, and the weights are scaled so that their squares add to one (the blend of independent noises keeps its spread; a plain average
of two has half the variance, and the seams would show as flat bands). Only `+ - * /`, `sqrt`, `abs`, `min`, `max` and `floor`, so it is bit for bit the same on every machine (no `sin`, no `atan`, no fused multiply-add). The planet is
`PlanetSettings::one_percent_of_earth`: radius 63,710, hills 160 voxels tall at the largest (wavelength 1,024, nine octaves down to 4), so that a kilometre of walking changes the height by tens of metres and the hills are as steep per octave as the
overworld's are, and the overworld's sheet caves in the planet's own coordinates (3D noise needs no bending).

**Bounds (built in 39-1).** Over a box, `|p|` is bounded by the nearest and the farthest corner; the height by the noise of each face that can count in the box (found from the box: a face counts only where its distance along its axis is above
the greatest of the nearest distances less the width of the seam), over the box of the heightmap that the box maps to. With one face counting the bound is that face's; with several the weights' squares add to one, so by the Cauchy-Schwarz
inequality the height is within the root of the sum of the squares of the faces' largest values. **Measured:** a brick at the surface has a bound 134 to 151 wide where one face counts and 404 where three blend (so the corners of the cube are looser, and
the cubes round them that may show are more; it costs only work). Sound by a property test over boxes from one voxel to the whole planet.

**The generator (`terrain::PlanetGenerator`).** Bricks come from the ground and the caves: air, and stone below the layers, are decided by bounds where they can be; the layers are read from the value of the ground (grass up to 1 under the surface, dirt
to 4, stone deeper), not from the voxels above as the overworld does, so a cave under the surface does not turn the ground beside it to grass. The coarse nodes and `may_show` come from the ground without the caves, so a cube is "nothing to see" by the
ground alone (a cube wholly under or over the shell of the surface). **A node of a planet** (`planet_node.rs`) is made from **columns along the axis the node is nearest to**: each of the 34 x 34 columns (the node and a ring round it) is walked from an
estimate of where the ground ends in it, asking the field at the middle of each cell until the cells are clearly air outward and clearly ground inward. A cell's fill is the value of the field at its middle over the width of the cell across the surface
(`edge * (|nx| + |ny| + |nz|)`, the extent of a cube along the normal), clamped; blocks and surfaces are those of `coarse.rs`, whose layering now takes the step to the cell over a cell (`up`) and where the fills come from, so that the layers look outward
along the axis, not up. A node that reaches the plane through the centre of the planet is made by asking about every cell. **Measured (release, one thread, the real planet, nodes at the surface):** 0.5 to 1.0 ms a node at levels 1 to 10 and 6 ms a brick.
The first version walked a fixed point along the column and failed where the surface folds over the column (steep hills, a column at 40 degrees or more to the surface): a search from outside found a root 6 voxels away. The walk asks about each cell, so
it does not depend on the ground being a graph over the axis, and it agrees with asking about every cell of every column in 0.0% to 0.3% of the cells with a surface in them on the real planet (1.6% on a small planet whose hills are twice as steep per
octave). **Accuracy against the voxels** (the node's cell drawn when most of the voxels under it, on a lattice of 4 x 4 x 4, are ground): wrong in 17% of the cells with a surface in them at level 1 (a 2-voxel cell is one question about eight voxels) and 3% to 6%
at levels 2 to 6; the lattice of 4 x 4 x 4 points of `coarse.rs` takes 20 to 29 ms for the fills of one node of this planet against 0.4 to 0.5 ms for the columns (40 to 55 times as much), and was not measured against the voxels here.

**A ship with terrain (`world_storage`, built in 39a).** `MovingGrid.terrain: Option<GridTerrain>`: **one generator version** (a `GeneratorId` of the world's table, which all levels share), the **extent** (a `BrickBox` in the ship's own coordinates) and the
**mass** it declares (`MovingGrid::mass` is set to it; the centre of mass is the ship's own field, the origin for a planet). I had planned a `WorldTerrain` with assignments over the ship's bricks; a planet is one generator version over one box, and a ship
that wants another version is another generator id, so the section is version 1 and carries a name, a box and a weight, and assignments can be a version 2 if a moon ever needs them. A brick that is not stored is generated; an edited brick is stored in `voxels`
and wins. `brick_extent` / `world_brick_bounds` are the hull of the extent and the stored bricks, so the bounds a parked ship is listed with, the leave rule and the box that replication meets are the planet's. The ship file gets the optional section
`engine:grid_terrain` (version 1: the generator's identifier, the box as six zigzag varints, the mass as `f32` bits; a damaged one, a mass that is not a weight, corners the wrong way round or a brick out of range, is refused; a ship that nothing generates
has no such section, so its file is byte for byte what it was). The generator is written by name and read by `ShipFormat` through the reading world's table (`engine:terrain_generator`, like the content of a channel): a world without the generator refuses the
file saying which (`UnknownContent`), and a generator of a plugin that is not loaded is a placeholder in the table, so the planet is kept and saved as it was.

**The server (built in 39b).** `ServerWorld` reads through the generator for a ship with terrain: `moving_voxel`, `moving_generated_voxel` and `moving_uniform_value` (what `GridView::get`, `generated` and `uniform_value` call for a ship) take the stored brick if there is
one, else the brick the generator makes if it is in the extent, else the channel's default. The bricks a planet's generator makes are remembered with a level's (`GeneratedBrickCache`, now keyed by `GridId`, so a level's root grid and each ship have their own), counted by
`bricks_generated`, and a generator whose plugin is missing is `AccessError::TerrainUnavailable` naming it. An edit of a brick that is not stored stores the generated brick with the voxel changed (as a level's terrain does), noted for the clients as a created brick; setting a
voxel to the value it has stores nothing. The bound of the ship is its extent (39a). **Until 39c a planet is left out of `ship_views`** (the wire cannot say what makes its bricks, and sending only its stored bricks would show a client the edits floating in nothing); 39c
sends the terrain once, with `GridsSpawned`, and after that the **stored** bricks only (a recording holds the generator ids and replays by regenerating).

**The wire and the client (built in 39c).** Protocol 8: `GridState` carries `terrain: Option<GridTerrainState { generator: u32, extent: BrickBox }>` (the generator as a number of the world description's table, as in an assignment; the mass is not sent, nothing on a client reads it). The server tells of a planet
as of any ship, with the terrain, and then sends the **stored** bricks only; `ship_views` no longer leaves it out. The client (`ClientGrid.terrain: Option<ClientGridTerrain { generator, extent }>`, translated through the same translator as an assignment) makes the rest:
`world_view::TerrainSource` (a trait that the mesher of the bricks of a level reads from, which `LevelData` and a ship's `ShipTerrainSource` now both are; `known` is a stored brick, or the empty brick beyond the extent, and `generator_for` is the generator) lets a `WorldMesher` do for a ship what it did for
a level, and `ShipTerrainMesher` is that mesher saying what it draws as bricks of the ship (`UploadGridBrick`), under the ship's pose. The `Session` keeps one for each planet, feeds it the camera in the planet's coordinates (`GridPose::world_to_local`), sends the changes of its stored bricks to it
and not to the mesher of ships (which no longer meshes the stored bricks of a planet alone, without the neighbours the generator makes), aims the crosshair through it, and waits for it when it settles. The view summary a replay is compared with names the generator and the extent. A brick beyond the extent is empty and the face of the brick against it is drawn,
so a planet cut off by its extent shows its side. **Golden sessions:** the four that pin a stream or a recording (2048, Eels, Cave, and the end-to-end recording) moved once, for the version in the welcome; each was checked by running the code of the commit before with only the version raised to 8, which gave the new hash and the same length, so none of them tells of a ship.
**Not yet:** a level and a game that have a planet in them (39f).

**Bodies (built in 39d).** A walker meets a planet through the generator. `grid_bodies::collision::GeneratedHull` is a `CollisionSource` that reads a ship's voxels through the world (`ServerWorld::moving_voxel` and `moving_uniform_value`, now `&self` and public: the bricks a generator makes are
remembered behind a cell, so a body can meet several ships at once while the world is borrowed shared). `ShipSource` is that or the `ShipHull` that reads a ship's stored voxels directly (kept as the fast path of an ordinary ship: the two agree for a ship without terrain, and a test cannot tell them apart,
which is why one mutant of the choice stays). `SolidAt` is a blanket trait on `CollisionSource` that replaces `ShipHull::solid_at`, so the rules of being aboard (`stands_on`, `has_left`, in Cave's `aboard.rs`) take either. Cave's `Blocks::source` chooses by `ship.terrain`, and `step_in_a_level` (the ships near
a walker, as foreign grids; standing on one; the leave rule) and `step_aboard` use it. What a block is to a body and what a whole brick is to it are two functions shared by `WorldTerrain` and `GeneratedHull` (`collision_of_voxel`, `hint_of_brick`), with unit tests; the hint no longer asks about orientations
(whether a shape is empty, a whole cell or part of one does not depend on how it is turned), which changes nothing but that a brick of one block in one state and several orientations is known at once. In 39d a planet had no body: `grid_bodies` leaves a ship with terrain out of its bodies (39g gives it a fixed one, for ships to meet, below), its weighing (the
mass is what the terrain declares, `ServerWorld::set_grid_terrain`) and its splitting and vanishing (its stored bricks are edits of a ground that is not stored, so "no blocks left" and "in pieces" mean nothing for it). A walker dropped over a planet lands on it, is aboard it (in its coordinates), walks on the ground the
generator makes, and falls into a hole that is dug (the edits are stored bricks); a slab put on it upside down is a ceiling slab to a walker.

**Gravity (built in 39d).** A level's own pull: `ServerWorld::set_level_gravity` (where it is not `GravitySettings::base`: none in space), `ServerConfig.level_gravity` by the level's identifier (a level the world lacks is ignored), and the game's `GameDefinition::{gravity, level_gravity}`. Cave: the base is 5 as it was,
`PULLING_MASS = 10^19` kilograms is the least mass of a ship that pulls (a moon, not a raft), and `voxunami_cave:mini_cosmos` has none. The planet's declared mass is M = 10 R^2 / G = 6.08 x 10^20 kilograms for R = 63,710 and the real G, which pulls 10.004 m/s^2 at 15 voxels over the ground
(measured in a test; the test asks for within a tenth). The field aboard is a vector fixed in the planet's axes, as 2.9 of `moving-grids-spec.md` says; the leave rule of a body aboard (a box of bricks) is the extent of the planet.

**What it costs (measured, release, this machine).** A walker meeting the planet for the first time makes the server generate about 50 bricks at 9 ms: joining and settling took 480 ms, against 20 ms with no planet there (five runs, 479 to 486; three runs, 19 to 20). Walking on, a tick is 0.05 ms at the median and 0.1 ms at
p99, and crossing into a new row of bricks costs one tick of 37 to 44 ms (about the size of the bricks of ground and stone below at 6 to 9 ms each; I did not count them). That is the whole of the hitch in 400 ticks of walking, and it is not spread over ticks (as 32h did for the terrain of a level). **Not done:**
(a) spreading the first contact and the crossings over ticks; (b) a rigid-body ship (`grid_bodies`) had no collider against a planet, **verified:** a ship of 4 x 4 x 4 stone let go 40 voxels over the planet accelerated at 10 m/s^2, went through the surface and came to rest on the hills of the overworld 320 metres lower (the colliders that
followed a ship were the level's terrain, and a planet is not that; **done in 39g, below**); (c) every brick below the surface that was probed (6 columns of 50 bricks each, from the surface to 1,600 voxels down) has air in it from a cave, so a brick of the planet is hardly ever one block and the hints seldom save a scan.

**Levels of detail for a planet (built in 39e).** `world_view::CoarseTerrainSource` (a version, `may_show`, `coarse_generator` and an optional `solid_core`; `LevelData` and `ShipTerrainSource` are both one) is all that `LodView::update` and `settle` ask of the terrain they draw, now
`(grid, source, viewer, settings)`, so the view that draws a level draws a planet. `ShipTerrainMesher::with_detail` wraps a `LodView` and says what it draws as `MeshUpdate::{UploadGridBrick, UploadGridCoarse, ...}` (the two new ones for nodes; the renderer already had `upload_grid_coarse_node`). The viewer is taken into the planet's axes
(`GridPose::world_to_local`); `may_show` is the generator's bounds inside the extent (a node with no brick in the extent is not made, one that reaches in is made whole: a planet's extent holds its ground); the nodes are the generator's. **The server does not correct the nodes of a planet**: an edit shows in the bricks round the
viewer and not in the nodes farther away (open: the messages and the store are keyed by grid already, so this is a matter of the server's side). **Known from anywhere:** `grid_replication` tells every client that watches a level of every planet in it, and the lifecycle never puts a planet in a file (it is frozen when no one is near, as any ship, and
stays in memory), so a client draws it from as far as there is anything to see. **The horizon:** a generator may say it has a solid core (`TerrainGenerator::solid_core`; the planet's is its radius less the square root of three times the greatest height of a face alone, which bounds the blend of the faces, less a voxel), and a view
does not make the cubes that are wholly behind it from a viewer outside it (`world_view::horizon::is_hidden_by`: the cube's bounding sphere against the cone of the lines that touch the core, and beyond the places they touch; a margin of a brick's diagonal, because a view is worked out again only when the viewer leaves its brick). It is
conservative, because the core is lower than the ground: its horizon is 8.4 km from a viewer on the ground, not the half a kilometre of the real one. **The game:** `GameDefinition::grid_detail()` (Cave: out to 10^7 voxels, light 0, cells of 3 pixels), `Session::enable_grid_detail` (called by the shell where it calls `enable_coarse_detail`),
and `fog_radius_bricks` reaches as far as the farthest of the level's terrain and the planet's.

**What it costs (measured, release, 4 worker threads, this machine; Cave's planet of 63,710 voxels, a picture of 1080 pixels).** From 2 m over the ground: 12,879 nodes (levels 1 to 7: 4,058, 2,784, 2,227, 2,019, 1,371, 385, 35) and 1,334 bricks, 4.8 million quads, settled in 6.0 s. Without the horizon: 18,764 nodes, 9.2 s. From 1 km up 9,929 nodes, 3.2 s (14,962 and 5.4 s
without); from 30 km up 1,995 nodes, 0.6 s (3,131 and 0.85 s); from 500 km up 36 nodes, 0.17 s. The nodes near the viewer are most of it, and the horizon of the core is far off, so the cull takes a third and not the 97% to 99% that the spike found with the real heights of columns (`lod-spike.md`): a tighter cull needs the heights of
the ground, per direction, which a generator would have to give. The setting that matters is `cell_pixels`: at the surface 4 gives 7,947 nodes, 6 gives 3,997, 8 gives 2,596 and 12 gives 1,322 (4.1 to 4.7 s whatever the setting: that is the 1,334 bricks, 6 ms each to generate). Cave uses 3 (the value the views of levels were tuned with); I have not looked at it
on a screen yet (39f does).


**The level and the game (built in 39f).** `terrain::empty` (a generator that makes air everywhere and says nothing may show; registered as `base_blocks:empty_v1` after the hills and the planet, so that the numbers of every older world's generators stand) is the generator of `voxunami_cave:mini_cosmos`, which
`CaveContent::new_world` makes first, with the overworld second (`ContentPackage::new_world_of` makes a world of several levels, each with its own default generator); the planet is made with the world: a ship in the first level at its origin, held, made by `base_blocks:small_planet_v1`, over 2,100 bricks each way (67,200 voxels,
which holds the ground with the highest hills it can have), of the mass that pulls 10 m/s^2 at 63,710 (`10 R^2 / G`, in `content.rs`). `CaveContent::new_overworld_world` is the world Cave began with (the overworld alone, no planet), which the tests that start in the overworld use (and the golden session); a world that was saved before there was a planet is
loaded as it was. **Where a player arrives:** `voxunami_cave::landing::on_a_planet` (the ground at the top of the first planet of a level, found by looking down the column of its +y axis through the bricks that are not all air, with what has been dug or built counted) is where a new player stands if their client began where the game says players
start (a client started with `--camera` is put where it asked), and where `/level go` puts a player in a level that has a planet; `/level go` from a level with a planet to one without puts them where players start there (the places of one are nowhere in the other), and otherwise they are at the same place, as always. For that
`player_presence::Presence::set_level_arrival` (a rule given the world, the level a player is in and the level they are sent to) and the bodies of Cave follow the record when the level changes (they kept their own place before). **A planet is always in memory:** the world file says which of its ships are planets (the flags byte of a ship in
`engine:grids`: bit 0 waits for those aboard, as before, bit 1 has terrain), and the lifecycle brings a planet back from its file whatever the distance (a world that was saved comes back with every ship in a file, and a client has to be told of the planet from anywhere).

**Ships meet a planet (built in 39g, 2026-10-25; the first thing the owner asked for after the stance above: "ships cannot yet collide with generated terrain that has not yet been modified", and then to look for the same weakness elsewhere).**
*Bodies.* `grid_bodies` gives a planet a **fixed body** in the frame of its level (`Planet` in `LevelBodies`: it is made once the level has a ship that is simulated, and a planet neither sets the frame nor keeps it alive), put where the world has it and moved with `BodyWorld::set_state` if anything moves it (a teleport: ships on it are
not carried; a kinematic body is for the rails). Its colliders are **only for the bricks that a free ship is near**, by the rules the level's terrain has had since 32k (`TERRAIN_*`: needed now, wanted nearest first at one brick a tick, kept a little longer; now `Bodies::bricks_to_change`, the same code for the level and for each planet, with `Ground::{Level, Planet}`
saying which): the bricks are seen in the planet's own coordinates (`BodyWorld::bricks_near_in`: the bounds of the ship, turned into the frame of the planet's body, so a planet at any place and turn works) and made with `BodyWorld::set_brick` from a `GeneratedHull` (the stored brick if the planet has one, else the generator's), so a ship meets what a walker meets and a client draws. `BodyWorld::remove_brick` lets a brick go. An
edit of the planet makes the colliders of the bricks that have them again (a ship asleep on a dug-out place is woken and falls); the bricks asked for are not limited to the generator's extent, because what is built past it is the planet's too (found by a test: the first version cut them by the extent, and a slab built above the last brick was fallen through). A ship that is held asks for no bricks of a planet (both are fixed).
**A planet farther than `PLANET_REACH_BRICKS` (4,096 bricks, 131 km) from the origin of the frame has no body**, because the planet's voxels are in its own coordinates, as far from the frame as it is, and an `f32` is exact to a hundredth of a voxel there. **[code, a throwaway test with the physics crate alone]:** a cube let down on a fixed body 63,680 voxels away sleeps in 44 ticks as it does at 64, and at 636,800 it never does (0.8 m/s of
noise); I did not measure between, so 131 km is a judgement. The mini planet is 2,000 bricks off at its pole. *Arrival.* `grid_lifecycle::Obstacles` (where a ship that comes back from its file may land) looked at the terrain of the level and the stored blocks of the other ships, so a ship could land in the unmodified ground of a planet and a player was
never asked. It now takes the ground of a planet (stored, else generated) as terrain: it blocks, "move the ship" finds the nearest place above it, and "break the terrain" digs it out of the planet (an edit stored over the generator). *The world's spawn.* The lifecycle's spawn is a rule given the world and the level (`GridLifecyclePlugin::new(presence, |world, level| ...)`); Cave's is `landing::spawn_in` (the ground at the pole of the planet of the level, else
where players start). A pilot whose ship is lost, or who leaves a blocked ship behind, no longer appears at (16, 110, 16) in the rock of the planet (**verified before the change:** the test put them at (12, 94, 38), pushed out sideways from the constant and inside the planet). *Merge.* `ServerWorld::merge_grids` refuses a ship with terrain (`MergeError::HasTerrain`); it would have made a new ship with no terrain of a planet and a new id,
which is neither what the owner wants of a ship that docks with a planet nor of two planets.
*Tests.* Cave `planet.rs`: a cube let go over the unmodified ground rests on it at the ground's height to 0.05 and to the bit, and the planet stores nothing; it falls into a hole dug under it by five voxels, to 0.05; it rests on a slab built past the generator's bricks; it rests on a planet turned a quarter about x and 63 km off the origin (within 0.05; this is the test that shows the turn and the offset of the frame); a planet that is moved has its ground
where it is put and one that is removed has none; a cube that is taken a hundred voxels up and put back finds the ground again; the bricks made are few and only for free ships (a held ship near a planet makes what it would with no planet, and a free one in the sky has a few empty bricks, counted: 16 and 12 in 60 ticks, the most in one tick 8 and 5). `physics`: `bricks_near_in` with the frame at an offset and turned, and `remove_brick` (a ship asleep on a brick
that is taken wakes, and a second that is let go over the place falls through). `grid_lifecycle` `arrivals`: four on the ground of a planet (blocked, free above it, moved, dug out), and one for the spawn of another level when a blocked ship is left behind (the one for a lost ship now expects the spawn of that level). Cave `cosmos.rs`: the lost-ship pilot of a new world stands on the planet, and `spawn_in`. `server` `merge`: a ship with terrain. **Mutation experiments:** 42 mutants of the new code in `grid_bodies` (16), `physics` (10), `arrival` (7), the lifecycle (2), `merge` (4), `landing` (2) and the wiring (1). The first tests missed 5: a brick that is let go still counted as made (a ship that goes away and comes back finds no ground); a held ship asking for the bricks of a planet; `remove_brick` filling a brick instead of emptying it (a ship inside a filled brick has no contacts and falls through anyway, so only a second ship let go over the place shows it); the warp to the spawn of another level; and the reach of a planet. The first four have tests now. **The one not caught:** the reach (`PLANET_REACH_BRICKS`) is not reached by any planet in the content, so only the function it uses is tested (`farther_than`). **Seen and not understood:** a cube whose face is exactly flush with a one-voxel step of the ground, pulled sideways into it by the planet's pull a few ten-thousandths off the vertical, never goes to sleep on the planet (a hundredth of a metre a second of jitter); the same cube half a voxel from the step sleeps in 44 ticks, and the same
step with the physics crate alone, at the same distance, sleeps. I think it is the noise of an `f32` at that distance against a contact that is held on, but I did not find it. The tests put their cubes half a voxel from the step.

**Frames (built after 39g, 2026-10-25; the owner's answer to (h): space exploration is the future of Cave; ships will go on rails when far from the players or in a time warp, which is for later, and until then simulation just stops beyond a distance of the nearest player, a server setting with a command).** The bodies of a level are no longer in one frame around the first ship: `grid_bodies` keeps a frame for each *place* (`Frame`, keyed by the level and a
number), a ship that is not within `FRAME_RADIUS_BRICKS` (500) of the origin of any frame of its level makes a frame centred on it, and a ship that goes twice as far from the origin of its frame is taken out of it and put in the frame of the place it is in. Planets get a body, and the level's terrain colliders are kept, in every frame; ships in different frames do not meet (they are more than 16 km apart). **Verified:** under a pull of 32, four ships at 0, 10, 40 and
400 km along x all fall (three frames: the first two share one); a ship put 60 km away is in a frame of its own and goes on falling. The distance from a player beyond which a ship stops was already a server setting (`ShipSettings::simulation_distance`, 256 voxels by default, `--ship-simulation-distance` for `serve`); the new command **`/ship distance [SIMULATE [UNLOAD]|everywhere]`** shows it or changes it while the server runs (one number changes the simulation distance,
and the unload distance with it if it would be less; two set both; a refusal says why). It is not saved with the world: a server starts from its flags. `BodiesReport::frames` counts the frames. *Not done:* a frame's origin never moves, so a ship that flies 16 km and stays in the frame is exact to a hundredth of a voxel at worst (the 2x rule moves it first); the planet's reach (4,096 bricks from a frame's origin) is now only that of a frame whose first ship is far from the planet; ships on rails (`future-goals.md`, "Space exploration").

**Bricks made ahead by a worker (built after 39g, 2026-10-25; issue (b)).** `ServerWorld::prefetch_ship_bricks(grid, bricks)` asks a worker thread (`server::prefetch`, started at the first request, ended when the world is dropped) for the bricks of a ship with terrain that are in its extent and neither remembered nor already asked for; `take_prefetched` (and every read that finds a brick missing) takes the finished ones into the cache of generated bricks. A brick is the same whoever makes it, so asking changes no value that is read, nor what the bodies simulate (the colliders a ship gets each tick are still chosen by counts); it only
changes whether a tick waits for the generator. `ServerWorld::bricks_prefetched` counts what the worker made that was taken in. Asked for: by `grid_bodies`, the planet's wanted bricks that are left for later ticks, with the bricks beside them and beside those made now; by Cave's `step_aboard`, the 5 x 3 x 5 bricks round a walker on a planet. **The cost was not what I said it was:** I measured (release, this machine) a walker on the planet crossing rows of bricks with a tick every 40 ms: the worst tick was 37 to 43 ms, all of it in the building of the character's colliders (`update_terrain`); one brick of ground takes 5 ms to generate and 2 ms to read, but
`physics::terrain::build_brick` looks at the voxels *beside* its brick to decide which boxes are buried and where bricks overlap, so it makes the generator make the six neighbours as well (a microbenchmark of one brick: 30 ms the first time, 1 ms when the neighbours were there). The first version of the walker's request (the 27 bricks round it) cut the worst tick to 26 to 28 ms; with a ring more (the 75 bricks above) it is 8.5 ms, and the next worst 8.3. For a ship skimming the ground at 60 m/s the worst tick fell from 15 ms to 11. The server's cache of generated bricks is 256 now (128 before; the walker asks for 75 at a time). The two ring sizes were chosen by that measurement and no test pins them (a test pins that the walker and a falling ship are served by the worker).

**Seen (2026-10-24, headless screenshots of the real game, ambient light raised for the picture: Cave's is 0, so terrain is black unless the player says `.ambient`):** the surface at the pole (rolling green hills, cave sheets breaking the ground, the far hills as nodes to the horizon); the horizon from 2.8 km up; the planet as a round disc from 400 km and 230 km (a view from the side needs the pitch of the local
horizon: a player's up is away from the planet, so a pitch of 0 looks along the ground wherever they are). From the ground the first picture took 8.7 s in the dev profile (9,171 bricks); from 400 km 1 s.

**Not done, and known:** (a) *done in 39g:* a rigid-body ship has no collider against a planet (39d); (b) *done after 39g, see "Bricks made ahead by a worker":* the first contact with the planet costs about half a second and a row of new bricks 40 ms at the server (39d); the first contact is still a wait (nothing was asked ahead), a row of bricks walked into is 8 ms now; (c) the server does not correct the nodes of a planet (39e), so a hole dug in it shows in the bricks round the player and not in the far nodes; (d) the horizon of the core is generous (39e); (e) *done in 39g:* a player whose ship is lost and who asks for the world's
spawn was put at the constant (16, 110, 16) of the level, which in space is inside the planet; (f) nothing lights the planet (Cave has no sky light yet), so it is as dark as the overworld unless `.ambient` is raised; (g) the planet's own seed makes its hills (the world's seed), so no two worlds have the same pole and a client cannot know the height
of the pole before the server says (a client that asks for `--camera` over it is put there, in the air or in the ground).
**Found in 39g by looking for the same weakness, and not fixed (the owner's to decide; `plan.md`, Open):** (h) *done the same day, see "Frames" below:* **free ships were simulated only within `FRAME_RADIUS_BRICKS` (500 bricks, 16 km) of the first ship of their level**; **verified:** two free ships 10 km and 40 km from the first, under a pull of 32: the nearer fell, the other did not move in 40 ticks (no gravity, no collision, no planet). (i) **Dropped items fall along the grid's -y and are removed below y = -1000** (`items.rs`: `fall`, `LOWEST_DROP_VOXEL`) **[from reading the code, not run]**: on a planet, an item dropped on the flank falls sideways along the planet's y axis and one in the southern hemisphere is removed at once; it wants the
gravity of its grid (`gravity_in`), and a decision of what the bottom of a world is. (j) `/ship create` reads the terrain of the level only, so blocks built on a planet cannot be made a ship. (k) Docking with a planet (the ship's blocks written into the planet's grid, its identifier kept) is not built: the ports of a planet are not looked at, `merge_grids` refuses it, and the overlap check will have to read the generated blocks. (l) The planet is held, not on rails: nothing carries a ship that rests on a planet that moves
(a fixed body moved by `set_state` is a teleport); the rails want a kinematic body. (m) The server remembers 256 generated bricks (128 until the worker), first in first out, for the walkers, the ships, the edits and the reads of every rule; a server with several players and ships on a planet, apart, may make a brick over and over at 6 to 9 ms. (n) `GridTerrain` holds only the identifier of a generator, so a planet's size and heights are the generator's version (data-driven planets want parameters in the file).
